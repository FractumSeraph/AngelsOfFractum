The Fractum keeps no calendar. It breaks what it breaks on whatever day it likes.

But the world has seasons, and the seasons have their own breaks: the smoke detector that chirps in November, the hose that freezes in December, the carts that multiply in the week before a holiday like loaves. The Angels of Fractum therefore keep a small liturgical year, not because the days are holy in themselves, but because a day with a name is a day one remembers to act.

None of these observances require a gathering. All of them can be completed alone. Most take an hour.

## The Fixed Observances

### The Week of the Severed Thread
*The first full week of January.*

The year begins with the quietest Fractum: the drift of bonds. For seven days, the Angel reaches out to one dropped thread each day. A message, a call, two sentences. No agenda, no apology demanded. By the seventh day, seven bridges have been proven to stand. Some will answer. Some will not. The thread was never cut. Only dropped.

### The Day of the Returned Thing
*The spring equinox, or the nearest Saturday.*

On the day when light and dark are balanced, the Angel balances the ledger of borrowed things: every one of them, at once. The mission of the Returning of the Borrowed Thing says how it is done. The equinox says only when, because the Doubt will otherwise choose a day called Later. Nothing is returned with a lecture, and nothing is returned with an excuse.

### The Thaw of the Trailhead
*The first Saturday of April.*

The thaw reveals what winter hid: the litter at the trailhead, the bags the snow swallowed, the cans in the ditch. The Angel takes a bag and gloves to one public place and leaves it clean. This is a Repair of Omission in its most muddy form, and it is performed without a vest, a banner, or a photograph.

### The Feast of the Coiled Hose
*The first frost, or the week before it is forecast.*

The Angel readies their own house for winter, and then, with the gloves still on, knocks on one other door, in daylight: "I am putting mine away. May I do yours while I am at it?" The hose is disconnected and coiled. The bag of salt is set by the door. The porch bulb, if it is out, is replaced with the one they hand you. The neighbor who is elderly, or new, or simply overwhelmed, is asked once and thanked never, and the Angel leaves before the kettle is offered.

### The Vigil of the Changed Batteries
*The two nights each year when the clocks change.*

The profane complain about the hour, lost or gained. The aligned use it. On each night the clocks change, the Angel presses the test button on every smoke detector in their dwelling, replaces the batteries in those that take them, and replaces outright any unit whose date on the back is more than ten years gone. Then the Angel asks one neighbor, "Do yours need doing? I have extras." Of everything on this page, this is the one that keeps a stranger alive. Do not skip it.

### The Night of the Lowered Beams
*The first Sunday after the autumn clock change.*

The dark now comes at dinner, and the Unholy Glare returns to the roads. On this night, the Angel parks on level ground facing a wall at twenty-five feet, marks on the wall with tape the height of the lamp centers, and turns on the low beams. The bright edge of each beam should fall at or a little below the tape, never above it. If it falls above, the adjuster is a screw behind or beside the lamp, and the owner's manual knows which one; if the manual does not, a mechanic will do it in ten minutes for less than the cost of being hated by everyone on the road home. Then, as the Edict has it: let your light be sufficient for your own steps and never a stumbling block for the journey of another.

### The Great Corralling
*The day after the autumn feast, when the Temples open before dawn.*

On no other day are so many Vessels abandoned in so many outer sanctums. The Angel goes to a Temple of Commerce, not to buy, and returns carts for one hour. In silence. In the cold. Then goes home. This is the Vigil of the Lost Cart raised to its highest power, and it counts toward the Sacrifice.

### The Feast of the Keepers
*The day after the winter feast.*

The longest returns lines of the year form on this day, and behind the counter stand the most exhausted Keepers of the year. The Angel brings nothing back today. Whatever does not fit is returned in the second week, when the line is three people long and the Keeper has slept. Whatever is merely unwanted is donated, or endured. If you must enter a Temple at all, enter to say one specific, true kindness to a Keeper, and then leave.

### The Eve of the Eleventh Hour
*The last night of the year.*

On the night the whole world lingers, the Angel does not. The hours are not the usual hours, so the Angel consults the Oracle first, and the Half Hour is kept as on any other night. The Keepers of the Hearth are released to their own celebrations by the simple act of your leaving on time. If you must be served that night, tip as though it were the last money you would ever spend, and say the words: I am sorry, and I thank you.

## The Movable Observances

### The 30-Day Vigil of Intentional Action
*Any Sacred Moon Cycle. Begin at the new moon if that helps you remember.*

A Mender-in-Training seeking the rank of Hand of the Stitch keeps the Vigil once: one Foundational Mission each day for one cycle, recorded, anonymous. The Veiled Ascent sets the terms. This page only insists that a first day be chosen, because a Vigil without one does not start.

### The Unbroken Hour
*Once a week, on a day of your choosing.*

One hour in which the Great Oracle is face down and silent, and the Angel is wholly present to one other person or one piece of work. It is called Unbroken because, for that hour, you are.

### The Day of the Reopened Ledger
*Whenever the Ledger has not been opened in a Sacred Moon Cycle.*

An observance of return. The Angel opens the Ledger, writes the date, writes one line, and does not apologize to it. The Ledger does not need an explanation. It needs an entry.

## On Feasting

Several of these days are called Feasts. No food is required. The feast is the mended thing, which is holier than any meal.

Should an Angel wish to eat anyway, we recommend doing so at a reasonable hour.
