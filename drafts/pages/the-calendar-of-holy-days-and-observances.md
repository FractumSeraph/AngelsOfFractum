The Fractum keeps no calendar. It breaks what it breaks on whatever day it likes.

But the world has seasons, and the seasons have their own breaks: the smoke detector that chirps in November, the hose that freezes in December, the carts that multiply in the week before a holiday like loaves. The Angels of Fractum therefore keep a small liturgical year, not because the days are holy in themselves, but because a day with a name is a day one remembers to act.

None of these observances require a gathering. All of them can be completed alone. Most take an hour.

## The Fixed Observances

### The Week of the Severed Thread
*The first full week of January.*

The year begins with the quietest Fractum: the drift of bonds. For seven days, the Angel reaches out to one dropped thread each day. A message, a call, two sentences. No agenda, no apology demanded. By the seventh day, seven bridges have been proven to stand. Some will answer. Some will not. The thread was never cut. Only dropped.

### The Day of the Returned Thing
*The spring equinox, or the nearest Saturday.*

On the day when light and dark are balanced, the Angel balances the ledger of borrowed things. The drill. The book. The casserole dish that has been in your cupboard since the funeral. The charger. Everything borrowed is returned, cleaned, and where possible improved: the blade sharpened, the tank filled. Nothing is returned with a lecture.

### The Small Hours of Spring
*The first Saturday of April.*

The thaw reveals what winter hid: the litter at the trailhead, the bags the snow swallowed, the cans in the ditch. The Angel takes a bag and gloves to one public place and leaves it as the earth intended. This is the great annual Repair of Omission, and it is performed without a vest, a banner, or a photograph.

### The Feast of the Coiled Hose
*The first frost, or the week before it is forecast.*

The Angel readies their own house for winter and then, quietly, readies one other. Disconnect and coil the hose. Check the walk for the ice that will come. Replace the porch bulb. The neighbor who is elderly, or new, or simply overwhelmed, finds in the morning that winter has been prepared for, and does not know by whom.

### The Vigil of the Changed Batteries
*The two nights each year when the clocks change.*

The profane complain about the lost hour. The aligned use it. On each night the clocks change, the Angel replaces the batteries in every smoke detector in their dwelling, and then asks one neighbor, "Do yours need doing? I have extras." It is the only Fractum on this site that can kill you. Do not skip it.

### The Night of the Lowered Beams
*The first Sunday after the autumn clock change.*

The dark now comes at dinner, and the Unholy Glare returns to the roads. On this night, the Angel parks facing a wall at twenty-five feet, examines the shape of their own headlights, and adjusts what can be adjusted. Let your light be sufficient for your own steps and never a stumbling block for the journey of another.

### The Great Corralling
*The day after the autumn feast, when the Temples open before dawn.*

On no other day are so many Vessels abandoned in so many outer sanctums. The Angel goes to a Temple of Commerce, not to buy, and returns carts for one hour. In silence. In the cold. Then goes home. This is the Vigil of the Lost Cart raised to its highest power, and it counts toward the Sacrifice.

### The Eve of the Eleventh Hour
*The last night of the year.*

On the night the whole world lingers, the Angel does not. No Temple is entered after half past nine. The Keepers of the Hearth are released to their own celebrations by the simple act of your absence. If you must be served that night, tip as though it were the last money you would ever spend, and say the words: I am sorry, and I thank you.

### The Feast of the Keepers
*The day after the winter feast.*

The longest returns lines of the year form on this day, and behind the counter stand the most exhausted Keepers of the year. The Angel brings nothing back. Whatever was given that does not fit is kept, donated, or endured. If you must enter a Temple at all, enter to say one specific, true kindness to a Keeper, and then leave.

## The Movable Observances

### The Sacred Moon Cycle
*Any month, begun at the new moon.*

A Mender-in-Training seeking the rank of Hand of the Stitch begins the 30-Day Vigil of Intentional Action at any new moon: one Foundational Mission each day for one cycle, recorded, anonymous. The moon is not required. It is simply easier to remember than the 14th.

### The Unbroken Hour
*Once a week, on a day of your choosing.*

One hour in which the Great Oracle is face down and silent, and the Angel is wholly present to one other person or one piece of work. It is called Unbroken because, for that hour, you are.

### The Day of the Lowered Count
*Whenever the Ledger has not been opened in a Sacred Moon Cycle.*

An observance of return. The Angel opens the Ledger, writes the date, writes one line, and does not apologize to it. The Ledger does not need an explanation. It needs an entry.

## On Feasting

Several of these days are called Feasts. No food is required. The feast is the mended thing, which is holier than any meal.

Should an Angel wish to eat anyway, we recommend doing so at a reasonable hour.
