The Orientation tells every new Seeker to keep a Sacrifice Ledger. It does not say how. This page says how.

## What the Ledger is

The Sacrifice Ledger is a private record of what you have mended: time given, cracks closed. It is the first document the Angels of Fractum ask you to keep, and the only one we will never ask to see.

It is not a scorecard. The Ledger exists because the Fractum has a trick: it makes every act of repair vanish. The cart, returned, looks as if it was never stray. The neighbor's gate, latched, looks as if it always latched. The world you have mended looks exactly like a world no one mended, and after a year of this the Doubt will whisper that you have done nothing at all.

The Ledger is your answer. Not for pride. For proof that you were here, and that here is slightly better for it.

## What goes in it

Three kinds of entries.

**Cracks closed.** Any Minor Breach mended, any Mission completed, any Edict kept at cost to yourself. One line each. "Returned two carts, Tuesday." "Turned the car around at 9:41." "Texted M. after eight months. She wrote back."

**Time given.** Hours of your life spent on mending that someone else needed: the shift at the pantry, the afternoon at the community garden's broken gate, the hour on hold with the utility company beside a neighbor who could not face it alone. Record the hours honestly. Round down.

**Breaks within.** The Fractum Personalis you noticed and worked on. "Apologized to J. without explaining myself." "Did not check the Oracle at dinner." These count. Some Angels keep them on a separate page. Some keep them on the same page, so that the two kinds of mending learn to recognize each other.

Failures may also be recorded. "Went through the drive-thru at 9:52. Said I was sorry. Tipped." This is not confession; it is maintenance history. A thing cannot be mended if no one wrote down where it broke.

## How hours become Sacrifice

When the Angels of Fractum report that our Human Sacrifice count has risen, we are reporting the sum of hours of human life voluntarily given to mending the world, as Angels have chosen to tell us. One hour given is one hour sacrificed. We call it sacrifice because it was: you had one life, and you spent an hour of it on a crack that was not yours.

Telling us is optional and anonymous. There is no form. You say a number to another Angel, out loud, once, and it is added and your name is not. We do not practice, condone, or perform the literal sacrifice of any human life. We would prefer not to keep saying this. The unaligned keep asking.

## The rules of the Ledger

- **It is private.** Showing it to another person for admiration is Fractum Personalis in its most paper-based form. Showing it to another Angel for guidance is permitted.
- **It is honest.** Do not record a mending you did not finish. The Ledger records the follow-through, not the intention.
- **It is brief.** One line per entry. The Angel who writes three paragraphs about a returned cart has begun to perform, and the audience is themselves.
- **It is kept.** Daily is best. Weekly is acceptable. Monthly is a Sacred Moon Cycle of forgetting; you will not remember Tuesday.
- **It is not compared.** Your Ledger is not longer or shorter than anyone's. It is yours.

## The template

Any notebook will do. Any file will do. A note in the Great Oracle in your pocket is acceptable, though the aligned prefer paper, because paper does not notify you of anything.

The first page may read:

**Sacrifice Ledger of a Seeker.** Begun on ______, with a First Mending of: ______.

| Date | What was mended | Time given | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | |
| | | | |

**Breaks within.**

| Date | The break | The stitch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| | | |

**Hours given this cycle:** ____.

That is the entire template. It fits on an index card. Most things worth doing do.

## When the Ledger is full

Begin another. Do not throw the first away. In twenty years you will be the kind of person who kept a ledger of returned shopping carts, and you will want the evidence.
