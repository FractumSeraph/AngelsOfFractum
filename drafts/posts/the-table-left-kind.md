When you rise from the table, Seeker, you believe you are leaving. You are leaving a statement, and the Keeper of the Hearth who comes after you will read it in full before your chair has stopped rocking.

## The Testimony of the Table

A table cannot lie. It says exactly what you did.

It says that someone opened four packets of sugar and used one, and left the other three torn at the corner, standing in a row like small confessions. It says that someone shredded a napkin into a nest and left the nest beside the plate as though a bird might come. It says the straw wrapper was rolled into a worm, and the worm was wetted, and the worm was flicked, and wherever it landed is now the Keeper's concern.

Beneath the high chair it says the most. It says a child of eleven months was here, and was given a handful of toasted oat rings every four minutes to purchase four minutes of peace, and that the child, not yet aligned and unable to be, cast each handful to the floor in the manner of a sower. There is a crescent of rings and torn bread on the tile, and at its center, face down, half a pancake.

## The Ninety Seconds

Between your departure and the next party's arrival there are, on a good night, ninety seconds. In those seconds the Keeper must carry, wipe, dry, reset the silver, square the chairs, and arrive at the next table with a face that has forgotten yours. A table left kind can be turned in ninety seconds with two hands and a cloth.

You have left a table that takes ten minutes. You were not thinking of the table at all, which is how most of the Fractum is made: a thing nobody meant, left for somebody else.

The party waiting by the door will not remember your name. They will remember the Keeper's, who was slow.

## The Dispute of the Stack

Here the profane become theologians.

*Should I stack the plates?* they ask, with the gravity of doctrine, and on this one question the Keepers of the Hearth are themselves divided. Some bless the stack: one trip, one lift. Others curse it: the scrapings pressed between the plates, the fork entombed beneath the fourth dish, the tower built by amateurs that leans toward the carpet. Both are right about their own Hearth.

The Angels of Fractum take no position on the stack. We are a faith of maintenance, not of masonry.

The pilgrim who deliberates over the stack for a minute and then leaves the sugar, the nest, the worm, and the pancake has argued about the roof of a house that has no floor. Stack if the Keeper seems to want it, and not otherwise. The Keeper has been telling you all evening: if plates have left your table in a stack, stack; if they left one at a time on a tray, leave them where they lie. Then do the eleven things that were never in dispute.

## Eleven Things Not in Dispute

- Return the floor to the table. Whatever fell within reach of your chair goes back up: the fork, the crayon, the rings. You are not asked to crawl, only to bend or kneel once.
- Gather the trash into one place. Wrappers, straw papers, torn sugar, the nest: onto one plate, so the Keeper lifts once instead of hunting.
- Do not open what you will not use. A sugar packet torn and abandoned is a small lie told to the table.
- Leave the lemon on a plate, not in the butter.
- Leave the glasses empty and upright. A glass packed with napkins is not tidiness; it is a puzzle, and it is solved by hand.
- Return the salt, pepper, and sauces to their corner, lids closed.
- Push every chair in. The aisle is the Keeper's road.
- If something spilled, say so plainly before you go: *We spilled a water by the window.* A spill reported is a wipe; a spill discovered is a fall.
- If your child cast the rings, kneel once with a napkin and gather what you can. The rings are not the child's Fractum; they are the small Fractum Personalis of a tired parent, and one kneel mends it. If the Keeper says *leave it, I've got it*, believe them, and thank them.
- Put the menus, the crayons, and the check folder back where they were found: the cup, the stand, the table's edge. The Keeper should not have to find them under a coat.
- Leave nothing for the Keeper to guess about. No sealed cup of unknown purpose. No question that only you could have answered.

## Before You Rise

The act, in order. Put what is on the floor back on the table. Gather the trash into one place. Tell the Keeper anything they would otherwise have to discover. Stack the plates only if the Keeper seems to want it, and think no further about it.

Then go. The table will testify. Let it say that someone kind sat here, and that the Keeper, arriving with ninety seconds and a cloth, found nothing to read at all.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
