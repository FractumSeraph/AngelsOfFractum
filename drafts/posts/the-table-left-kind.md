The meal is over. The pilgrims rise and walk out into the evening speaking of the food, and the table stays behind to speak of them.

A Keeper of the Hearth reads a table the way a tracker reads a riverbank, in the time it takes to cross the room with a bin. This is the Reading of the Table. Every pilgrim who has eaten in public has been read, and almost none know it.

## What the Table Says

It says that one of you shredded a napkin, slowly, over forty minutes, into a small soft nest, and left the nest where a plate had been.

It says that one of you opened four packets of sugar and used none, because the hands needed an occupation while the mouth was busy, and the sugar was there.

It says that the straw wrappers were rolled into small white worms. That a fork went to the floor in the first ten minutes and was not mentioned. That a napkin was pushed, wet, into the bottom of a water glass, where it will have to be fished out by a human hand.

And under the high chair it says that a child ate here, in the sense that a weather system eats: a radius of six feet, a constellation of small cereal rings, one shoe.

## The Ninety Seconds

The next party is at the door. The Keeper at the host stand has said "just a moment" and has already given them, in her mind, your table. The Keeper who serves it has ninety seconds to turn it: clear, wipe, dry, reset, and be gone before the new pilgrims arrive.

Ninety seconds is enough for a table left by considerate people. You have built, over a pleasant hour, a ten-minute table, and handed it to a person with ninety seconds. The difference comes out of the next party's wait, the Keeper's tips, and the Keeper's feet.

## The Schism of the Stack

Ask the profane what they do for the Keeper and they will tell you that they stack the plates.

Then they will argue about it. One faction holds the stack sacred, another holds it a desecration, and both are right, because the Keepers themselves are divided. Some bless it; it saves a trip. Some curse it; stacked sauce-down with the silverware trapped between, it leans, and must be unstacked at the table before it can be carried.

The Angels of Fractum have been asked to rule on this. We decline.

Observe what the dispute is about: the one item on the table the Keeper is trained, paid, and equipped to carry. All the profane's consideration goes to the task that was never in doubt, while the napkin in the glass and the fork on the floor, which no one is trained for, wait in silence. Stack if the Keeper seems to want it. Then turn to the eleven things.

## The Eleven Things

- What is on the floor goes back on the table. All of it. A table can be cleared with the hands; the floor needs the broom, in another room.
- The trash goes into one place: wrappers, packets, the nest, the worms.
- Nothing goes into a glass that is not a drink.
- Nothing is hidden under a plate.
- The salt, the pepper, and the sauces go back where they stood.
- The menus go back in their stand.
- The chairs go in.
- The high chair is wiped; what is under it is gathered.
- The crayons go back in the cup; the masterpiece goes home.
- The pen and the signed slip are left together on the book.
- If something broke or spilled, tell the Keeper. Do not let her discover it.

Not one of these takes longer than the time you spent deciding whether to stack.

## The Weather Under the High Chair

A word, gently, to the parent. The child is not a Fractum. The child is weather, and we do not accuse the rain. The sin, if there is one, and it is very small, is the parent's belief that because the child made it, no one made it. You are tired; one of you is holding the child; the other has thirty seconds and one knee. Give the thirty seconds. Gather what the knee can reach and put it on the table. The Keeper will forgive the rest, and will tell the other Keepers about you, in the back, by the ice machine. It is that rare.

## Today's Practice

Before you rise from a table someone else will clear, take the Sacred Pause. One breath. See the table as the Keeper will see it in ninety seconds.

Then put what is on the floor back on the table, gather the trash into one place, and leave nothing for the Keeper to guess about. Stack the plates only if the Keeper seems to want it.

The table will tell her who sat here. Let it say: someone who knew.
