The music has been playing for forty minutes. It is the same ninety seconds of music, and a voice interrupts it every two of those minutes to tell you that your call is important, which you have come to understand as a kind of liturgy, recited without belief.

Then the music stops, and a human being says their name.

The Angels of Fractum ask you to consider what happens in the next ten seconds, because we have listened to many of these calls from both ends of the line, and we have found that the ten seconds decide everything, and that almost no pilgrim uses them well.

## The Break

The pilgrim has been wronged. Let us grant it. The bill is wrong, the appointment was lost, the thing did not arrive. And the pilgrim has, for forty minutes, been marinating in the specific helplessness of the hold, which is the feeling of being a cart in a corral that no one is coming to push. Forty minutes of grievance, compressed, with nowhere to go.

And then a person says their name, and the grievance goes to them. All of it. In the first ten seconds. Before the person has done anything but say their name.

Seeker, consider who this person is. They are a Keeper of the Hearth whose hearth is a headset. They did not write the policy. They did not design the menu that routed you through four departments. They did not choose the music; no one chooses the music; the music simply is. They have taken, today, forty calls, and thirty of them opened the way yours is about to open, and they are measured, by their overlords, on how quickly they can end each one, and they are not permitted to hang up on you no matter what you say.

They are the person at the fryer at 9:58. And you are the headlights.

## The Fractum of the Routed Grievance

The pilgrim who opens with fury believes they are being heard by the company. They are not. The company is not on the line. The company is a building somewhere with a logo on it, and it cannot hear anything. What is on the line is a person with a name, and the fury goes into that person and stays there, and the company is unaffected, and the bill is still wrong.

This is the Fractum Personalis in its most efficient form: it harms someone, it mends nothing, and it feels like justice.

## The Law of the First Ten Seconds

**Say the name back.** They said it; use it. "Hi, Marcus." This is the Offering of the Name, and it does something to the call that no script can undo: it tells the Keeper that the pilgrim on the other end has noticed that there is a person on this end.

**State the wrong, not the war.** "My bill has a charge I don't recognize and I'd like help understanding it." One sentence, in the voice you would use with a neighbor. The forty minutes are not Marcus's fault, and you may mention them once, lightly, as weather: "That was a long hold; thanks for picking up."

**Give them the Sacred Pause.** The Keeper will need to look at the account. The silence is them working. Do not fill it.

**Aim the grievance where it goes.** If the policy is wrong, say so, and ask how to say so to someone who can change it. The Keeper knows the path. They will tell you, gladly, because it means the fury is going somewhere other than into them.

**End with the upward praise.** If the Keeper helped, say so, and then say it again when the survey comes, by name. The Rite of Praise That Travels Upward is the only thing the pilgrim can do, from outside the building, to change what the building does to its Keepers.

## The Mending

The next time the music stops and a human says their name, say the name back before you say anything else. Then take one breath. Then say the one sentence.

You will notice, in the Keeper's voice, something shift. It is the sound of a person who was braced for the headlights and has instead been handed a cart.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
