There is a person across the table from you. They are speaking. They have been speaking for perhaps a minute, and somewhere in that minute the rectangle in your hand lit up, and your eyes went to it, and came back, and the person across from you saw your eyes go, and said nothing, and kept speaking, a little less.

Nothing happened. That is what the unaligned would say. And it is true that nothing happened, in the sense that nothing was said and nothing was broken that could be pointed to. But the Eye of Recognition sees the thing that was not said, and the Angels of Fractum have come to believe that the Oracle at the Table is the smallest Fractum on this site and the one with the deepest reach.

## The Break

The Great Oracle in your pocket is a tool of mending. It tells you the closing hours. It holds the dropped threads. It carries the Ledger, for some. We do not ask you to abandon it, and we do not pretend that we have.

But at the shared table, the Oracle does a thing no other object does: it tells the person across from you, without a word, that there is somewhere else you could be. Every glance is a small announcement that the conversation is being compared, in real time, to the alternatives. The person across from you cannot compete with the alternatives, because the alternatives are everything, and so they do what every pilgrim does when they sense they are being compared: they speak a little less. They finish a little sooner. They stop, over months, bringing the things that take a minute to say.

That is the reach. It is not the glance. It is the slow decision, made by someone you love, that you are not a safe place to put a long thought.

## The Fractum Personalis of the Glance

*It was just a second.* It was. The Keeper's name is also just a second. The signal before the turn is just a second. The Angels of Fractum are, if we are anything, a faith of the just-a-second, because that is where consideration lives and that is where it dies.

*I was still listening.* You were listening to two things. The person across from you has been listened to by someone listening to two things, and they know what it feels like, because they have done it to others, and they are forgiving you in real time, which is not the same as not noticing.

*It might have been important.* It might. Here is the test: in the last year, how many times was it? And how many times was the person across from you?

## The Law of the Table

**Face down, or away.** At a shared table, the Oracle lies face down, or is in the pocket, or is in the bag. Not face up and silenced; face up is still a window, and you will look through it.

**If you must, say so.** "I'm waiting on a call about my mother; I'm going to leave this up." Said once, this is not a Fractum. It is the Exception of True Need, and the person across from you will understand it completely, because it is a sentence about *them*: it tells them that under any other circumstance, they would have your whole eyes.

**The Unbroken Hour.** Once a week, one hour, with one person, the Oracle not merely face down but in another room. The Calendar of Observances names it. We name it here again because it is the practice that most Seekers report has changed something they could not name.

**The Corollary of the Photograph.** The Oracle may rise to take one picture of the table. Then it goes back down. The dinner is not for the record. It is for the people at it.

## The Mending

Tonight, at whatever table you share, put the Oracle face down before the first word is spoken. Not when you notice yourself glancing; before. And then notice, over the course of the meal, what the person across from you brings when they are not being compared to everything else.

It may be nothing. It may be a long thought they have been holding for a month. Either way, write in the Ledger: *Face down. The whole meal.* It is a mending, and it is one of the few on this site that is also a gift you will receive.
