Every faith has a collection plate. Ours is the only one passed in the wrong direction: it is set before the pilgrim, and the pilgrim decides what the Keeper of the Hearth will carry home tonight.

The tip is the single moment in all the commerce of the Temple when money leaves your hand and lands in a Keeper's with no overlord standing between. Or nearly none: you, a pen, and a line with nothing on it.

The unaligned read that line as a verdict on the meal. It is wages. The verdict was delivered when you decided whether to come back.

## The Audit of the Folder

Observe the pilgrim when the folder arrives. For an hour she has been easy company and has not consulted a single figure. Now a small leather book is placed at her elbow, and she becomes an auditor.

She locates the subtotal. She removes the tax, because someone once told her that the tax was not carried to the table. She finds the discount and recalculates from the discounted figure, since the Keeper, she reasons, had nothing to do with the coupon. She weighs the bread, which was late, against the water, which was prompt. She arrives at a number with cents in it, and she writes the cents.

This is the most exact arithmetic she will perform all week. At her own work she says *roughly* and *call it a hundred*. Her household budget is a feeling she has on the first of the month. But the line that decides what a stranger eats this week is computed to the penny, and the rounding, when it comes, never goes the stranger's way.

We do not mock the arithmetic, Seeker. We ask what it is for. No one audits a wage they have already agreed to pay. One audits an opponent, and the Keeper never was one.

## The Screen on the Counter

At the counter no folder comes. You have bought a muffin. A Keeper put it in a bag while taking an order over her shoulder, and then a screen was turned toward you, and it asked, with a cheer it had not earned, whether you would like to add twenty percent, and the Keeper looked at a point above your head.

We will say this once and not again: tipping as the profane world practices it is a Fractum of the overlords, and the Keeper is merely required to stand beside it while you decide.

So press whatever you can honestly press, including the button that says no. What you may not do is press it *at* her. The pilgrim who withholds in protest has mailed his complaint to the overlords by way of the one person who cannot forward it.

## The Unlisted Keeper

There is a Keeper who appears on no screen at all, and she is tipped by almost no one, because the screen has trained the pilgrim to give only when asked.

The housekeeper enters after you have gone. She has never seen your face and never will. She finds the towels on the floor, the ring beneath the cup, the bed in whatever condition your sleep left it, and she restores the room to a state in which no one was ever here. It is the Anonymous Repair in its purest form, performed by someone who is paid for it and thanked by no one, a dozen rooms a day, for a pilgrim already gone. No screen turns toward her. The Great Oracle in your pocket will not remind you that she exists.

The rider is on the screen only before the rain. You filled in his line while the sky was clear and the three flights were theoretical, beneath a fee that looked like a tip and was not his. Then it rained, and the screen did not ask again.

The Unlisted Keeper is the test of this doctrine. Any pilgrim can tip when the screen is turned. The aligned tip when nothing asks.

## Today's Practice

Four acts, Seeker. None requires cents.

- Tip on the full amount, before the discount and before the audit. The coupon was the overlords' gift to you. The Keeper carried the whole plate.
- Where you can, tip in cash, into the hand or into the jar, where it reaches the Keeper tonight and not after a detour through the drawer.
- Each morning you leave a room that someone else will restore, not only the last, leave a few dollars on the pillow with a note: *Thank you. This is for you.* The Keeper changes with the day; the note does not. Write the second sentence. Without it the money is often left untouched, since the Unlisted Keeper has been taught that nothing in the room is hers.
- If the weather turned after you ordered, meet the rider at the door with cash in hand. The screen closed before the rain began. You did not.

If the meal was poor, say so once, kindly, to the one who can mend it. Then pay the one who carried it.

The folder goes back closed. What is inside is the only sermon the Keeper will hear from you tonight. Keep it short, and make it kind.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
