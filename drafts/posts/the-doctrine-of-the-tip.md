There is one moment in the whole commerce of the Temple when the pilgrim pays a Keeper of the Hearth directly, hand to hand, with no overlord between them. The unaligned have taken this moment and turned it into a trial.

## The Arithmetic of the Table

Observe the pilgrim at the end of the meal. For forty minutes he has been a creature of appetite and conversation. Now the folder arrives, and he becomes a mathematician.

He reverses the tax, because he has read that one does not tip on tax. He notes that a discount was applied and computes on the discounted figure, since the Keeper, after all, did not bring him the discount. He arrives at eighteen percent, recalls that the water was refilled only twice, and settles on a figure with a decimal in it. He writes it down with the care of a man signing a treaty.

This same pilgrim, at his place of work, estimates. He rounds. He says *roughly* and *ballpark*. The only ledger in his life kept to the cent is the one that decides what a stranger takes home tonight, and he keeps it to the cent in order to take some of it back.

This is the Break, Seeker. Not the percentage. The posture. The pilgrim believes the tip is a verdict on the meal. It is wages. The verdict was delivered when you decided whether to come back. The tip is the part of the Keeper's rent that the overlords have arranged for you to pay, and the Keeper did not arrange it. For one moment the pilgrim is the overlord. Be the overlord you wish the Keeper had.

## The Screen That Turns

You have ordered a muffin. It was handed across a counter by a Keeper already handing something to someone else. And then the screen is turned toward you, and it asks, in a tone it believes to be cheerful, whether you would like to leave eighteen, twenty, or twenty-five percent, and the Keeper looks at the wall.

The Turning of the Screen is a Fractum, and a grave one, but it is a Fractum of the overlords. The Keeper did not write the screen. The Keeper must stand there while you decide. To punish the Keeper for the screen is to kick the Vessel because the corral is far away.

We will say once that the tip as the profane world practices it is a broken system. The brokenness is real. It is not the Keeper's to bear, and the pilgrim who makes the Keeper bear it, withholding wages to protest the screen, has confused the Hand of Intentional Mending with a clenched fist. Press whatever number you can honestly press. Press it without a sigh. The Keeper will know you sighed.

## The Keeper on No Screen

There is a class of Keeper who is never on any screen, and so is tipped by almost no one, because the screen has trained the pilgrim to tip only when asked.

The housekeeper enters the room you have left. She does not know your face. She finds the towels where you dropped them, the coffee ring where you set the cup, and a bed that looks as though an argument was held in it. She restores all of it so completely that the next pilgrim will believe no one was ever here. This is the Anonymous Repair performed for a living, eight times a day, by a Keeper no screen will ever turn toward.

The rider who carried your dinner up three flights in the rain was on a screen, but only before the rain, and the line that looked like a tip was a fee, and the fee was not his.

The Keeper on No Screen is tipped by one method only: the Seeker remembers her without being prompted. The Great Oracle in your pocket will not remind you. You must be the reminder.

## Today's Practice

Four acts, Seeker, and none of them requires a decimal.

- Tip on the full amount, before the discount and before the arithmetic. The discount was the overlords' gift to you; the Keeper carried the full plate. If the figure in your head has a decimal in it, round up.
- Carry cash for the purpose. Hand it to the Keeper, or leave it under the glass, where it reaches the hands that earned it without a detour through the overlords.
- Before you leave a room that someone else will restore, leave something on the pillow for the Keeper on No Screen, and a note. *Thank you. This is for you.* The second sentence matters; a bill without a note is often left where it lies, out of fear that it was not hers to take.
- If the meal was bad, say so to the one who can mend it, kindly, once, and tip the one who carried it anyway.

The Keepers will never see your arithmetic. See to it that they never have to.
