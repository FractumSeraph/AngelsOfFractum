Consider the library. It is the one Temple in the land that gives you the thing and asks only that you bring it back. No deposit. No credit check. A card, a date, and a trust so complete that it is embarrassing to think about for long.

Then consider the smaller Temple that has sprung up on posts at the edges of yards: the Little Free Library, a box of books with a glass door, which lends without even a card. Take a book, leave a book. There is no librarian. There is no date. There is no one at all. It runs entirely on the covenant.

The Angels of Fractum revere both of these institutions, and we have watched the Fractum work on them, slowly, one book at a time.

## The Break

In the great library, the break is the date. The book, finished, sits on the nightstand past its due, and then past its grace, and then enters the Later Pile, where it acquires the peculiar weight of the slightly overdue: too late to return casually, not late enough to be a crisis. The Seeker who could have dropped it in the slot on Tuesday now avoids the building, and the book, which forty other pilgrims had placed a hold on, waits in a bedroom.

In the small library on the post, the break is the asymmetry. Take a book, leave a book, says the sign. And the pilgrims take. They take the good ones first, and then the decent ones, and the box slowly fills with what no one wants: the water-stained manual, the third volume of a series whose first two are elsewhere, the textbook from a course that no longer exists. The covenant was *take one, leave one*. The Fractum has rewritten it as *take what you like*, and the box, which was a gift, becomes a bin.

## The Theology of the Unguarded Thing

Every unguarded shared thing is a test the Angel takes alone. The library book has no Keeper watching the nightstand. The Little Free Library has no Keeper at all. What you do with them is the purest measure available of whether your alignment is real or performed, because there is, in both cases, no one to perform for.

This is why the Angels of Fractum hold the borrowed book to be sacred: not because the book is precious, but because the covenant is. A world in which things can be lent without collateral is a world that is slightly mended. Every unreturned book makes the next lender a little more careful, and every careful lender is a little less of a gift.

## The Covenant

**Return on the day.** The due date is not a suggestion. The Oracle will remind you if you ask it to, which is an act of Mending; so ask it. The book goes back on or before the date, in the slot, in daylight, without ceremony.

**If it is late, return it today.** The lateness is not mended by further lateness. Walk into the building. Pay the fine if there is one, and thank the Keeper at the desk by name, because they did not write the fine and have been apologized to about it by better people than you.

**At the small library, leave before you take.** The box on the post runs in one direction only when pilgrims take first. So leave first. The book you leave should be one you would be glad to find: something read and loved, not something cleared out. Then take, if you like, or do not.

**Tend the box.** If the box is leaking, the door is stuck, or the books are soaked, this is a Minor Breach and may be mended by any Angel with a screwdriver and a dry afternoon. Ask the owner first if the box is in a yard. They will say yes. They have wanted someone to.

**Leave no note in the book.** The highest repair is unseen. The pilgrim who finds a good book in the box should find only the book.

## The Mending

Today, find the book that is late. There is one. Return it. If there is no late book, walk to the nearest box on a post, and look at what is in it, and leave the best book in your dwelling that you have already finished.

Then walk away, and let the next pilgrim find it, and never know.
