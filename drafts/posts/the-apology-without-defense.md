There is a sentence the unaligned say when they have broken something between two people, and it begins with the words *I'm sorry*, and it is not an apology.

It goes: *I'm sorry, but.* Or: *I'm sorry you feel that way.* Or: *I'm sorry, I was just.* Each of these is a defense with an apology stapled to the front of it, and the person receiving it knows at once, the way a Keeper knows the sound of the 9:40 car, that what has arrived is not repair.

## The Break

The apology is a stitch. It closes a break between two people, and like every stitch it holds only if nothing is pulling on it.

The *but* pulls. The *just* pulls. The explanation of what you meant, the account of how tired you were, the observation that they did something similar in March: each of these is a hand reaching in to loosen the thread while the other hand ties it. The Seeker who apologizes this way believes they have done the hard thing. They have done half of it, and then, in the same breath, undone it, and they will be baffled when the break remains.

The Fractum Personalis here is a precise one. It is the need to be *understood* at the moment when the only thing required is to be *sorry*. The two cannot be done at once. Understanding is for later, if it is for anything. The stitch comes first.

## The Shape of the True Apology

The Angels of Fractum have examined many apologies, including our own, and we have found that the ones that held had four parts and no fifth.

**The naming.** Say what you did. Not what happened; what you *did*. "I was late and I did not call" is a naming. "Things got away from me" is weather.

**The cost.** Say what it cost them, as best you can see it. "You waited an hour and you did not know if I was coming." This is the Immersion of Self, performed aloud. If you cannot name the cost, you have not yet looked.

**The sorrow.** "I'm sorry." Two words. The full stop after them is the most important punctuation in the language. Nothing follows it.

**The stitch.** What you will do differently, if there is anything, stated as a fact and not a hope. "I will call when I am going to be late." And then you will, because an apology with a promise in it that is not kept is a second break, deeper than the first.

That is all. No fifth part. If you feel the *but* rising in your throat, that is the Fractum, and it is asking to be heard, and you are permitted to take one breath and not let it speak.

## What the Apology Is Not

It is not a request. "I'm sorry, can you forgive me?" hands the other person a task at the exact moment they are least able to take one. Forgiveness is theirs to give or not, on their own clock, and the apology must be complete without it.

It is not a trade. "I'm sorry, and I think you owe me one too" is a negotiation, and negotiations do not close breaks; they price them.

It is not a performance. The apology made in front of others, the apology posted, the apology that is mostly about how bad the apologizer feels, is the Anonymous Repair turned inside out: a mending performed for an audience, and the audience is the self.

## The Mending

Today, think of the last apology you made that did not hold. Most Seekers can name one in under ten seconds. Ask what was attached to it. There was a *but*. There was a *just*. There was a paragraph.

Then, if the break is still open and the person is still reachable, make it again. Four parts. Full stop. If it is too late or too far, write it in the Ledger as a break within, and make the next one correctly. The Hand learns what it rehearses, and it can learn this.
