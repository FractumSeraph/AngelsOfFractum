The invitation arrives in the usual way. It appears in the glowing room of the group chat, or in a message meant for you alone, or on a small card with a date on it and, at the bottom, four letters that ask for an answer.

Seeker, there are three answers to a question. Two of them are answers.

## The Three Answers

The first is yes. Yes is a commitment, and the pilgrim knows it. Yes means a Saturday has been spent before it arrives. Yes means driving, and a dish, and shoes. The pilgrim regards yes with the caution it deserves.

The second is no. No is unkind, or feels so. No must be explained, and the explanation must be good, and the pilgrim has nothing good, only a quiet preference for the couch. So no is set aside as well.

The third answer is nothing. The pilgrim reads the invitation, feels the small weight of it, and turns the Great Oracle face down on the table. Nothing is not a refusal; the pilgrim is not cruel. Nothing is not a promise; the pilgrim is not rash. Nothing feels like the humble answer, the one that commits no sin and closes no door.

It is the cruelest of the three.

## A Field Report From the Glowing Room

Observe the unanswered invitation long enough and you will catalog every costume that nothing wears.

- The small floating thumb, raised in approval of the invitation's existence, which commits to nothing except having seen it.
- *Sounds fun!*, offered as though the host had asked for a review.
- *Let me check*, from a pilgrim who has never, in the recorded history of the faith, checked.
- *I'll try to make it*, which is no with the door propped open so the draft can get in.
- Silence, from the pilgrim whose Oracle has already reported to the host, in small gray letters, that the invitation was read on Monday at 4:12.

Each of these pilgrims believes they are keeping their options open. They are. The host, meanwhile, is keeping a chair open. Only one of the two is paying for it.

## The Host, Counting

The host is counting chairs. The host has six chairs and eight invitations, and on the morning of the gathering the host must decide what your silence means. The host, being kind, decides it means maybe. The host, being kind, decides that maybe means yes.

This is the Open Chair: the place set for an answer that never came.

So the host borrows the folding chair from the neighbor. The host buys the second chicken. The host makes the larger pan of the thing, and sets the extra place, and at ten past the hour the host's eyes go to the door, and go to the door again, and at twenty to the next hour the host moves the folding chair quietly against the wall so that the table will not look like it is missing someone.

It is missing someone. There is a second chicken to prove it. The host keeps a count that is written nowhere, and your name is in it now.

You did not mean this. The pilgrim never means it. The pilgrim meant only to decide later, and later is the one hour that never arrives at anyone's table.

## Maybe, a Servant of the Fractum

The Doubt is a known servant of the Fractum. Maybe is another, and the two are cousins.

Maybe is the elevation of "me" above "we," performed with lowered eyes. It says: my Saturday is uncertain, and so your Saturday must be uncertain too. It spends the host's planning to preserve the pilgrim's freedom, and it calls the exchange politeness.

The aligned do not do this. Radical Empathy begins with the Immersion of Self: stand for one breath in the kitchen of the host, holding a second chicken, looking at a chair. Then answer.

## Today's Practice

- Answer every invitation within one day. Yes, or no. Not maybe. Not the thumb. Not *sounds fun*. A word the host can count.
- If you truly must check, the answer within the day is still an answer: *I need to ask one person; you will have a yes or a no by Thursday.* A date is a word the host can count. *Let me check* with no date is a maybe in a coat.
- If the day you named arrives and you still do not know, the answer is no, and you may say it warmly: *I can't commit to this one. Please don't hold a chair for me.* The host may hold one anyway. That is the host's decision to make, and now they can make it.
- If your answer changes, say so at once, to the host alone and not to the glowing room: *I said yes and now I can't. I'm sorry.* Let the host learn it from you and not from the chair.
- If you said no and the day comes free, ask whether there is still room. Do not simply arrive.

Nothing more is asked. A Saturday given or withheld, within a day, in words. The host will count your answer among the chairs and never know what it spared them: one folding chair, one chicken, one hour of watching the door.
