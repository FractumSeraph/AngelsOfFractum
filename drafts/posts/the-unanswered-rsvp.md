An invitation has arrived. Someone has looked at a calendar, chosen a day, chosen a table, and written your name on a list. Then they sent you the list and asked a question that is very old and has only two answers.

You have given it a third.

## The Three Answers

The first answer is no. The pilgrim does not like to say no. No is a small door closing in someone's face, and the pilgrim has been taught that closing doors is unkind.

The second answer is yes. The pilgrim does not like yes either. Yes is a nail driven into a future Saturday, and who can say what that Saturday will hold? Something better might arrive. The pilgrim would prefer to keep the Saturday soft.

The third answer is nothing. The pilgrim reads the invitation, feels the two doors, and chooses neither, and this feels like the gentlest of the three. The invitation goes to the Later Pile, beside the thank-you card and the form that is filled but not sent, to wait for a day called Tomorrow.

Seeker, hear this plainly. Nothing is not the absence of an answer. It is an answer, and it is the only one of the three that costs the other person money.

## The Host, Counting

Consider the one who asked.

She has a table, and the table has a number of chairs, and the number is not infinite. Beside each name on her list there is a yes, a no, or a blank. The yeses she can seat. The nos she can grieve briefly and release. The blanks she can do nothing with, and so she does the only thing a careful person can do with a blank: she counts it as a maybe, and plans for the maybe, because the alternative is a guest at the door with nowhere to sit.

If she asked in the small glowing room where the friends gather in text, it is worse. The Oracle tells her that eleven have read it. She can see the number. She cannot see a single yes.

So she buys the extra chicken. She buys it by the pound, and one of the pounds is you. She sets the extra place. She borrows the folding chair from the neighbor. She arranges the seating so that your silence sits beside someone your silence would like.

This is the Held Chair: a seat kept open at a table you have not decided to attend, paid for by a person you have not answered. You believe you are keeping your options open. She is keeping a chair open. Only one of you is paying for it, and it is not you.

## Maybe, a Servant of the Fractum

We have written elsewhere of the Doubt, a known servant of the Fractum, which speaks in a reasonable voice and always counsels delay. Maybe is its cousin. Maybe wears the robe of humility. *I don't want to commit and then let her down. I don't want to say no and hurt her.* Both of these sentences are about the pilgrim's comfort, and neither is about the chicken.

This is Fractum Personalis in its politest dress. The pilgrim has weighed a no, a yes, and the host's arithmetic, and has decided that the arithmetic is the one that can be left unsolved. "Me" above "we," wearing a cardigan.

Understand that a no is not unkind. A no is a number the host can use. The chair is released, the chicken is lighter by a pound, and the folding chair stays with the neighbor. A prompt no is among the kindest things a pilgrim can send, and the pilgrim who withholds it out of kindness has misunderstood kindness.

## The Changed Answer

Sometimes a yes must become a no. The child is sick; the car is not; the week collapsed. The Angels of Fractum do not condemn the changed answer. We condemn the unsent one.

A yes that becomes a no, spoken at once, is a small sorrow for the host and a solved equation. A yes that becomes a silence is discovered at seven o'clock on the day, by an empty chair with a plate before it, while the other guests pretend not to look. The chair tells her what you would not. Do not let the chair speak for you.

## Today's Practice

Open the Oracle. Find the invitation you have not answered. There is always one.

- Answer it now, yes or no. Not maybe. Maybe is not an answer; it is a chair.
- From this day, keep the Rule of the Single Day: every invitation is answered within one day of its arrival. If you truly cannot know within a day, say so, say when you will know, and then know by then.
- If an answer you have given changes, send the change in the hour it happens. The host would rather have the news than the chicken.

Then write one line in the Ledger: *Answered. Released a chair.*
