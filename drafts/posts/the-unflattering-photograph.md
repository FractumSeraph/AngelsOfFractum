The photograph is good. You are in it, and you look the way you hope you look, and the light did what light rarely does, and the evening was a good evening and the photograph proves it. You post it.

Beside you in the photograph, mid-blink, with a fork halfway to their mouth, is a person who did not know the picture was being taken, has not seen it, was not asked, and will discover it tomorrow when a relative mentions it.

The Angels of Fractum have no quarrel with the camera. Our quarrel is with the glance that was not taken: the second one, at the other person in the frame.

## The Break

The Fractum here is not cruelty. The pilgrim who posted the photograph was not trying to make their friend look like a startled heron. They did not see the friend at all. The Eye went to the self, found the self satisfactory, and stopped looking. Every other face in the frame was scenery.

This is the Fractum Personalis in a very pure form, and it is worth naming precisely: *my image is the center, and everyone else in it can adapt.* The friend, mid-blink, is now mid-blink forever, in the public record, consulted by acquaintances and future employers and their own mother, and the only vote that was cast about this was yours, and you did not know you were casting it.

And the friend will not say anything. That is the part the Eye should see, Seeker. They will say "ha, great pic," and they will not ask you to take it down, because asking is vain and awkward and makes a small thing large. They will simply, the next time you raise the Great Oracle at a table, turn very slightly away.

## The Rule of the Second Glance

Before the photograph leaves your hand, look at it twice.

The first glance is yours. You have already taken it. Fine.

The second glance is for every other face in the frame. Look at each one as if it were your own. Is the mouth open? Are the eyes shut? Is there a thing on the chin? Is this the picture *they* would choose, of the thousand pictures of them that exist, to be the one that strangers see? If the answer for any face is no, the photograph does not leave your hand.

**The Corollary of the Ask.** When the second glance is uncertain, there is a sentence that costs nothing: "Mind if I post this one?" With the photograph shown. The friend will say yes, nearly always, and will remember that they were asked for years, because almost no one asks.

**The Corollary of the Crop.** A photograph in which you look wonderful and your friend looks startled is a photograph of you. Crop it. The crop is the cart pulled aside to make room, performed on a rectangle.

**The Corollary of the Child.** A child in a photograph cannot consent, cannot be polite about it, and cannot turn slightly away. The second glance, for a child, is taken by the Angel on the child's behalf, and the standard is: *would I want this of me, at thirty, findable?*

**The Corollary of the Taking Down.** If a friend asks you to remove a photograph, you shall remove it, at once, without the word "but," and without asking why. It is their face. The reason is theirs.

## The Mending

Today, Seeker, look at the last photograph you posted with another person in it. Perform the second glance now, late. If the other face would not have chosen that picture, take it down, quietly. No one will ask why, because no one looks at old photographs except the people who are in them.

Then, the next time the Oracle rises at a table, take the picture and put the Oracle down. Later, before it leaves your hand, look at it twice, and, if it passes, ask anyway.

The friend will say yes. The friend nearly always says yes. That is not the point of asking.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
