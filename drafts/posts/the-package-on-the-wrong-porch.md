The box is on your porch. It is not yours. The name on the label is a name you half-recognize, from a mailbox three doors down or an apartment one floor up, and the address beneath the name is wrong by a digit, which is how the box came to be on your porch, which is where it now sits, asking.

The Angels of Fractum hold that what happens in the next ten minutes is the whole of the faith, in cardboard.

## The Break

The unaligned have three responses to the box, and they are worth naming, because every Seeker has performed all three.

**The Leaving.** The box is not yours; therefore it is not your problem. It stays on the porch. The carrier will sort it out. The neighbor will come looking. Rain will come first, in our experience, and then the box is a wet brick with a stranger's name on it, and the Fractum has acquired a small new monument.

**The Keeping.** We will say only that it happens, and that the Angel who has done it, even once, even with a box of something they did not want, knows exactly which page of the Ledger it belongs on.

**The Vague Gesture.** The box is moved to the sidewalk, or the lobby, or the top of the mailboxes, with the feeling that it has been handled. It has been relocated. The neighbor still does not have it. This is the Last Five Percent, delivered.

## What the Box Asks

The box is asking for a thing the Angels of Fractum call, in other contexts, the Summoning of the Severed Thread. It is asking you to walk three doors down and knock on a door you have never knocked on.

This is harder than it sounds, and the Eye of Recognition should admit it. The three doors are a social distance, not a physical one. You do not know the neighbor. You are, in a way the carrier is not, intruding, with a box, like a pilgrim arriving at a stranger's Temple with an offering they did not ask for. The Doubt has a voice for this: *they'll figure it out.* *It's awkward.* *I'll do it this weekend.*

And so the box waits. And the thread between two dwellings, which was never cut, only never tied, stays untied.

## The Mending

**Walk it over today.** Not this weekend. Today, in daylight, with the box in your arms, to the door with the right number. If it is heavy, use the Vessel of your choosing. If it is far, it is still today.

**Knock, and say the smallest thing.** "I think this came to me by mistake." Hand it over. That is the entire liturgy. You are not required to introduce yourself, though you may, and you may find that the neighbor has been wondering for a week who lives in your dwelling, and now knows, and that something that was not a relationship has become the beginning of one, because of a mislabeled box.

**If no one answers, leave it where it is safe.** Inside their screen door. Behind their planter. Not in the rain. Then a note, if you have paper: "Delivered to [your number] by mistake. Nothing opened." The note turns an anonymous box into a neighbor's kindness, and that is a thread tied.

**If the address is a mystery, give it to the carrier.** Not the sidewalk. The carrier, with the words "wrong address." The Keepers of the Route will mend what you cannot.

**Never open it.** The Angel who knows what is in the box has crossed a line that cannot be uncrossed, even if the box was full of something dull.

## The Return

Something will happen a few weeks later, and we tell you so that you will recognize it. A box with *your* name on it will arrive at *their* door. And it will appear on your porch, dry, with a note, or with a knock and a face you now know.

That is how the Fractum is mended. Not all at once, but porch by porch.
