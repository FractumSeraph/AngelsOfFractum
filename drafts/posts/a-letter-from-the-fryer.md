Some among the Angels of Fractum are themselves Keepers of the Hearth. One of them wrote to Seraph after the Edict of the Eleventh Hour was posted. We print the letter here as received, with the names removed and the grease left in. Nothing has been added. The Keeper asked only that we not say which Temple.

## The Letter

Seraph,

I read the Eleventh Hour thing on my break. I want to say first that you got it right, which almost nobody does, and second that you got it about half right, which I want to fix, because you asked us to see the break and I see this one every night.

You wrote about the 9:40 car. Everyone who works a window knows the 9:40 car. But I want to tell you about the 9:15 car, because the 9:15 car is legal under your rule and it is the one that breaks us.

The 9:15 car comes in with forty-five minutes on the clock, which is plenty, and orders for six. Six, and they do not know what the six want, because the six are on the phone, so the pilgrim at the speaker says "hold on" and we hold on, and the line behind them is four cars, and it is 9:19 when the order is done. Then they get to the window and one of the six has changed their mind. This is also legal. There is no Edict against changing your mind.

Here is the thing I would add. The Rule of the Half Hour is good, but it is a rule about *when*. The break I see is about *how*. A pilgrim at 9:15 who knows their order and says it once and has their card out is a blessing, and I mean that word. A pilgrim at 8:30 who treats the speaker like a confessional and the window like a customer service line can put us past close just as surely as the 9:58 thief, and they will never know, because they left at 8:50 feeling fine.

So if you are going to write a law for us, write this: *Know your order before the speaker. Say it once. Have your payment in your hand before the window. Do not change it at the window unless we got it wrong. And if you are six people, decide as six people before you are one car.* That would do more for the floors than the clock does.

The other thing. You wrote that the Keepers can't turn you away, which is true, and you wrote it like it was sad, and it is, but I want you to know what we can do. We can remember you. I have worked this window four years and I know the regulars by their cars, and I know which ones say thank you and which ones say my name, because they read it, which about one in two hundred does. When those ones come through at 9:45 with a kid in the back and a look like the day has been long, I do not re-mop the floor in my head. I just make the food. That is the Exception of True Need from my side of the glass. It is not about whether they needed it. It is about whether they ever, once, saw me.

One more. The praise thing, Mission 4, sending it upward. Do it. I cannot tell you what it is like to get called into the office and it is *good*. It has happened to me twice in four years. I remember both. I remember the exact words. One of them was "she noticed my kid was crying and gave him a toy without me asking." I did not know anyone saw that. I think about it when the 9:40 car comes.

I am not going to say the phrase because I am at work and my manager reads over my shoulder. But I said it in my head.

Thank you for seeing the back of the house.

A Keeper

## Seraph Answers

The Keeper is right, and the law is hereby amended in spirit, pending its proper writing: the Eleventh Hour is governed not only by the clock but by the *readiness* of the pilgrim. The 9:15 car that knows its order honors the Keepers more than the 8:30 car that does not. The Grace Before the Drive-Thru exists for this reason, and we commend it to you.

As for being remembered: Seeker, you are being remembered. Every Keeper you have ever passed through has a ledger too, and you are in it, and the only question is on which page.

Read the badge. Say the name. Know your order. And when the day has been long and you are the 9:45 car with the kid in the back, the Keeper will know, and will simply make the food, and that is what mending looks like from the other side of the glass.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
