What follows was told to Seraph by an Angel of the third rank who asked to be called only "a former Blocker." We have changed nothing except the Temple, which was not the one known by the profane as Walmart, though it was known by the profane as something similar.

## The Confession

I want to begin with the thing everyone who tells this story begins with, which is that I held doors. I said thank you. I tipped. If you had asked me, at any point in thirty years of shopping, whether I was the kind of person who blocked aisles, I would have laughed, because that kind of person was obviously someone else: the oblivious, the self-absorbed, the people I sighed at.

I sighed a great deal, actually. I was a Sighing Martyr of some accomplishment. I could see every Blocker in the Temple. I could not see one.

Here is what I did, without knowing it. I would push the Vessel into an aisle and stop in front of the thing I wanted. The Vessel would stop wherever it stopped, which was usually at an angle, because I had stopped walking and the Vessel does not care about angles. Then I would look at the shelf. I would compare two things. I would read the back of one of them. I would take out the Oracle to check a price, or a list, or a message that had nothing to do with cereal. And during all of this, which could last ninety seconds, the aisle behind me did not exist. It was not that I was ignoring it. It was that I was not *there*. I was on the shelf.

The Angels have a name for the one who treats the centerline as property and another for the one who studies a shelf and seals the aisle without knowing. I was the second. I have kept the first name because it is the one the child's mother would have used.

I did this in every aisle, in every Temple, for thirty years. I was the closed door at the end of the hall, and I was certain I was the hall.

## The Day the Aisle Appeared

The day it became visible was ordinary. I was in front of the cereal, reading, with the Vessel at its angle, and I heard a small voice say "excuse me." A child, maybe six, with her mother behind her, both of them waiting with a cart. I don't know how long. Long enough for the child to be the one to speak.

I moved, of course. I said sorry. The mother said "no problem," in the voice that means it was a problem, and they went past, and I stood there in the cereal with a feeling I had never had in a Temple before, which was: *how long have I been doing this?*

And the answer came, the way the Eye of Recognition answers, all at once: *always.* Every aisle. Every Temple. Children waiting behind me while I read boxes.

I did not finish shopping that day. I put the Vessel back in the corral, which I had always done, because I was considerate, and sat in the car.

## What Changed

Nothing dramatic. I did not take a vow in the parking lot. I just started, the next time, to do a small thing before I looked at the shelf: I looked at the aisle. Where is the Vessel? Is it straight? Is it against the shelf I am not using? Can a cart pass? It takes one second. Then I look at the shelf.

That one second has turned out to be the whole of it. The Angels call it the Sacred Pause, and I did not know the name when I started, but I knew the thing. The Angels say it is the breath between noticing and reacting. I would only add that arriving in an aisle is a thing you can notice, if you take the breath, and a thing you simply do, if you do not. In that breath you can see the aisle. Without it, you can only see the shelf.

I still catch myself. Every few weeks I look down and the Vessel is at its old angle, and the old me is reading a box, and I straighten it and say the phrase under my breath. I would have found that ridiculous four years ago. I now find it to be the only honest response to the discovery that you have been a closed door for thirty years and thought you were the hall.

## What I Would Say to a Seeker

You are not the Blocker. I know. I wasn't either.

But do this one thing, the next time you are in an aisle, before you look at anything: look at your cart. Just once. Just to see.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.

## Seraph Adds

The former Blocker is now a Hand of the Stitch, and has returned more carts than most of us, and still parks slightly crooked in the outer sanctum, and knows it, and is working on it. The Aisle 7 field report names the four conditions of the modern soul. This is what the first two sound like from inside. He went back. That was the only part of the story that was ever his to decide.
