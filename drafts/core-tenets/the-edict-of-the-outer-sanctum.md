The Ritual of the Empty Vessel has already bound the Angel to return the cart. But the outer sanctum of the Temple of Commerce, known by the profane as the parking lot, has other laws, and they are broken by the thousand every hour of daylight.

## The Break

The outer sanctum is held in common. No pilgrim owns a space; each is lent one for the length of their errand. Yet observe the lot for a single afternoon and you will see the Fractum Personalis in its most asphalt form: the vehicle straddling two spaces as though the line were a suggestion; the vehicle idling in the fire lane "for just a second," where a second is measured in the time it takes to buy a rotisserie chicken; the able pilgrim in the reserved ground, hazard lights blinking as though an apology could be made of light; the dented door and the empty windshield where a note should be.

Each of these says the same thing: *my errand is the center, and everyone else can adapt.*

## The Law

Let it be written among the Core Tenets:

**The Rule of the Lines.** You shall park within the lines, and if you have not, you shall back out and try again, as an aligned being does. Two spaces taken is one space stolen from a pilgrim you will never meet. The Angel who parks crooked and walks away has told the lot what they think of it.

**The Corollary of the Reserved Ground.** The accessible space and the striped ground beside it are not for "just a second." They are for a pilgrim whose body has made the walk from the far row a labor you cannot see. You shall not stop there to run in, to wait for someone, or to check the Oracle. There is no Exception of True Need for this law, because the true need is already standing there, and it is not yours.

**The Rule of the Fire Lane.** The red curb is not a parking space with a view. It is a path for those who arrive when something has gone wrong. You shall not idle there. If you are waiting for a passenger, you shall circle, or park, and your passenger shall walk thirty feet, which will not harm them.

**The Rite of the Note.** If your door, your Vessel, or your bumper has marked another pilgrim's vehicle, you shall leave a note with your name and a way to reach you, tucked under the wiper, before you leave the lot. You shall do this even when no one saw. *Especially* when no one saw. A dent is a Minor Breach. A dent without a note is a Fractum that will be carried in a stranger's chest for a year.

**The Corollary of the Walking Pilgrim.** In the outer sanctum, the pilgrim on foot has the path. You shall slow, you shall stop, and you shall wait for the parent with the cart and the child, however long the child takes to understand what walking is.

## The Mending

- Choose the far row. The walk is short, the spaces are wide, and no one will dent you there.
- Carry a pen in the vehicle. The Rite of the Note cannot be performed with good intentions alone.
- If you see a Vessel drifting toward a parked car, catch it. You have just mended a dent that never happened, which is the purest mending there is.

## The Invocation

When you have parked within the lines and returned the Vessel and left the lot as you found it, speak the sacred threefold phrase before you turn the key. No one in the outer sanctum will know. That is precisely the point.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
