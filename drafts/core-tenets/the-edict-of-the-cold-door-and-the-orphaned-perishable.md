In the Temple of Commerce there is a cold chain, and it is holier than the pilgrims know. It runs from the field to the truck to the case to the Vessel to the kitchen, and it is broken by the thousand every day, in two ways, by people who mean no harm.

## The Break

The first break is the open door. A pilgrim opens the freezer case, and then stands in its light, reading the back of a box while the frost of forty years of engineering rolls out across their shoes. The door is held open, Seeker, to consider. The door is held open to call a spouse. The door is held open while the pilgrim decides between two things that are the same thing. Behind the glass, a hundred dinners begin, very slightly, to thaw.

The second break is the Orphaned Perishable. A package of chicken, chosen and then reconsidered, set down on the nearest dry shelf among the cereal. A pint of ice cream, abandoned on a rack of batteries at the register. A gallon of milk among the greeting cards, sweating onto the sympathy section. The Keepers have a name for these: they call them go-backs, and at close they walk the aisles collecting them, and most of them go in the bin, because no one can know how long the chicken was among the cereal.

## The Law

**The Rule of the Closed Door.** You shall decide with the door closed. Look through the glass. Choose. Open, take, close. If you must read the box, take it out and close the door behind you, and if you reject it, open the door again and return it. The door is a window only while it is shut.

**The Rule of the Return to the Cold.** If you change your mind about a cold thing, you shall carry it back to the cold. Not to the nearest shelf. Not to the register, where it will be found an hour later sweating on the candy. To the case it came from, or to the nearest case of its kind.

**The Exception of the Keeper's Hand.** If you cannot find the case, or you are already at the register and the Vessel is unloaded, you shall hand the cold thing to a Keeper and say, "I've changed my mind about this. I'm sorry." The Keeper will take it back to the cold, and will think of you, briefly, as one of the good ones, which you will have been.

**The Corollary of the Dry Orphan.** A dry thing abandoned on a wrong shelf is a Minor Breach, and the Angel who passes it may mend it: carry it home, if you know where home is. If you do not, leave it, for a box of crackers among the soap harms no one, and the Keeper will find it. Only the cold cannot wait.

## The Mending

- Decide before you open. The door is the last step, not the first.
- Take the cold things last, so they spend the fewest minutes in the Vessel and the door is opened once.
- If you see an Orphaned Perishable, do not return it to the case. You cannot know how long it has been among the cereal, and neither can the pilgrim who would buy it after you. Carry it to a Keeper, or leave it where the Keeper will find it. The chicken is past saving, and you are not its priest.

## The Invocation

When the door seals with its small sucking sound and the cold is kept, speak the phrase. The dinner you did not thaw belongs to a pilgrim you will never meet. Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
