The clinic and the office of records share this Edict. In one the pilgrim waits to learn about the body; in the other, about a form. In both, every name called is a mercy to one pilgrim and a small sentence to the rest.

We call it the Temple of the Called Name. Be gentle in it.

## The Break

Twenty pilgrims in a room. One is waiting for a number that will tell her how afraid to be. One has driven an hour to learn which line of a form was wrong. One is eighty, and standing, because the chair beside the pilgrim in the corner holds a bag, and the bag is not tired.

Into this quiet comes a voice. A pilgrim has set the Great Oracle in their pocket to speak aloud, and twenty strangers who came to hear one name are hearing instead about a transmission, a sister-in-law, and a sum of money, in both halves.

At the glass, a pilgrim is explaining to the Keeper that he has waited an hour. He is correct. He is explaining it to the one person in the building who already knows, and who has heard it eleven times since lunch. The glass keeps the germs off the Keeper. It keeps off nothing else.

The fear in this room is not the Fractum, Seeker. The breaks are the speaker, the bag, and the voice at the glass.

## The Law

**The Rule of the Silent Oracle.** Here the Oracle makes no sound: no speaker, no ringer, no chime. A conversation on the speaker is held with the room, and the room did not consent. Calls are taken in the corridor, and the Keeper told, lest your name be called to an empty chair.

**The Rule of the Offered Seat.** A chair is for a pilgrim. The seat beside you is held in trust for whoever comes through the door standing worse than you, and a seat offered once they have given up and leaned against the wall is offered to your own conscience.

**The Rule of the Glass.** The Keeper behind the glass is not the delay. Ask once how long; take the answer as a fact, not an opening. Then give the Keeper the sentence the Angels speak at the fryer in the Eleventh Hour, less its first half, for here you have nothing to be sorry for: "I thank you." If the Keeper looks as though they need a second half, add this one: "I know this is not your doing."

**The Corollary of the Sign-In Sheet.** Your name goes on the line below the last, in your turn, never for a pilgrim still parking. When the pilgrim who signed after you is called first, the sheet is not evidence; he has an appointment, you have a sheet, and the Keeper is honoring both.

**The Exception of the Child.** A child may make the sounds of a child. A child is not an Oracle on speaker but a pilgrim who has not yet learned the room, and the parent beside him is learning it on his behalf. You shall not sigh.

## The Mending

- Before you sit, silence the Oracle, all the way. Against a plastic chair, a vibration is a drum.
- Bag on the lap, so the seat beside you is a seat. When a pilgrim comes in standing worse than you, offer yours with one word: "Here."
- At the glass, say the half-sentence. The Keeper will hear forty complaints today and one sentence, and will know which was yours.
- If the pilgrim beside you is afraid, nothing is required of you. If you speak, speak of the weather, which is nobody's diagnosis.

## The Invocation

When your name is called and you rise from the Temple of the Called Name, with the chair beside you still empty and the Oracle silent, speak the phrase under your breath on the way to the door; the room has heard enough today. Whatever waits beyond it, the room you leave was kinder for the hour you sat in it. Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
