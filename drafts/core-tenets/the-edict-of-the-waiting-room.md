There is a Temple in which nothing is sold. Its pews are vinyl, its hymnal is a magazine from a season that has ended, and every pilgrim is waiting to be called by name. Its Keepers sit behind glass, which is to say they are visible and cannot leave.

## The Break

Twenty pilgrims in a room. The one by the window is waiting for a word about her blood. The one by the door is waiting to learn that a form he filled out in April was filled out wrong. The one with the cane stands against the wall, reading the poster about hand-washing a third time, because the chair beside the pilgrim in the corner holds a bag, and the bag has an appointment.

Into this quiet comes a voice from an Oracle set to speak aloud, held at chin height, so that twenty frightened strangers now also know how the cousin is doing, in both directions, at a volume calibrated for a kitchen.

At the glass, a pilgrim is explaining to the Keeper that he has waited forty minutes, which the Keeper knows. The Keeper did not book the appointment, did not decide that the pilgrim ahead would need twenty minutes instead of ten. Behind the glass sits the one person who can do nothing about the wait and must hear about it from everyone.

The Fractum here is not the fear. It is the speaker, the bag, and the voice at the glass.

## The Law

**The Rule of the Silent Oracle.** In the waiting room the Oracle speaks only into the ear: no speaker, no video with the sound on, no ringer. If a call must be taken, take it in the hall, and tell the Keeper so, that your name is not called to an empty chair.

**The Rule of the Offered Seat.** A chair holds a pilgrim, not a bag. When the room fills, the bag goes to your lap. When a pilgrim enters who stands worse than you, with the cane, the belly, the sling, the years, you rise and say "Here." Pity looks at the cane. Empathy looks at the chair.

**The Rule of the Glass.** The Keeper behind the glass is not the delay. You may ask once, kindly, how long, and you shall take the answer as you take weather. Then speak the sentence the Angels speak at the fryer, worn to fit the glass: "Thank you. I know it isn't you."

**The Corollary of the Sign-In Sheet.** Your name goes on the line below the last, in your turn, in your hand. You shall not sign in a pilgrim still in the parking lot, nor argue the sheet with the Keeper. It is a covenant in pen on a clipboard, and holier than most things in pen.

**The Exception of the Child.** A child may make the sounds of a child, and the parent managing one is performing a labor you shall not sigh at. A cartoon at a whisper is a smaller Fractum than a scream, and the parent knows the arithmetic better than you.

## The Mending

- Before you sit, silence the Oracle. It takes one second.
- Bag on the lap. Keep the chair beside you a chair, for whoever needs it more.
- At the glass, say the sentence. The Keeper will have heard forty complaints that day and one sentence, and will remember which.
- If the pilgrim beside you is afraid, you need not speak. Silence kept well is also a Mending.

## The Invocation

When the name called is not yours, and the Oracle has sat silent through an hour you did not want, speak the phrase without sound, which is the only way this Temple permits. Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
