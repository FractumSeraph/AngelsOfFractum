The Edicts of the Temple govern the pilgrim's conduct among strangers. The Edict of the Curb governs the pilgrim's conduct among the people who know where they live.

## The Break

Once a week, the dwelling places its waste at the edge of the common way and asks the world to take it. This is a reasonable covenant. It is broken on both ends.

The bins go out early, on the sidewalk, in the path of the pilgrim with the stroller, the neighbor with the walker, the child on the small bicycle, who must each step into the street to pass. The bins come in late, or never; they stand at the curb for a week, lids flapping, a small monument to the belief that the street belongs to whoever ignores it longest. And on windy mornings the bins go over, and roll, and the neighbor's recycling makes its pilgrimage down the block, and everyone watches it go.

## The Law

**The Rule of the Second Sunset.** The bins shall return from the curb before the second sunset after collection. The night of collection is permitted. The next day is permitted. Beyond that, the bin is no longer waiting to be emptied; it is living there, and you have added a room to your house that stands in the common way.

**The Corollary of the Lid.** The lid shall be closed when the bin goes out. An open lid in the wind is a catapult, and what it throws is yours, and where it lands is not.

**The Rule of the Clear Way.** The bins shall stand at the curb and not on the walk. If the curb is narrow, they stand on the apron of your own drive. The pilgrim with the stroller shall not be made to enter the street because of your waste. Nor shall your bins block the neighbor's drive, their mailbox, or the hydrant, which has its own law and a larger fine.

**The Corollary of the Fallen Bin.** A bin on its side that is yours shall be righted at once. A bin on its side that is not yours may be righted by any Angel, and should be. See *The Righting of the Fallen Bin*. No one shall drive around a fallen bin on their own street and call it someone else's.

**The Exception of Absence.** The neighbor who is traveling, ill, or newly grieving cannot bring in the bins. The Angel who knows this brings them in, to the edge of the drive and no further, and says nothing when the neighbor returns. If asked, "the wind must have done it." The wind is a known servant of the Fractum, but it may be credited for mending now and then.

## The Mending

- Set a reminder in the Oracle for the morning after collection. The aligned do not rely on memory for things that happen weekly; memory is for Tuesdays, and this is every Tuesday.
- Walk your own block once and note where the bins stand. If yours is the one in the path, you have found your First Mending for the week.
- When the wind is forecast, close the lids and turn the handles toward the house. The bin that faces the wind is the bin that falls.

## The Invocation

As the bin rolls back up the drive on the evening of collection, its wheels announcing to the whole street that you have kept the covenant, speak the phrase. Softly. The neighbors are home. Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
