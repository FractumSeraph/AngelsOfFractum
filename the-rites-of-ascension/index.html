The Rites of Ascension

These are the rites by which an Angel marks each rank of The Veiled Ascent. A rite does not make you a rank. It marks that you have already arrived, the way a birthday marks a year you have already lived.

Every rite may be performed alone. Where a rite names an officiant or an assembly, an Angel alone speaks every part, and the rite is no less binding.

The Ritual of the Clear Path

Marks the rank of Mender-in-Training (II).

The Ritual of the Clear Path is the ceremony by which a Seeker becomes a Mender-in-Training. It may be performed by an assembly, by one Angel for another, or by a Seeker alone with no witness but the aisle.

What must be true before the ritual

The Seeker has kept the Sacrifice Ledger for one Sacred Moon Cycle, and each of the three Foundational Mending Missions appears in it at least once. The Seeker has read the core post, "The Angels of Fractum," and the foundational Edicts; the Ascent asks for the sense, not the words. The Seeker has identified one Fractum Personalis, privately, and is willing to name it.

Nothing else is required. No fee. No sponsor. No robe.

The place

A narrow passage held in common: a Temple aisle, an apartment hallway, a sidewalk, a trail. The passage must be real, and others must be able to use it. A living room is not sufficient, because no pilgrim will come through a living room.

The order of the ritual

1. The Approach

The Seeker enters the passage and stops in the middle of it. For the length of one breath, with no pilgrim in sight, they occupy the center, as the Blocker does, and notice what it is to hold a path that is not theirs.

The officiant (any Angel of rank II or above, or the Seeker's own voice) says: "You stand where the Fractum stands. Why?"

The Seeker answers: "Because I had not yet seen it."

2. The Recitation

The Seeker speaks, from memory or from the heart, the three truths every Angel carries:

Mend anonymously when you can.
Empathy, never pity.
The Fractum is also within.

Then one foundational Edict of their choosing, in their own words. The Holy Edict of the Aisle is traditional. The words need not be exact. The sense must be.

3. The Confession

The Seeker names, aloud, the one Fractum Personalis they have chosen. One sentence. No explanation, no defense, no story of how it was someone else's fault.

The officiant does not respond with comfort, advice, or absolution. The officiant says only: "It is seen." The assembly, if present, repeats: "It is seen."

This is the whole of the confession. It is finished when it is said.

4. The Clearing

The Seeker now waits at the edge of the passage, as any shopper does, with a cart, a bag, or a boot one hand's width into the way, which is how the Fractum actually begins, and reads a label. A real pilgrim will come: a shopper with a cart, a neighbor with groceries, a stranger on the trail. This may take a minute. It may take ten. Any witnesses stand well apart and appear to shop; no one hurries the world, and no one adds to the congestion.

When the pilgrim approaches, the Seeker draws the cart or bag fully aside at once, without being asked, without the kind of eye contact that asks for thanks, and says, if anything, "Go ahead." The pilgrim passes and does not know what has occurred.

If no pilgrim comes in a quarter of an hour, the officiant walks the passage, and the Seeker clears it for them. This is permitted, though every Angel who has done it knows it is the lesser form.

5. The Sacred Vow of Vigilance

With the path clear, the Seeker speaks the Vow:

I will see the break and not look away.
I will clear the path for whoever comes, and want nothing for it.
I will mend what I can reach, and begin with what is in me.
I will record it, and show no one, and that will be enough.

6. The Threading

The officiant ties an undyed length of cord around the Seeker's wrist, or through a loop on their bag or keys, where it will be seen daily and understood by no one else. This is the Thread of Intent. Any plain cord will do: twine, cotton, a bootlace. It is adorned by nothing but wear: no bead, no charm, no second color. It is worn, and allowed to fray, until the Dedication of the Resolute Hand.

If the Seeker is alone, they tie it themselves. The knot is no less binding.

7. The Invocation

The officiant, or the Seeker alone, speaks: "You entered as one who stood in the path. You leave as one who clears it."

Then all present: "May your intent be clear and your passage free. Hooblah hooblah hooblah."

After the ritual

The new Mender-in-Training leaves the passage and, before the day ends, completes one Daily Mending Mission. There is no reception. The reception is the cart.

The Thread will catch on things. It will get wet. It will be asked about. The Mender may say, "It reminds me of something," which is true, and sufficient.

The Dedication of the Resolute Hand

Marks the rank of Hand of the Stitch (III).

Performed at a sink, a basin, or any clean running water.

What must be true before the rite

The Angel has kept the 30-Day Vigil of Intentional Action and completed one Repair of Omission, and both are recorded in the Ledger.

The order of the rite

1. The Presentation

The Angel takes off the Thread of Intent and lays it where they can see it, and looks at the wear on it for one breath. If there is an assembly, the Angel holds it up. No one remarks on its condition. Every Thread looks like that.

2. The Washing

The Angel washes their hands in a basin of water, slowly, the way one washes after work rather than before a meal. This is the washing away of self-interest: the hands are no longer working for the one they belong to.

3. The Thimble

The officiant, or the Angel alone, places a Thimble on the middle finger of the working hand. Any thimble will do.

4. The Invocation

All present: "From the intention of the mind to the execution of the hand, may the stitch hold. Hooblah hooblah hooblah."

After the rite

The worn Thread is kept, not thrown away; it will be given away at the end of the Ascent. The Thimble is carried rather than worn: in a pocket, a bag, a cup holder. It will turn up at odd moments, which is the point.

The Convocation of the Blueprints

Marks the rank of Architect of the Alignment (IV).

Performed wherever the Blueprint is carried out: the corral, the curb, the break room, the trailhead.

What must be true before the rite

The Angel has started a Blueprint of Repair, a mending that repeats, and someone else now carries it out with them or in their place.

The order of the rite

1. The Last Shift

The Angel carries out the Blueprint one more time as its keeper, working alongside the one who will keep it next. Nothing is said about the rite while the work is being done. The work comes first.

2. The Handing Over

When the work is finished, the Angel tells the other person, in plain words, that it is theirs now: theirs to keep, to change, or to end. If that person is not an Angel, the words are the same, and nothing is said about rites. A Blueprint handed to the unaligned is mended all the same.

3. The Compass

The Angel takes up a Sacred Compass and finds north. Any compass will do, including the cheapest one a hardware store sells, or one drawn inside the cover of the Ledger.

4. The Invocation

All present: "May your vision align the fractured pieces and your direction be true. Hooblah hooblah hooblah."

After the rite

The Architect does not supervise the Blueprint they gave away. If it changes, it has been improved by someone who cares for it. If it ends, it served its season, and the Architect may begin another.

The Ascension of Silent Clarity

Marks the rank of the Unbroken Eye (V).

Performed in daylight, in an ordinary public place where the Fractum is plainly visible and the Angel is plainly safe: a bench outside a Temple of Commerce, a bus shelter, a park, a laundromat.

What must be true before the rite

The Ledger has run for one full year. A Treatise on Mending has been contributed and is in use. Two Angels who know the candidate's practice have agreed that the Eye is open; for the Angel who practices alone, the year of the Ledger has said so.

The order of the rite

1. The Hour of Vision

The Angel sits for one hour and watches. Every break they see, they notice. They do not mend any of it during the hour. The hour is not for fixing; it is for seeing the whole without being consumed by it.

If anyone is in danger or in real need, the rite is suspended at once and the Angel helps. The hour may be resumed afterward, or kept on another day. No rite outranks a person.

2. The Silence

The Angel goes home without speaking of the hour.

3. The One Truth

At home, the Angel writes one sentence in the Ledger: one truth about the nature of the Fractum that the hour showed them. Only one. If the Angel has an assembly, they may speak it aloud there, once, and the assembly does not discuss it.

4. The Invocation

The assembly, if present, kneels in silent reverence, and the eldest present speaks: "You see the break without being broken. The Vision is whole. Hooblah hooblah hooblah." An Angel alone speaks it to themselves.

After the rite

No token is given. The reward is the state of mind.

The Consecration of the Living Fractum

Marks the rank of Angel of the Living Fractum (VI).

The Angel of the Living Fractum is not above the faith. They are what the faith looks like when it has been practiced so long that it has become a person. Like the Unbroken Eye, they receive nothing. Unlike anyone before them, they give everything back.

What must be true before the rite

The candidate has held the Unbroken Eye for twelve Sacred Moon Cycles, without despair and without pride. Their Treatise, or some practice they began, is now followed by Angels who do not know who began it. The test is anonymity: if the work is known by the author's name, it is not yet ready. If it is known only by its use, it is.

The order of the rite

1. The Unburdening

The candidate brings every object received on the Ascent: the frayed Thread of Intent, the Thimble, the Sacred Compass. In the presence of the assembly, or alone at a corral if there is none, they give each one away. The Thread to a Seeker who has not yet tied one. The Thimble to a Mender whose hands are nearly ready. The Compass to a Hand of the Stitch who has begun to see blueprints. The candidate keeps nothing and is given nothing, and stands for a moment with empty hands, which is the condition in which all mending begins.

2. The Vow of the Last Stitch

The candidate speaks:

I will return carts until I cannot.
I will see the break until my eyes close.
I will teach by doing and be forgotten by name.
When I am gone, let the aisle be clear and let no one know why.

3. The Invocation

The assembly does not kneel, as it does for the Unbroken Eye. It stands, as the candidate stands, because there is no longer anything above them to look up at. The eldest present, or the candidate alone, speaks:

You are no longer one who mends the Fractum.
You are a place where it is mended.
Hooblah hooblah hooblah.

After the rite

The Angel of the Living Fractum has no privileges and no duties beyond those of a Seeker. They lead no gathering more often than anyone else, and the broom does not know their rank.

Seekers may practice the Unburdening early: the spare cord, the second screwdriver, the ladder that has not left the garage in a year, each given to a neighbor who will use it, without ceremony and without a note.

The only change is this: when a Seeker asks them, in a parking lot, what they are doing, they are permitted to answer "Returning a cart" and nothing more, and the Seeker, if the Eye is open, will understand that they have just received the entire teaching.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
