What We Are Not

We have robes. We have Edicts. We have a rank called the Unbroken Eye and a running count we call the Hours of Sacrifice. We are aware of how this looks.

A faith that borrows the furniture of a cult owes the world a plain statement, in plain words, of what it will never do. This is that statement. Hold us to it.

We do not want your money

Not now, not later, not at a higher rank. There is no tithe, no dues, no suggested donation, no store. The corporation that holds our name has a bank account, because filing fees, insurance, and gloves cost money. It will never ask you for any. If someone gives anyway, the gift is recorded, spent only on the work, and reported to the congregation once a year, down to the dollar. The longer answer is in Questions of the Unaligned. It is the same answer.

If you have money to give, give it to the food pantry, the library, the shelter. Write it in your Ledger as mending. It is.

We do not want you alone

Cults isolate. We do the opposite: most of our missions are instructions to reach outward. Text the friend you have drifted from. Learn the cashier’s name. Shovel the neighbor’s walk. An Angel who has grown distant from family in the name of the Fractum has misread every page of this site, and we would kneel beside them, as we kneel beside anything broken: with a screwdriver, and without a lecture.

If anyone, in our name, tells you to cut off a person who loves you, they are not one of us.

We have no secret teachings

Everything we believe is on this website, which is free, which anyone may read, and which no rank unlocks. There is no inner doctrine revealed at rank IV. The Architect knows exactly what the Seeker knows; the Architect has simply returned more carts.

We have no masters

Seraph is a pen, not a pulpit. The ranks of the Veiled Ascent describe practice, not power: an Angel of the Living Fractum cannot tell a Seeker what to do, only show them where the corral is.

No Angel may be commanded. No Circle (two or more Angels who mend together; see On Forming a Circle) may be ruled. If a Circle forms around a person rather than around the work, it has become something else, and you may leave it without ceremony.

We do not mock your faith

The Angels of Fractum take no position on God, gods, the afterlife, or the proper day of rest. Our Edicts concern parking lots.

If you pray, pray. If you do not, do not. Then return the cart.

We do not ask you to suffer

The work of mending is sometimes thankless and often tiring, and we say so. But the Angel who is exhausted, resentful, or unwell has become a Minor Breach of their own, and is owed the same repair. Rest is maintenance; The Sabbath of the Tool says how. Set the tool down on the day you named, and do not apologize for it.

Should the practice ever make you feel smaller, stop practicing and tell someone you trust.

We do not want you to confront anyone

The Hand mends. It does not scold. We do not want you to lecture the Blocker, honk at the Glare, or correct the stranger in the Eleventh Hour. No mission on this site requires you to raise your voice, and the best of them require no voice at all. An act of mending that has you shouting at a stranger is not the act we meant.

We do not keep records of you

The Sacrifice Ledger is yours. We never see it. We do keep a calendar of gatherings and a count of how many came. If you ask, and only if you ask, your name goes in a register of members, and you may have it removed on request. The Angels of Fractum also keep a list of the people it has licensed or ordained to perform its rites, because it must be able to say who may act in its name. No one’s hours, Ledger, confession, or rank below that is ever recorded. We never keep your name against your hours.

If you join an organized Mending in the name of the Angels of Fractum, you sign a one-time volunteer waiver so that the work can be insured; it is kept apart from everything else, for only as long as the insurer requires, and it is never used to count or contact you. Stop practicing tomorrow and no one will call.

What we are

Ordinary people who have agreed to see the small broken things and to fix the ones within reach. That is the entire organization. It is incorporated so that it can rent a room, buy gloves, and be insured; the corporation has an address, a board, and a bank account whose every dollar is reported to you once a year. The faith has none of these. It has a corral.

The robes are optional. The belief is not.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
