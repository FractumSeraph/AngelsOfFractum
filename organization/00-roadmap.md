- **The faith has said, in public, that it wants no money, keeps no roster, and has no masters.** ("We do not want your money." "We do not keep records of you." "Seraph is a pen, not a pulpit.") A corporation, by contrast, must have at least three directors, must list its officers and directors with the state every year, and will need a small treasury to pay filing fees and insurance. A website that describes itself as satire is the single largest risk to this application. It is addressed in the risk memo, and nothing in this roadmap should be filed until that memo has been acted on.

## Part A. The decision: church, or religious public charity?

Arkansas does not force this choice. Under Arkansas law the organization will be a "religious corporation" either way (Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-202(a)(2); see Step 5). The choice is federal, and it is made on IRS Form 1023, Schedule A, or by choosing not to file Form 1023 at all.

### A.1 What the two paths are

**Path 1: Church.** A church is a subset of "religious organization" under section 501(c)(3). Under IRC § 508(c)(1)(A), churches are automatically treated as exempt and are not required to apply for recognition; under IRC § 6033(a)(3)(A)(i), churches are not required to file the annual Form 990-series return. The IRS applies fourteen non-exclusive characteristics (listed in A.3) together with all the facts and circumstances, and courts apply the "associational test": a church must have, at minimum, "a body of believers or communicants that assembles regularly in order to worship" (*American Guidance Foundation, Inc. v. United States*, 490 F. Supp. 304 (D.D.C. 1980), adopted in *Foundation of Human Understanding v. United States*, 614 F.3d 1383 (Fed. Cir. 2010)). The Form 1023 instructions put it directly: an applicant "is generally required to have a congregation or other religious membership group."

**Path 2: Religious 501(c)(3) public charity (not a church).** A religious organization that is not a church gets the same income-tax exemption and the same deductibility of gifts, but it generally must apply for recognition within 27 months of formation to be exempt from its formation date, and it must file Form 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N every year. Failure to file for three consecutive years results in automatic revocation under IRC § 6033(j).

There is a third path the founder should at least see: **a secular charitable 501(c)(3)** (a community-repair charity with no religious claims). It would be the easiest to defend and would forfeit every religious protection. Because the founder's stated goal is a religious organization with religious protections, this roadmap does not plan for it, but the risk memo discusses when counsel might recommend it.

### A.4 The sincerity question

The IRS's two guidelines for religious organizations (Pub. 557) are (1) that the beliefs are "truly and sincerely held" and (2) that the practices are not illegal or contrary to clearly defined public policy. The second is easy here: returning carts, replacing smoke-detector batteries, and thanking cashiers violate no law. The first is the hard one.

Courts use *United States v. Seeger* (sincere belief that "occupies a place in the life of its possessor parallel to that filled by the orthodox belief in God") and the *Malnak/Africa* indicia: the belief system addresses "fundamental and ultimate questions," it is comprehensive rather than an isolated teaching, and it has "formal and external signs" (services, clergy, holidays, literature). The Angels of Fractum have the third in abundance, have a fair claim to the second (a complete ethics of attention, repair, humility, and rest), and must make a case for the first: that the sacredness of a mended thing, the brokenness within, and the duty to the unseen stranger are ultimate concerns and not merely etiquette.

The adverse precedent is *Cavanaugh v. Bartelt*, 178 F. Supp. 3d 819 (D. Neb. 2016), which held that Pastafarianism is "a parody," not a religion, because its gospel "is plainly a work of satire." The favorable data points (The Satanic Temple's reported church recognition in 2019; the First Church of Cannabis's 501(c)(3) in 2015) rest on secondary reporting and involved groups that submitted creeds, rituals, literature, and real congregational activity. Form 1023 asks for the organization's website, and the IRS has denied an application in part for inconsistency between the application and the website (Determination Letter 201823006, as transcribed by a third party). Assume the examiner will read fractum.faith.

**The founder's decision, in writing, before Step 1 is complete:** Is this a religion whose liturgical voice is theatrical, or a satire whose kindness is real? Only the first can be the basis of a church. The faith's own pages already draw that line ("The scaffolding is theatrical. The beam is load-bearing."). The legal task is to make the beam visible to a stranger reading quickly.

### A.5 Recommendation

Form the Arkansas religious corporation now (Steps 2 through 8), and operate it, from the first week, as a church would: a fixed place, a fixed schedule, a counted congregation, a written course of study, records. Do not file Form 1023 until the organization has at least six months of documented practice, and preferably a year. Then file Form 1023 with Schedule A requesting church classification. If, when the time comes, counsel judges the congregation too thin, file for religious-organization status instead and request church classification later on Form 8940 once the congregation exists. The Form 1023 instructions describe exactly this two-step path.

Everything in Part B assumes that recommendation.

**Note: the risk memo recommends the opposite sequence** (religious-organization status first, on the full Form 1023 without Schedule A, and church classification later on Form 8940). The two paths cost the same and use the same corporation; the difference is whether a possible denial of church status is risked now. Counsel decides at the June 2027 checkpoint (Step 10 and the C.1 timeline), and the risk memo should be conformed to whichever is chosen. The compliance calendar is written for this roadmap's sequence and marks, entry by entry, what changes under the risk memo's. Until the checkpoint, the two sequences ask for exactly the same work: form the corporation, fix the place and schedule, keep the records.

### Step 4. Recruit the initial board (November through December 2026)

**Who:** Founder recruits; the incorporator names them in the articles or the board is seated at the organizational meeting.
**Cost:** None. Directors of this organization should serve without compensation.
**Time:** Three to six weeks.
**Before this:** Step 1's decisions; Step 2's name, so you can tell recruits what they are joining.

**Minimum size and terms.** Arkansas requires a board of "three (3) or more individuals" (Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-803(a)), and the number may be changed "but to no fewer than three (3)" (§ 4-33-803(b)). Director terms may not exceed six years; if the articles and bylaws are silent, the term is one year (§ 4-33-805). Every corporation must have a board (§ 4-33-801). The SOS annual report form itself says "minimum of 3 persons."

**Why the founder should not be the whole board, or control it:**

- The IRS asks, on Form 1023 Part V, whether officers, directors, and trustees are related to each other by family or business relationships, and uses the answers to look for private benefit and inurement. The IRS's 2008 governance paper favors an active, engaged, appropriately sized board whose members are independent of one another. Form 990's test for an "independent voting member" (no compensation as officer or employee, under $10,000 as a contractor, no reportable transactions) is a good benchmark even for a church that will never file Form 990.
- Private inurement is the one absolute rule: "any amount of inurement is, potentially, grounds for loss of tax-exempt status" (Pub. 1828). A founder-run board with no one to question a founder's expense, salary, or use of property is where that rule is most often broken. The courts' examples of denied or revoked "churches" (*Church of the Chosen People*, the Universal Life Church revocations) are, at bottom, stories of a church operated for one person.
- The faith's own doctrine requires it. "No Angel may be commanded." "Anyone has been called the founder" is listed as a sign a Circle has gone wrong. A board that could outvote Seraph is not a concession to the IRS; it is the Edicts applied to the corporation.

**Recommended composition:** Five directors (three is the floor; five gives a quorum when someone is away). The templates in the articles, minutes, and Form 1023 narrative show three rows; add two if five are seated, so every document lists the same board. The founder may be one of them. The founder, as Keeper of the Record, may sit on the board but may not be President or Treasurer or a bank signer (bylaws §§ 7.3, 8.4). At least three should be unrelated to the founder and to each other by family or business. Nobody should be paid. Terms of one or two years, staggered, consistent with the faith's distrust of anyone who "has held the hour for three gatherings." Each recruit should receive the statement of faith, the draft bylaws, the conflict-of-interest policy, and a plain one-page description of a director's legal duties **[VERIFY: the Act's director standard-of-conduct section was not researched; counsel to supply the citation]**.

**Who to look for:** people who already practice (attend the gatherings in Step 3), who live in Arkansas (helpful for the registered agent and for in-person meetings, though the Act sets no residency requirement for directors that the research found **[VERIFY]**), and among whom at least one is comfortable keeping minutes and one keeping books.

**Which form.** The SOS offers two: Form NPD-01 (Articles of Incorporation, Domestic Nonprofit, Rev. 12/24) and NPD-01-501-c-3, which pre-prints IRS-style purpose, inurement, lobbying, political-activity, and dissolution clauses. **What the articles must contain** (Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-202(a); Form NPD-01 tracks it item by item):

1. The corporate name (Step 2).
2. The statement "this corporation is a religious corporation." Arkansas has no separate religious-corporation statute; a church incorporates under the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993 (Act 1147 of 1993, § 4-33-101 et seq.) and checks the "Religious Corporation" box. The payoff is § 4-33-180: "If religious doctrine governing the affairs of a religious corporation is inconsistent with the provisions of this chapter on the same subject, the religious doctrine shall control to the extent required by the Constitution of the United States or the constitution of this state or both." That is the main state-law "religious protection" available at the corporate level.
3. The registered agent (Step 5).
4. The name and address of each incorporator; each must sign (§ 4-33-201; § 4-33-202(c)(1)). One incorporator is enough.
5. Whether the corporation will have members. This is a real decision with governance consequences under the Act **[VERIFY the member-rights provisions before choosing]**. The draft bylaws in this packet should match whatever is chosen. A common pattern for a church that wants a counted congregation but no voting-member bureaucracy: the articles state the corporation has no members within the meaning of the Act, and the bylaws define "members of the congregation" as a religious category with no corporate voting rights. Counsel should confirm that this pattern works under Arkansas law and that it does not undercut the Schedule A membership showing.

### Step 8. Hold the organizational meeting (within 30 days after the articles are accepted)

**Who:** The initial board; the Secretary takes minutes.
**Cost:** None.
**Time:** One meeting of two to three hours, with documents circulated a week ahead.
**Before this:** Steps 6 and 7; counsel's review of the bylaws, statement of faith, and conflict-of-interest policy in this packet.

The organizational meeting is where the corporation becomes an organization. Its minutes are among the records to keep forever. The agenda:

1. **Adopt the bylaws.** They should describe both the corporate layer (board, officers, meetings, quorum, terms, amendment) and the religious layer (the congregation and the opt-in register, Circles and the Established Congregation, the Hand of the Hour, the Veiled Ascent mapped to Licensed Officiant (rank IV), Ordained Minister (rank V) and Elder (rank VI), the Council of the Unbroken Eye as ecclesiastical body, and the Keeper of the Record as the founder's ecclesiastical office, as the draft bylaws Articles 3, 4, 5, 8 and 9 provide), and should invoke § 4-33-180 by stating that the statement of faith and the Edicts are the religious doctrine that governs the corporation's affairs.
2. **Elect officers.** The SOS annual report lists "names of principal officers"; the Act's specific officer requirements were not retrieved **[VERIFY: the Act's required-officers section was not researched; counsel to supply the citation]**. At minimum: a President, a Secretary (minutes, registers, records), and a Treasurer (books, bank, acknowledgments). The faith's rotating Hand of the Hour can be honored by a President (the bylaws allow the title Presiding Officer) elected for one-year terms, no more than three in a row (bylaws § 7.2), but one person must be identifiable to the state and the bank at any given time.
3. **Adopt the statement of faith** as the creed (Schedule A, line 1) and the **Code of Doctrine and Discipline** (the Edicts, "What We Are Not," the rules of the Ledger, and the signs a Circle has gone wrong, collected under one title) (Schedule A, line 3).
4. **Adopt the conflict-of-interest policy** (the packet's draft follows the IRS sample in Appendix A to the Form 1023 instructions) and have every director sign the first annual statement that night. Form 1023 Part V asks whether such a policy has been adopted.
5. **Adopt an ordination policy.** Who may be ordained, licensed, or commissioned; the required course of study (the Veiled Ascent, administered, examined, and recorded); the approving body; the certificate; the register; removal. Schedule A, line 12 says a prescribed course of study "doesn't include self-ordination or paying a fee for an ordination certificate without completing a course of study." The draft bylaws already answer this: Licensed Officiants (rank IV) and Ordained Ministers (rank V) officiate; the Council of the Unbroken Eye examines and confers; the Keeper of the Record (the founder) maintains the literature but holds no authority over money or persons. Do not ordain anyone at the organizational meeting. The draft minutes (§ 19) ordain the founder on day one on the strength of having written the course of study; that is the pattern Schedule A line 12 calls self-ordination, and it conflicts with bylaws § 9.7(c). Instead, enter the founder in the course-of-study record as a candidate, have the two independent examining directors review the founder's documented practice, and confer licensing or ordination only after the minimum periods in bylaws § 9.7(c) have run, or ask counsel in writing whether an exception is defensible. Whatever the title, the person must actually perform religious functions (conduct worship, perform rites, administer the religious body), because that is what the minister-level tax rules and the ministerial exception turn on.
6. **Fix the fiscal year.** Calendar year is recommended: it lines up the Arkansas annual report (August 1), donor acknowledgments (by January 31), and, if ever needed, Form 990-N (May 15).
7. **Adopt the Calendar of Holy Days and Observances** as the church's liturgical calendar and the weekly order of service as its regular service (Schedule A, lines 6 and 7).
8. **Resolve the church posture.** A resolution stating that the corporation is organized and operated as a church within the meaning of IRC §§ 170(b)(1)(A)(i) and 508(c)(1)(A), that it will hold regular religious services at an established place of worship, and that it intends to seek recognition on Form 1023 with Schedule A after a period of documented practice. If instead the board chooses the religious-organization path, resolve that and calendar Form 990-N.
9. **Authorize the bank account** and name the signers (Step 9). Adopt a two-signature or dual-approval rule for disbursements above a threshold; the research found no legal rule requiring it, so it is the board's own internal control, and it should be written into the bylaws or a finance policy.
10. **Adopt a records policy** (retention periods in the compliance calendar) and designate the Secretary as custodian.
11. **Adopt a political-activity statement.** All 501(c)(3)s, "including churches," are absolutely prohibited from participating or intervening in any candidate's campaign (Pub. 1828). The 2025 consent-judgment effort in *National Religious Broadcasters v. Long* was reportedly dismissed for lack of jurisdiction on March 31, 2026, so the ban is fully in force. The faith's own rule ("No politics") makes this easy to adopt and easy to keep.
12. **Accept the founder's transfer of the website, domain, and literature** to the corporation, or a license of them, with the terms written down. If the founder keeps personal ownership of fractum.faith while the church uses it, that is a related-party arrangement the IRS will ask about (Form 1023 Part V); the cleanest answer is an outright gift to the corporation, documented.

### Step 10. Decide on Form 1023, prepare it, and file it (prepare March through June 2027; file July through December 2027)

**Who:** Board authorizes; Treasurer and Secretary assemble; counsel (and, if used, a CPA) reviews; an officer signs.
**Cost:** $600 user fee, paid on Pay.gov when filed (IRS fee page last reviewed October 9, 2026; "subject to change"); professional fees [ATTORNEY/CPA FEE FOR FORM 1023].
**Time:** Two to three months to prepare well; IRS processing time **[VERIFY the IRS's currently posted processing times; not researched]**.
**Before this:** At least six months of Step 3 records; the website changes in the risk memo completed; Steps 6 through 9 done.

**The choice: file, or rely on IRC § 508(c)(1)(A)?**

A church is not required to apply, and many never do. Reasons this church should apply anyway:

- Nearly every Arkansas benefit in Steps 11 through 13 presumes an IRS letter: the SOS Form EX-01 asks for IRS documentation "if applicable" and the SOS three-step process says the applicant "must have IRS Tax Determination letter" for solicitation registration; DFA's state income-tax exemption route for organizations with an IRS letter is simpler than Form AR1023CT; the Act 1007 sales-tax application reportedly requires the determination letter.
- Donors, grantmakers, and banks expect it. Pub. 1828: many churches seek recognition "because this recognition assures church leaders, members and contributors that the church is recognized as exempt."
- A determination letter classifying the organization as a church is the cleanest proof that no Form 990 is required and that IRC § 7611 applies.

Reasons to hesitate: $600 plus preparation; the approved Form 1023 and IRS correspondence become open to public inspection; and a denial creates a record. The risk memo addresses when a denial is likely enough that counsel would advise waiting.

Promoters who sell "508(c)(1)(A) churches" as a way to escape all oversight should be ignored; the section only excuses churches from applying, and a court has called arguments built on it frivolous (*Steeves v. IRS*, per secondary reporting).

**Timing.** Form 1023 must be filed electronically on Pay.gov, with all attachments combined into a single PDF in the prescribed order (organizing document, amendments, bylaws, powers of attorney, supplemental responses, expedite request). A church may be recognized from its formation date regardless of the 27-month rule; a non-church religious organization must file within 27 months after the end of the month of formation to be exempt from formation (for a December 2026 formation, by March 31, 2029). Because the fallback is religious-organization status, treat March 31, 2029 as a hard outside deadline and the second half of 2027 as the target.

**What goes in it:**

- **Part III**: the articles with the purpose and dissolution clauses.
- **Part IV narrative**: for each activity, what it is, who conducts it, when and where, how it furthers exempt purposes, what percentage of total time it takes (totaling 100%), and how it is funded. Volunteer hours count. A restatement of the mission is the most common defect. Everything must be consistent with the articles, bylaws, website, and financials.
- **Part V**: directors, officers, their relationships, compensation (none), the conflict-of-interest policy.
- **Financial data** for the periods the form requires **[VERIFY the current years required]**.
- **Schedule A**: creed (line 1), literature (2), code of doctrine and discipline (3), ecclesiastical government (4), association with other churches (5: none), form of worship (6), regular days and times with order of service and how they further religious purposes (7), average attendance of members and non-members (7a), established place of worship (8), established congregation and membership (9 through 9e), rites (10), religious instruction of the young (11: none), prescribed course of study for religious leaders (12), and any additional support (15).
- **The website**: the IRS will read it. Attach a representative copy as of the filing date.

Do not use Form 1023-EZ; churches are ineligible.

### Step 11. Charitable-solicitation exemption with the Secretary of State (file Form EX-01 as soon as the IRS letter arrives, or earlier on counsel's advice)

**Who:** Secretary, with counsel.
**Cost:** No fee.
**Time:** One submission by email to charities@sos.arkansas.gov (501-683-0094).
**Before this:** Step 6 (articles), proof of good standing, and ideally the IRS letter (Step 10).

Arkansas charitable registration is administered by the SOS Charities section, not the Attorney General, under Ark. Code Ann. §§ 4-28-401 to -416. A "charitable organization" must register (§ 4-28-402, Form CR-01, free) before soliciting contributions and must file an annual financial report (§ 4-28-403, Form CR-03) by August 1. Religious organizations are exempt under § 4-28-404(1) if exempt from federal tax and free of private inurement, "provided each organization shall submit an application for a reporting exemption" (Form EX-01, Exempt Organization Verification, 2025 version) with IRS documentation "if applicable," the filed articles, and proof of good standing. Incomplete submissions are rejected.

**Two open questions counsel must resolve:**

- The mirrored statute says exempt organizations are relieved of the "reporting requirement under §§ 4-28-403 and 4-28-405," while SOS guidance says the exemption covers registration under § 4-28-402 as well. If the narrower reading is right, a church that solicits may still have to file CR-01 **[VERIFY]**.
- Whether a church that has not applied to the IRS can satisfy EX-01's "if applicable" with a statement that churches are automatically exempt **[VERIFY with the SOS Charities section]**.

**Practical note:** if the church keeps its promise and never solicits contributions (no appeals, no fundraising counsel, no paid solicitors, no sales promotions), the registration requirement may never be triggered at all. Filing EX-01 anyway is cheap insurance.

### Step 12. Arkansas tax exemptions (after the IRS letter; some items earlier)

**Who:** Treasurer, with counsel or a CPA.
**Cost:** No state fees were found for these applications **[VERIFY]**.
**Time:** Each is a single submission; DFA response times **[VERIFY]**.
**Before this:** Step 10's determination letter for the simplest routes.

**12a. State income tax.** Arkansas does not automatically honor federal exemption. Under Ark. Code Ann. § 26-51-303 and DFA Rule 1.26-51-303, the organization applies to the Arkansas Revenue Division, Corporation Income Tax Section, P.O. Box 919, Little Rock, AR 72203-0919. With an IRS letter: submit a copy of the letter, pages 1 and 2 of Form 1023, and a statement citing § 26-51-303. Without an IRS letter: Form AR1023CT (Application for Income Tax Exempt Status) with the articles and bylaws. Whether a church with no Arkansas-source unrelated business income must file anything at all is an open question **[VERIFY]**; the conservative course is to file.

**12b. Sales and use tax.** Two different rules:

- Sales *by* the church are exempt under § 26-52-401(1) ("by churches, except when the organizations may be engaged in business for profit"). This matters only if the church ever sells anything, which its doctrine says it will not.
- Purchases *by* the church are **not** exempt under the general rule (DFA Rule GR-39(B)) unless for resale. There is a new route: Act 1007 of 2025 added § 26-52-457, exempting sales to a "qualified nonprofit organization," meaning a 501(c)(3) with an annual operating budget under $200,000 that "performs charitable community-based services in the state to benefit residents of the state that are in need of assistance." The church's gloves, bags, batteries, and bulbs would be the purchases at issue. The church must apply to DFA for a certificate; a law-firm article reports the application is through ATAP, requires the IRS determination letter, officer and director names, NAICS code, EIN, and an activity description, and must be re-certified every three years (those details are from the firm, not DFA **[VERIFY]**). Two unresolved points: the effective date (DFA lists October 1, 2025; the firm says June 1, 2025) and whether a church's repair work counts as "charitable community-based services" to residents "in need of assistance," which is a fact question. Excluded purchases include motor vehicles, computers, cell phones, appliances, and construction materials. Keep the operating budget under $200,000, which will not be difficult.

**12c. Property tax.** Only relevant if the church ever owns or uses real property as a church. Ark. Const. art. 16, § 5(b) exempts "churches used as such"; Ark. Code Ann. §§ 26-3-301(12) and 26-3-303 elaborate; the test is actual use, not ownership (*Phillips v. Mission Fellowship Bible Church*, 59 Ark. App. 242 (1997)). In Sebastian County, call the Assessor at 479-784-1516 (Fort Smith office 479-783-8948) or email to have an application sent; no form number or deadline is published. The burden of proof is unsettled (AG Op. 2026-040 declined to opine while the standard was pending before the Arkansas Supreme Court) **[VERIFY the status of that case before applying]**. A rented community room does not raise this question for the church; it may for the landlord.

**12d. Employer registrations.** Only if the church ever pays anyone. Withholding: Form AR-1R (Combined Business Tax Registration) with DFA, or register online through ATAP; new registrants are monthly filers until DFA says otherwise. Unemployment insurance: service "in the employ of a church" is excluded from covered employment (§ 11-10-210(a)(4)(A)), but confirm the exclusion applies to this entity before relying on it **[VERIFY]**. Federally, churches and religious organizations are not liable for FUTA; minister compensation is subject to SECA rather than FICA, and any housing allowance must be designated by official board action in advance of payment (IRC § 107; Pub. 1828). None of this applies while everyone is a volunteer.

### Step 13. Insurance (quotes in January 2027; bind before the first organized cleanup with tools or ladders)

**Who:** Treasurer, with an insurance broker who handles nonprofits.
**Cost:** [GL PREMIUM], [D&O PREMIUM], [VOLUNTEER ACCIDENT PREMIUM] per year.
**Time:** Two to four weeks for quotes.
**Before this:** Steps 6 through 9 (the applications ask for formation date, EIN, bylaws, director list, financials, and activity descriptions).

Three separate products apply to a volunteer body that picks up litter, replaces bulbs, coils hoses, and tests smoke detectors in other people's homes:

1. **Commercial general liability** (injury or property damage caused by the church's activities).
2. **Directors and officers liability** (claims against board decisions; almost always claims-made, so continuous coverage and retroactive dates matter from the first policy onward).
3. **Volunteer accident/medical** (a volunteer's own injuries, regardless of fault; some carriers bind it only alongside GL). Workers' compensation generally does not cover unpaid volunteers.

Applications typically ask for legal name, formation date, 501(c)(3) status, bylaws and indemnification provisions, director and officer list, financial statements, employee and volunteer counts, activity descriptions, whether volunteers are screened and sign waivers, prior coverage and claims, and knowledge of any incident that could lead to a claim. Misstatements can void coverage.

The federal Volunteer Protection Act (42 U.S.C. §§ 14501-14505) and Arkansas's volunteer immunity statute (Ark. Code Ann. § 16-6-105) protect individual volunteers acting within scope and without gross negligence (and not while driving); neither protects the organization, and neither replaces insurance. Adopt a volunteer waiver and a safety rule that mirrors the faith's own: nothing electrical, nothing on a roof, nothing hazardous barehanded, nothing without permission.
