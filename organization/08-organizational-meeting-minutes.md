## 11. Compensation; Political Activity; No Solicitation

The chair reminded the Board of the restrictions in Article VI of the Articles of Incorporation and of the Internal Revenue Service's rules for churches and religious organizations: that no part of the Corporation's net earnings may inure to the benefit of insiders, that the prohibition on inurement is absolute, that no substantial part of its activities may be attempting to influence legislation (and that a church cannot elect the expenditure test of section 501(h)), and that the Corporation is absolutely prohibited from directly or indirectly participating or intervening in any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for elective public office. The chair noted that the 2025 effort to obtain a consent judgment limiting enforcement of the campaign prohibition against two churches was reportedly dismissed for lack of jurisdiction on March 31, 2026, and that the prohibition remains fully in force. The chair also noted that the Corporation's own doctrine states that it collects no tithes, dues, or suggested donations and takes no position in politics. On motion duly made, seconded, and unanimously carried, it was:

**RESOLVED,** that no director, officer, or minister of the Corporation shall receive compensation for service in that capacity, and that the Corporation shall pay no salary, stipend, or housing allowance to any person unless and until the Board, with every interested person recused under the Conflict-of-Interest Policy, approves the arrangement in advance as reasonable, documents the basis for that determination, and, in the case of any housing allowance for a minister, designates the allowance by official action taken in advance of payment, as the Internal Revenue Service requires for the exclusion under section 107 of the Internal Revenue Code;

**RESOLVED FURTHER,** that the Corporation shall not participate or intervene in any political campaign for or against any candidate for public office, in any publication, at any service, or through any channel, and that no statement of the Corporation shall endorse or oppose any candidate; that directors, officers, and ministers may speak as individuals but shall not do so in the Corporation's name, in its publications, or at its services; and that no substantial part of the Corporation's activities shall consist of attempting to influence legislation;

## 17. Church Posture; Authorization to Apply for Recognition of Exemption (Form 1023 with Schedule A)

The chair reviewed with the Board the two federal paths described in the packet's roadmap: that a church meeting the requirements of section 501(c)(3) is automatically exempt and is excepted from the requirement to apply for recognition (IRC § 508(c)(1)(A)) and from filing annual Form 990-series returns (IRC § 6033(a)(3)(A)); that many churches nevertheless seek a determination letter because it assures members, contributors, banks, and state agencies that the church is recognized as exempt; that a church cannot use Form 1023-EZ and must file the full Form 1023 with Schedule A; that the user fee is $600, paid through Pay.gov when the application is filed (as of the IRS page updated October 9, 2026, and subject to change); that a church may obtain recognition from the date of its formation even if it applies more than 27 months after formation; and that, if the Corporation cannot show an established congregation when it applies, it may seek recognition as a religious organization first and request church classification later on Form 8940. The chair also noted that Form 1023, Part IV, requires a complete narrative of each activity (what, who, when, where, how it furthers exempt purposes, the percentage of total time, and how it is funded), that the examiner will read fractum.faith and compare it to the narrative, and that the packet's risk memo lists the changes to the website to be completed before the narrative is written. On motion duly made, seconded, and unanimously carried, it was:

**RESOLVED,** that the Corporation is organized and shall be operated as a church within the meaning of sections 170(b)(1)(A)(i) and 508(c)(1)(A) of the Internal Revenue Code: it holds a recognized creed and form of worship, a definite ecclesiastical government, a formal code of doctrine and discipline, a literature of its own, an established place of worship, a regular congregation, and regular religious services, and it ordains ministers after a prescribed course of study, as the records adopted at this meeting reflect;

**RESOLVED FURTHER,** that the Corporation intends to seek recognition of exemption under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code by filing Form 1023 with Schedule A (Churches) after a period of not less than [SIX / TWELVE] months of documented regular services, observances, and acts of mending, on a date to be fixed by the Board on the advice of counsel; that until a determination letter is received the Corporation shall rely on the automatic exemption afforded to churches, and shall so state to any bank, donor, or agency that asks;

**RESOLVED FURTHER,** that [OFFICER NAME], [TITLE], is authorized to prepare and file Form 1023 with Schedule A on behalf of the Corporation through Pay.gov, to pay the user fee of $600 (or such other amount as is then in effect) from the Corporation's funds, to execute the application and any Form 2848 or 8821 appointing counsel to represent the Corporation before the Internal Revenue Service, to furnish the file-stamped Articles, the certified Bylaws, the Conflict-of-Interest Policy, the Statement of Faith, the Code of Doctrine and Discipline, the ordination policy, the records of services, and a representative copy of fractum.faith as attachments, and to respond to any request for additional information; and that the application shall be reviewed by counsel and approved by the Board before it is filed;

**RESOLVED FURTHER,** that, should counsel judge at the time of filing that the Corporation's congregation is not yet sufficiently established to support church classification, the authorized officer may instead apply for recognition as a religious organization under section 501(c)(3), in which case the Corporation shall file Form 990-N (if its gross receipts are normally $50,000 or less) or Form 990-EZ or Form 990, as applicable, by the fifteenth day of the fifth month after each fiscal year end, and shall request church classification on Form 8940 once the congregation is established; the Board records that an organization required to file an annual return that fails to do so for three consecutive years loses its exemption automatically; and
