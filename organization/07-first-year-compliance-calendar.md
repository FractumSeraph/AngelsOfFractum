# First-Year Compliance Calendar and Annual Recurring Obligations

*Draft for review by an Arkansas attorney. Not legal advice.*

**Organization:** Church of the Angels of Fractum (working legal name; alternative "Angels of Fractum")
**Place of formation:** Fort Smith, Sebastian County, Arkansas
**Prepared:** November 4, 2026
**Companion documents:** the roadmap (`00-roadmap.md`), the risk memo (`01-risk-memo.md`), and the draft articles, bylaws, statement of faith, and conflict-of-interest policy in this packet.

This calendar assumes the articles of incorporation are filed with the Arkansas Secretary of State in **December 2026** and that the corporation adopts the **calendar year** as its fiscal year. If the articles are filed in January 2027 instead, the first Arkansas annual report moves from August 1, 2027 to August 1, 2028 **[VERIFY against the NPD-01 packet, which says reports are due "August 1st the year following filing"]**, the 27-month outside deadline on the non-church path moves to April 30, 2029, no 2026 Form 990-N arises, the organizational meeting moves to February 2027, and everything else stays where it is.

The packet's risk memo recommends applying first as a 501(c)(3) religious organization and requesting church classification later on Form 8940; the roadmap and draft minutes instead have the board resolve church posture and file Schedule A after six to twelve months. This calendar is written for the second track and marks every entry that changes on the first. If the board follows the risk memo: file Form 1023 (no Schedule A) in February or March 2027, file Form 990-N by May 15, 2027 and every May 15 after, and move the Form 1023 entries in June through October 2027 to a Form 8940 request once the attendance record supports it. Where the obligation differs for an organization that is **not** a church, the entry says so.

Every deadline, fee, and form below comes from the research compiled on November 4, 2026, or is marked **[VERIFY]**. Fees and forms change; check the current versions before each filing.

Roles used in this calendar: **Board** (the directors); **Presiding Officer**; **Secretary** (minutes, registers, filings, records custodian); **Treasurer** (books, bank, acknowledgments, tax filings); **Keeper of the Record** (the founder's ecclesiastical office under bylaws Article 8; keeps the literature archive with the Secretary); **Counsel** (the Arkansas attorney); **Officiants** (those authorized under the ordination policy to conduct rites).

## Part 3. Records: what to keep, and for how long

### Keep forever (the permanent record)

1. The file-stamped articles of incorporation and every amendment.
2. The bylaws as adopted and every amendment, each dated.
3. The EIN confirmation letter (CP 575).
4. The Form 1023 exactly as filed, every attachment, the Pay.gov receipt, every IRS letter and every response, and the determination letter. (These are also the documents open to public inspection.)
5. Minutes of the organizational meeting and of every board meeting and written consent, signed.
6. The statement of faith, the Code of Doctrine and Discipline, the liturgical calendar, and the order of service, each version dated.
7. The conflict-of-interest policy and every signed annual statement.
8. The ordination policy and the ordination register, including the course-of-study records and the approving body's decisions.
9. The membership register and the weekly attendance records (the proof of a regular congregation and regular services; they are never stale for this purpose).
10. The written religious history, kept current.
11. Dated archives of the website and of every piece of published literature, including the versions before the pre-filing revisions.
12. The founder's written decision on sincerity from the roadmap's Step 1, and the deed of gift or license for the website, domain, and literature.
13. Every Arkansas annual report as filed, with confirmation.
14. Every state exemption document: SOS EX-01 acceptance, DFA income-tax exemption letter, Act 1007 certificate, property-tax exemption decisions.
15. Every insurance policy ever held, with declarations pages and the D&O retroactive date (claims-made policies make old policies relevant to new claims).
16. Deeds, leases, and titles to any property.
17. Records establishing the basis of any property, for as long as the property is held and then for the limitations period after disposal (Pub. 4221-PC).

### What the church deliberately does not keep

Consistent with the faith's promises and the IRS's needs, the church keeps the number of attendees at each service, not their names; a person who has asked to be entered in the membership register is recorded there, not on the attendance sheet (bylaws § 12.4). One exception must be stated plainly, on the website and in the records policy: anyone who takes part in an organized Mending in the church's name signs a one-time volunteer acknowledgment and waiver (bylaws §§ 4.6, 13.6), which carries their name and is kept only for as long as the insurer and counsel require. A waiver is signed once, not at every service, is filed separately from the attendance records, and is never used to count, rank, or contact anyone. The Sacrifice Ledger is each person's own and is never collected. The Count reported at a gathering is recorded as a sum, never by name. Write this into the records policy so that the examiner, the congregation, and the website all describe the same practice.
