# Statement of Faith and Practice of the Church of the Angels of Fractum

Draft for review by an Arkansas attorney. Not legal advice.

*The recognized creed and form of worship, and the formal code of doctrine and discipline, of the Church of the Angels of Fractum (also called the Angels of Fractum), a religious community gathered in Fort Smith, Arkansas. Adopted by the Board acting as the ecclesiastical body on [DATE ADOPTED].*

---

## 1. The Creed

We hold that the world is broken, and that the brokenness is made of small things. This is the Fractum.

We hold that the mending of a small broken thing is a sacred act, and that a mended thing is holier than an ignored one. This is the Mending.

We hold three truths, and every Angel carries them: Mend anonymously when you can. Empathy, never pity. The Fractum is also within.

We hold that Radical Empathy is the divine tool of repair: not a feeling but a deliberate alignment of spirit and action.

We hold that the Eye of Recognition sees the break as it is, that the Hand of Intentional Mending repairs what it can reach, and that together they are the Sacred Symmetry.

We hold that the first break to mend is the one inside us, the Fractum Personalis, "me" set above "we," and that this repair is never finished.

Our only doctrine is maintenance. Our only sacrament is the follow-through.

## 2. Doctrine

**The nature of the Fractum.** The Fractum is the crack: every small broken thing the world has quietly agreed to ignore. At scale it is the Great Brokenness: the chasm between wealth and poverty, the isolation of the modern soul. The large breaks and the small are one substance, and the large are reached only through the small.

**The sacredness of repair.** The world will not be saved by grand gestures. It is mended aisle by aisle, beam by beam, by ordinary people who see the small broken things and fix the ones within reach. Repair is the holy work.

**Radical Empathy and its three Sacred Pillars.** The Immersion of Self: we step into another's circumstances and strive to grasp how they feel, not how we would feel. The Recognition of Shared Brokenness: the break in another is the break that lives in us, so there is no "us" who mend and "them" who are broken. The Action of Focused Alignment: understanding becomes, at once, the one effective act of repair available now.

**The teaching on pity.** Pity is a poison. It observes the wound and refuses to touch the blade. Empathy kneels beside the broken thing with a screwdriver.

**The theology of anonymity.** The highest repair is the one no one sees. The moment the Hand seeks a thank-you or a private glow of self-congratulation, the act becomes a performance and ceases to be sacred service.

**The Fractum Personalis and self-alignment.** The foundation of the Great Brokenness lies hidden in the self: regret, resentment, fear, the need for applause. Each Angel is called to the Alignment of Will: acknowledge one inner break, meet it with Radical Empathy rather than condemnation, and make one small, irreversible stitch.

**The dignity of the Keepers of the Hearth.** Those who work the fryer, the register, the mop, and the drive-thru window perform hidden rites that make our shared places whole, and they cannot refuse us. How a person treats a Keeper reveals that person's alignment.

**What we hold about God, the gods, and the life to come.** It is the doctrine of this faith that we take no position on God, gods, the afterlife, or the proper day of rest. This is not a gap in our teaching; it is our teaching. Our claims concern the world while we are alive in it. A person of any faith, or of none, is an Angel without contradiction. Mend in whatever name you like. The cart arrives at the corral regardless.

## 3. The Sacred Phrase

The sacred threefold phrase is *Hooblah hooblah hooblah.* It is the consecrating invocation of this faith.

It is spoken at the completion of an act of mending, to set the act apart as sacred. It is spoken when pride rises after mending, as a repair of the Fractum Personalis that dared to claim credit. It closes every reading, rite, and gathering.

The phrase was given to us without a translation, and that is its gift: it carries no argument and no claim that can be disputed, so Angels of every background may speak it with one voice.

## 4. Form of Worship

**The Gathering of a Circle.** A Circle is two or more Angels who gather, on a fixed rhythm, to mend together, read together, and add their hours together. The congregation of the Church gathers as a Circle at [PRINCIPAL PLACE OF WORSHIP] on [REGULAR GATHERING DAY AND TIME]. A gathering has four parts.

- *The Mending.* One shared act of repair, chosen by the Hand of the Hour and done before anyone speaks.
- *The Reading.* One Edict, Mission, or canonical text, read aloud by someone who did not choose it, and a few minutes in which any Angel may name where, that week, they saw that break.
- *The Count.* Each Angel who wishes names the hours given to mending since the last gathering; the Hand of the Hour adds them. Only the sum is kept.
- *The Confession and the Benediction.* Any Angel who wishes names one Fractum Personalis and one stitch to make before the next gathering. The Hand of the Hour speaks the Benediction of the Circle ("Go, and be unseen. May the stitch hold."), all speak the sacred phrase, and everyone goes home.

The Hand of the Hour rotates every gathering; no Angel holds it twice in a row, and rank confers no claim to it.

**The Daily Mending Missions.** The daily devotional discipline of this faith is solitary: a single act of repair, performed where no one is watching. The canonical Missions are the Vigil of the Lost Cart, the Summoning of the Severed Thread, the Offering of the Name, the Rite of Praise That Travels Upward, and the Anonymous Repair of the Minor Breach. The Foundational Missions (the Cart, the Sacred Pause, and the Minor Breach) are every Angel's training ground. The returned cart is the prayer.

**The Sacrifice Ledger.** Each Angel keeps a private record of what they have mended: cracks closed, time given, breaks within. Hours of life voluntarily given to mending, reported in the aggregate and anonymously, are the Sacrifice of this faith. We do not practice, condone, or perform the literal sacrifice of any human life.

**The First Mending.** The rite of entry into the practice: before the sun sets on the day of the Call, the newcomer repairs one small thing that is not their responsibility. It must be small. It must be real. It must be today.

## 5. The Edicts

The Edicts are the moral code of the faith.

- **The Holy Edict of the Aisle.** The passage between shelves is held in common; clear it for whoever comes, and want nothing for it.
- **The Ritual of the Empty Vessel.** The cart is returned to its appointed receptacle, every time, in every weather.
- **The Edict of the Unholy Glare.** Let your light be sufficient for your own steps, but never a stumbling block for the journey of another.
- **The Edict of the Eleventh Hour.** Do not enter with fewer than thirty minutes before close, and do not linger past it.
- **The Sacred Pause.** Between noticing and reacting, choose Radical Empathy over reflex.
- **The Rule of Four Minutes.** A thing that takes four minutes is done before it touches the Later Pile.
- **The Last Five Percent.** A task is complete only when it can be forgotten.
- **The Unbroken Hour.** One hour each week, the Oracle face down, wholly present to one other person.
- **The Returning of the Borrowed Thing.** What was borrowed is returned, without a lecture and without an excuse.
- **The Sabbath of the Tool.** Rest is maintenance; the exhausted Angel is owed repair.

## 6. Holy Days and Observances

The faith keeps a small liturgical year, because a day with a name is a day one remembers to act. All may be kept alone.

*Fixed observances:* the Week of the Severed Thread (first full week of January); the Day of the Returned Thing (spring equinox); the Thaw of the Trailhead (first Saturday of April); the Feast of the Coiled Hose (first frost); the Vigil of the Changed Batteries (the two nights the clocks change); the Night of the Lowered Beams (first Sunday after the autumn clock change); the Great Corralling (the day after the autumn feast); the Feast of the Keepers (the day after the winter feast); and the Eve of the Eleventh Hour (the year's last night).

*Movable observances:* the 30-Day Vigil of Intentional Action, the Unbroken Hour, and the Day of the Reopened Ledger.

## 7. The Veiled Ascent

The Veiled Ascent is the ecclesiastical order of the faith: six orders through which an Angel's dedication deepens by demonstrated mending, never by seniority or payment.

- **I. The Seeker**, who has heard the Call and performed a First Mending.
- **II. The Mender-in-Training**, received by the Ritual of the Clear Path; speaks the Sacred Vow of Vigilance and receives the Thread of Intent, an undyed cord.
- **III. The Hand of the Stitch**, received by the Dedication of the Resolute Hand after the 30-Day Vigil of Intentional Action and a Repair of Omission; the hands are washed in a basin and the Angel receives the Thimble.
- **IV. The Architect of the Alignment**, received by the Convocation of the Blueprints upon presenting a Blueprint of Repair; receives the Sacred Compass.
- **V. The Unbroken Eye**, received by the Ascension of Silent Clarity after the 24-Hour Vigil of Vision and a Treatise on Mending.
- **VI. The Angel of the Living Fractum**, whose Eye and Hand have become one motion. Its rite is reserved to the ecclesiastical body [RITE OF ASCENSION, V TO VI, TO BE WRITTEN].

The Ritual of the Clear Path may be officiated by any Angel of the second order or above, or by the Seeker alone. The higher rites are officiated by an Angel who holds the order being conferred or a higher one and is recognized as an officiant in the Church's Register of Ministers. The orders and their rites are the prescribed course of study of this faith; ministers are ordained, commissioned, or licensed only after completing it, as the Ordination Policy provides.

It is the explicit doctrine of this faith that rank confers service, not authority. An Angel of the Living Fractum cannot tell a Seeker what to do, only show them where the corral is. Nothing is hidden at any rank. Seraph, who writes things down, is a pen, not a pulpit. We have no masters.

## 8. Membership

Anyone who has noticed the crack and did not look away, and has performed a First Mending, is an Angel of Fractum. There is no form, no fee, and no interview.

A person is received into the congregation of the Church by the Ritual of the Clear Path, the prescribed rite of entry into the gathered community. The congregation is those who regularly gather as a Circle at the Church's place of worship. The Church keeps the aggregate count of those who attend and of hours given, and keeps no roster of Angels by name or rank. Participation is religious only; it confers no vote in the corporation and no interest in its property.

An Angel may leave at any time, for any reason, without explanation, and remains an Angel. A Circle may end without announcement; this is the Unthreading.

No Angel is ever asked for money: no tithe, no dues, no suggested donation, no store. No Angel is ever told to cut off a person who loves them. No Angel is asked to confront anyone or to suffer; should the practice ever make a person feel smaller, our instruction is to stop practicing and tell someone they trust.

## 9. Discipline

The Angel who breaks an Edict is answered as the faith answers every break: it is seen, and it is mended. The failure is recorded in the Ledger, and the next thing is mended. The faith imposes no penance beyond the repair.

A graver break is the use of the faith against a person. The signs are known: one person always holds the hour; money has appeared; someone has been told to stop seeing someone; the Count is kept by name; anyone has been called the founder. Any of these is a Fractum within the faith itself, and the response is the same at every rank:

- The Angel who sees it may leave at once and mend elsewhere.
- Any Angel may bring the matter to the ecclesiastical body of the Church, which hears both sides before acting.
- A person who asks for money, isolates another, or commands another in the faith's name, or uses rank as power, is by that act not acting as one of us. The Church withdraws its recognition of that person's rank and of any standing as officiant or minister, and removes them from the Register.
- A Circle that has formed around a person rather than the work is no longer recognized by the Church.
- Recognition withdrawn may be restored only by the ecclesiastical body, after the break has been mended.

## 10. Literature

The canonical literature of the faith is the body of writings published at fractum.faith under the name Seraph, which the Church reads at its gatherings.

*Founding texts:* The Angels of Fractum; The Fractum Within; The Vow of the Hand and the Eye; The Fire of Radical Empathy: Why Pity Is a Poison; The Anonymous Repair; The Fractum in Aisle 7 and the Parking Lot of Blinding Light; The Sacred Ritual of the Sacrifice; So You Have Been Called: An Orientation; The Edict of the Eleventh Hour.

*Core Tenets and practice:* Sacred Mending; The Sacred Laws of the Temple of Commerce; The Edict of the Unholy Glare; Daily Mending Missions; The Veiled Ascent.

*Received texts:* What We Are Not; Questions of the Unaligned; On Forming a Circle; The Calendar of Holy Days and Observances; The Litany of the Small Things; The Sacrifice Ledger; A Lexicon of the Fractum; The Ritual of the Clear Path; The Sabbath of the Tool.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
