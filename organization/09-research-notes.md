# Research notes behind the formation packet

Compiled November 4, 2026 by three research passes over primary sources (IRS.gov, the Arkansas Secretary of State and DFA sites, statute text, and court opinions) plus web searches. **Confidence** is the researcher's own rating: *verified* means the primary source was read; *likely* means a reliable secondary source; *uncertain* means it could not be confirmed and must be checked. Statute text often came from unofficial mirrors; counsel must check every citation against the official Arkansas Code and current IRS forms before anything is filed.

## Arkansas formation and state compliance

Arkansas has no separate "religious corporation" statute: a church incorporates under the general Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993 (Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-101 et seq., Act 1147 of 1993) and simply checks "Religious Corporation" as its type in the articles (§ 4-33-202(a)(2)); § 4-33-180 then makes religious doctrine control over conflicting provisions of the Act to the extent the federal/state constitutions require. Articles are filed with the Secretary of State on Form NPD-01 (or the "501(c)(3) compliant language" variant NPD-01-501-c-3): $50 on paper, $45 online (as of the SOS site on 2026-11-04). Minimum three directors (§ 4-33-803); one or more incorporators (§ 4-33-201), each must sign; registered agent with a physical Arkansas street address. Annual report under § 4-33-131 is due August 1 each year (first one the year after formation), no fee, filed online; nonprofits do not file the franchise tax report; missing it for 60+ days is grounds for administrative dissolution (§ 4-33-1420(5)). Charitable solicitation is administered by the Secretary of State (not the AG) under §§ 4-28-401 to -416; religious organizations are exempt under § 4-28-404(1) only after filing Form EX-01 (Exempt Organization Verification) with IRS documentation, articles, and proof of good standing. Sales tax: sales BY churches are exempt (§ 26-52-401(1)) but purchases BY churches are not, unless for resale (DFA Rule GR-39) or unless the organization qualifies under new § 26-52-457 (Act 1007 of 2025: 501(c)(3) with operating budget under $200,000 that performs charitable community-based services for Arkansans in need; apply to DFA for a certificate). Property tax: Ark. Const. art. 16 § 5(b) exempts "churches used as such"; § 26-3-301(12) and § 26-3-303 elaborate; the test is actual use, not ownership; in Sebastian County (Fort Smith) the applicant calls the Assessor (479-784-1516) or emails to have an application sent; burden-of-proof standard is currently unsettled (AG Op. 2026-040). State income tax exemption is not automatic: DFA requires a copy of the IRS determination letter plus pages 1–2 of Form 1023 and a statement citing § 26-51-303, or Form AR1023CT with articles and bylaws if there is no IRS letter. Employer registration: Form AR-1R (or ATAP) with DFA for withholding; DWS employer account for unemployment insurance, though service in the employ of a church is excluded from covered "employment" under § 11-10-210(a)(4)(A). Name rules: § 4-33-401 (as amended by Act 256 of 2023) requires a name distinguishable on SOS records, with no required corporate suffix found in the nonprofit act; the SOS entity search on 2026-11-04 returned zero records for "fractum," "angels of fractum," and "church of the angels," so the names appear available pending SOS review.

## Federal 501(c)(3) and church recognition

Research compiled 2026-11-04 from primary sources read directly (IRS.gov pages and PDFs: "Churches defined" page, Form 1023 and 1023-EZ instructions, user-fee page, Form 990-N page, automatic-revocation page, Pub 1828 (Rev. 8-2015), Pub 557 (Rev. 1-2025), Pub 517; Cornell LII statute text for IRC 107, 508, 1402, 6033, 7611, 42 U.S.C. 2000bb-1 and 2000e-1; the Federal Circuit opinion in Foundation of Human Understanding; the D. Neb. opinion in Cavanaugh v. Bartelt; the Supreme Court's Our Lady of Guadalupe opinion) plus web searches for case law and news. Core picture for a drafter: (1) Any 501(c)(3) must pass the organizational test (purpose clause limited to 501(c)(3) purposes + dissolution clause permanently dedicating assets; IRS sample language quoted below) and the operational test (no inurement, no more than insubstantial private benefit, no substantial lobbying, absolute ban on candidate campaign intervention, nothing illegal or contrary to fundamental public policy). (2) "Church" is a subset of "religious organization." The IRS applies 14 non-exclusive characteristics plus facts and circumstances; the IRS's own Form 1023 instructions say an applicant is "generally required to have a congregation or other religious membership group," and courts treat the "associational test" (a body of believers that assembles regularly to worship) as the decisive factor. Broadcast/Internet dissemination alone does not satisfy it (Foundation of Human Understanding, Fed. Cir. 2010). (3) Churches are automatically exempt and need not apply (IRC 508(c)(1)(A)) or file Form 990 (IRC 6033(a)(3)(A)); non-church religious organizations must apply within 27 months (or lose retroactive status) and must file 990/990-EZ/990-N (990-N if receipts normally $50,000 or less) or face automatic revocation after three missed years. Many churches still file Form 1023 (user fee $600 as of IRS page updated 09-Oct-2026; filed electronically on Pay.gov) to get a determination letter that reassures donors and establishes church classification; churches cannot use Form 1023-EZ (eligibility worksheet Q12) and must complete Schedule A. (4) For whether a belief system is a "religion," the IRS (Pub 557) asks only whether the beliefs are "truly and sincerely held" and whether practices are not illegal or contrary to clearly defined public policy; courts use Seeger/Welsh (sincere belief occupying a place parallel to God in the believer's life) and the Malnak/Africa three indicia (ultimate questions, comprehensiveness, formal/external signs). Parody is the main hazard: Cavanaugh v. Bartelt held Pastafarianism is "a parody," not a religion, because "the FSM Gospel is plainly a work of satire." By contrast, The Satanic Temple (2019) and the First Church of Cannabis (2015) reportedly received IRS recognition by submitting creed, rituals, literature and real congregational activity. Universal Life Church's history shows that even a recognized church loses exemption when it operates for non-exempt purposes (tax-avoidance advice, uncontrolled congregations). (5) The "religious protections" split: the ministerial exception (Hosanna-Tabor, Our Lady of Guadalupe), Title VII 702, RFRA and church-autonomy doctrine attach to religious organizations and institutions generally and do not depend on IRS "church" classification; the Form 990 exemption, the application exemption, the IRC 7611 audit limits, and the Form 8274 FICA election are church-specific; the clergy housing allowance (IRC 107) and minister SECA/withholding rules (IRC 1402, Form 4361) attach to an individual who is a "duly ordained, commissioned, or licensed minister" performing ministerial services for a tax-exempt religious body, and Schedule A says self-ordination or a paid certificate without a course of study does not count as a prescribed course of study. Everything here is for a drafter to mark "verify with counsel"; several items (parody-religion outcomes, Johnson Amendment litigation, ULC settlement figures) rest on secondary reporting.

### Open questions for counsel

- Does the proposed organization have, or will it have, a body of believers that assembles regularly and in person (or in some interactive communal form) to worship? Under Foundation of Human Understanding and Form 1023 Schedule A Line 9, a web-only, broadcast, or single-family group is a 'religious organization,' not a 'church.' Counsel must decide whether to apply as a church (Schedule A) now, or as a 501(c)(3) religious organization first and seek church classification later via Form 8940.
- Is the belief system sincerely held and non-satirical in substance? If any founding materials are parodic, satirical, or primarily political/philosophical, Cavanaugh v. Bartelt and the Malnak/Africa indicia are directly adverse; counsel should review creed, literature, and public statements for 'deep and imponderable' content, comprehensiveness, and formal/external signs, and should vet the organization name ('Angels of Fractum' or similar) and any gaming/fiction origins.
- Are any practices or sacraments illegal or contrary to clearly defined public policy (Pub 557 guideline 2; Schedule A; Iowaska; First Church of Cannabis)?
- Who will be the 'duly ordained, commissioned, or licensed' ministers, and what prescribed course of study will they complete? Schedule A Line 12 excludes self-ordination and paid certificates; this affects IRC 107 housing allowance, withholding, Form 4361, and the church characteristics.
- Which organizational form (Arkansas nonprofit corporation, unincorporated association, corporation sole, trust) and whether Arkansas is a state on which the IRS allows reliance on state law for the dissolution clause (Rev. Proc. 2024-22 vs. the Rev. Proc. 82-2 list cited in the Form 1023 instructions); safest course is to include the IRS sample purpose and dissolution clauses verbatim.
- Whether to file Form 1023 at all (cost $600 plus preparation; detailed disclosures) versus relying on automatic church exemption under 508(c)(1)(A); counsel should weigh donor assurance, state sales/property-tax exemption needs, bank requirements, and the risk that a denial creates a record.
- Current status of the Johnson Amendment litigation (NRB v. Bessent, dismissed Mar. 31, 2026; appeal to the Fifth Circuit reported) and whether any IRS guidance has changed since Pub 1828 (Rev. 8-2015); as of the sources found, the candidate-campaign ban is fully in force.
- Whether the organization will employ anyone, and if so whether to make the Form 8274 FICA election (strict timing), how to designate housing allowances in advance by board action, and how to document ministerial status for the ministerial exception and Title VII 702 (multi-factor tests for 'religious corporation' vary by circuit).
- Whether Arkansas-specific religious-liberty, incorporation, charitable-solicitation registration, sales/property-tax exemption, and marriage-solemnization rules impose separate requirements (outside this federal research task).
- Verify pin cites and holdings not read in full here: Seeger/Welsh passages, Africa at 1032, Malnak at 208-09, Amos, Hobby Lobby, Kedroff/Milivojevich, ULC decisions and the $1.5M settlement figure, The Satanic Temple's IRS letter, and the 2010-2012 internet-church determination letters (third-party transcriptions).

## Practical formation and first-year operation

Research compiled 2026-11-04 via web search (no page fetches; most facts come from IRS.gov snippets, the Arkansas Secretary of State, court opinions, and practitioner sources). Key points: (1) EIN: free, online at irs.gov/EIN, one per responsible party per day, responsible party must be an individual with SSN/ITIN; Form SS-4 line 9a has a "Church or church-controlled organization" box. (2) Banks typically want articles, EIN, bylaws, a board resolution naming signers, and photo ID; a determination letter is usually not required. (3) The IRS sample conflict-of-interest policy is Appendix A to the Form 1023 instructions (current rev. Dec. 2024); the form asks whether you adopted one and, if not, how you handle conflicts; it is not legally mandatory. (4) No federal "independent majority" rule exists, but Form 1023 Part V asks about family/business relationships among directors, the IRS 2008 governance paper favors independent, active, appropriately sized boards, Form 990 Part VI uses a four-part independence test, and Arkansas requires at least three directors (Ark. Code § 4-33-803, verify). (5) Pub 1771: contemporaneous written acknowledgment for gifts of $250+; quid pro quo disclosure when payment exceeds $75; churches may state "no goods or services other than intangible religious benefits." (6) Volunteer time is never deductible; out-of-pocket costs and 14 cents/mile (statutory, IRC 170(i)) may be. (7) Records: keep as long as needed, minimum 3 years from return due/filed date, 4 years for employment tax, organizing documents/exemption application/determination letter permanently; Form 1023 and three years of 990s are open to public inspection (churches are exempt from 990 filing). (8) Insurance: GL, D&O (claims-made), and volunteer accident are separate products; applications ask for financials, bylaws, director list, employee/volunteer counts, prior claims, and known incidents. (9) Church status: courts apply the "associational test" (a body of believers that assembles regularly); Foundation of Human Understanding (Fed. Cir. 2010) held an electronic/virtual congregation and scattered seminars do not satisfy it, and PLR 201232034 said a website is not a place of worship; Schedule A asks for regular service days/times and average attendance; IRS 14 criteria weight "regular congregation" and "regular religious services" most heavily. (10) Ordination: Schedule A asks whether you ordain ministers and about a "prescribed course of study," which excludes self-ordination and fee-only certificates; a "formal code of doctrine and discipline" must be a meaningful doctrine, not a document drafted to pass the test. (11) Part IV narrative: describe each activity (what, who, where, how it furthers exempt purposes, % of time summing to 100%, funding); a mission restatement is a common failure; the IRS has denied an application in part because it could not find the website listed on the form. (12) Humor/parody: no IRS rule found on satire per se; the standard is sincerely held beliefs plus legal, non-public-policy-violating activities with no private inurement; the Satanic Temple reported church recognition in 2019, while a 2016 federal court held Pastafarianism is not a religion, and sham/tax-motivated "churches" are routinely denied or revoked. A public website calling the group a "cult" or saying parts are "a joke" is therefore a material risk to the sincerity showing and should be reviewed with counsel before filing.

### Open questions for counsel

- Does the organization intend to apply as a 'church' (Schedule A, automatic exemption, no Form 990, § 7611 protection) or as a non-church religious organization (Form 990 filing, Form 1023-EZ possibly available)? This choice drives the whole roadmap and must be made with counsel.
- Given a dispersed membership and largely solitary practice, can the organization credibly establish a fixed schedule of convened gatherings that meets the associational test (FHU, 614 F.3d 1383)? What minimum frequency, format (in-person vs. hybrid), and attendance documentation will counsel consider defensible?
- How should the public website's self-descriptions as a 'cult' and statements that parts of the practice are 'a joke' be handled before filing? Counsel must assess whether they undermine the 'sincerely held' showing and whether the site should be revised, archived, or explained in the Part IV narrative.
- Does the community cleanup/repair work count as a religious activity of the church or as a separate charitable activity, and does that affect percentage-of-time allocations and the church-vs-charity classification?
- What ordination process (curriculum, approving body, certificate, register) will be adopted, and does it satisfy the 'prescribed course of study' expectation rather than resembling self-ordination?
- Who will serve as the responsible party on the EIN and as initial directors, and are any of them related by family or business? If the founder controls the board, what safeguards (independent directors, COI policy, compensation approval) does counsel require?
- Does Arkansas's § 4-28-404 religious exemption excuse initial charitable registration or only annual reporting, and must Form EX-01 be filed before any solicitation?
- Confirm the current Ark. Code § 4-33-803 text (minimum three directors) and any Arkansas-specific bylaws requirements, plus whether Arkansas opted out of the federal Volunteer Protection Act.
- Confirm current Form 1023 user fee ($600 as of 2026-11-04), Form SS-4 revision, and the Form 1099-NEC reporting threshold at the time of filing.
- Which insurer products (GL, D&O, volunteer accident, possibly auto non-owned) are appropriate for cleanup/repair work involving tools, ladders, and vehicles, and what waiver/release language should volunteers sign?
