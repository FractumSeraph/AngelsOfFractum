## Why the IRS asks for this, in plain words

Form 1023, the application for recognition of exemption under section 501(c)(3), asks in Part V whether the organization has adopted a conflict of interest policy consistent with the sample policy the IRS publishes as Appendix A to the Form 1023 instructions and, if not, how the organization will handle conflicts. The IRS says the sample is an example only, that it does not prescribe specific requirements, and that organizations are expected to adapt it. Adopting a policy is not a legal condition of exemption. The IRS asks anyway, for a reason that goes to the heart of the tax law.

The one absolute rule for every 501(c)(3), churches included, is that none of its net earnings may inure to the benefit of an insider. IRS Publication 1828, the tax guide for churches and religious organizations, lists the insiders as the minister, the church board members, the officers, and in some circumstances employees, and states that "the prohibition against inurement to insiders is absolute; therefore, any amount of inurement is, potentially, grounds for loss of tax-exempt status." An insider who receives an excess benefit may also owe an excise tax under section 4958 of the Internal Revenue Code, and the managers who approved it may owe one too **[VERIFY: IRC § 4958(a)(2); not in the packet research]**. The IRS also asks on Form 1023, Part V, whether officers, directors, and trustees are related to one another by family or business, and uses the answers to look for private benefit. A written policy, followed and recorded, is how an organization shows that it noticed its own conflicts and handled them before anyone else had to.

This Church has an additional reason. Its own doctrine says there are no masters, that no Angel may be commanded, and that a Circle that has formed around a person rather than around the work is a sign something has gone wrong. A conflict of interest policy is the corporate form of that rule: no one decides a question in which they have a stake, and the record shows it.

This policy follows the structure and substance of the IRS sample and adapts it to the Church's own offices (the Board, the officers, the Keeper of the Record, the Council of the Unbroken Eye, and the clergy) and to the Church's own promises (no money collected by Circles, no compensation to directors, no secret arrangements). It is incorporated into the bylaws by Section 11.1 of the bylaws.

---

## Article 1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to protect the interests of the Church of the Angels of Fractum (the "Church") when it is considering entering into a transaction or arrangement that might benefit the private interest of a director, officer, or other person described in Article 2, or that might result in a possible excess benefit transaction. This policy is intended to supplement, not replace, any applicable state and federal laws governing conflicts of interest in nonprofit and charitable organizations.

---

## Article 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Interested person

An **interested person** is any of the following who has a direct or indirect financial interest, as defined below:

(a) a director of the Church;

(b) an officer of the Church (the President, the Secretary, the Treasurer, and any other officer the Board creates);

(c) the Keeper of the Record;

(d) a member of the Council of the Unbroken Eye, once the Council exists;

(e) a licensed officiant, ordained minister, or elder of the Church;

(f) a member of any committee to which the Board has delegated any of its powers; and

(g) any employee of the Church, if the Church ever has one, whose position gives them substantial influence over the Church's affairs.

### 2.2 Financial interest

A person has a **financial interest** if the person has, directly or indirectly, through business, investment, or family:

(a) an ownership or investment interest in any entity with which the Church has a transaction or arrangement;

(b) a compensation arrangement with the Church or with any entity or individual with which the Church has a transaction or arrangement; or

(c) a potential ownership or investment interest in, or compensation arrangement with, any entity or individual with which the Church is negotiating a transaction or arrangement.

**Compensation** includes direct and indirect remuneration as well as gifts or favors that are not insubstantial. Reimbursement of documented out-of-pocket expenses under Section 10.5 of the bylaws is not compensation.

A financial interest is not necessarily a conflict of interest. Under Article 3, a person who has a financial interest may have a conflict of interest only if the Board or the appropriate committee decides that a conflict of interest exists.

### 2.3 Family

**Family** means a person's spouse or domestic partner, ancestors, siblings (whole or half blood), children (natural or adopted), grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and the spouses or domestic partners of siblings, children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren. Wherever the bylaws speak of a person "related by blood, marriage, or domestic partnership," they mean family as defined in this section.

### 2.4 Transactions and arrangements that this Church must watch in particular

Without limiting the definitions above, the following are transactions or arrangements in which an interested person may have a financial interest and which must be disclosed and reviewed under this policy whenever they arise:

(a) any compensation, stipend, honorarium, or payment of any kind to a director, officer, the Keeper of the Record, or a clergy member, including any decision to compensate a minister for ministerial services (bylaws Section 9.12);

(b) any gift, license, lease, sale, or other transfer to or from the Church of the fractum.faith domain, the website, the literature of the faith, or any intellectual property in them, including the founder's initial transfer or license under Section 8.6 of the bylaws;

(c) any rental or use of property owned by an interested person or a family member, including a home used for the Reading, a vehicle used for Mendings, or a storage space for supplies, whether or not rent is charged;

(d) any purchase of goods or services (supplies, printing, web hosting, insurance, accounting, legal services) from an interested person, a family member, or a business in which either has an interest;

(e) any loan, guarantee, advance, or extension of credit between the Church and an interested person or family member, which Section 10.9 of the bylaws forbids in any case;

(f) any arrangement under which an interested person or family member would receive a fee, commission, royalty, or other payment from a third party because of the Church's activities, such as sales of robes, books, cords, thimbles, or other objects associated with the faith; and

(g) any reimbursement claim by an interested person above the thresholds in the bylaws or outside the budget.

## Article 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Duty to disclose

In connection with any actual or possible conflict of interest, an interested person must disclose the existence of the financial interest and be given the opportunity to disclose all material facts to the directors and to the members of any committee with Board-delegated powers that is considering the proposed transaction or arrangement. The disclosure must be made as soon as the interested person knows of the possible conflict, and in any event before the Board or committee votes.

### 3.2 Determining whether a conflict of interest exists

After disclosure of the financial interest and all material facts, and after any discussion with the interested person, the interested person shall leave the Board or committee meeting while the determination of a conflict of interest is discussed and voted on. The remaining Board or committee members shall decide, by majority vote of the disinterested members present, whether a conflict of interest exists.

### 3.3 Procedures for addressing the conflict of interest

(a) An interested person may make a presentation at the Board or committee meeting, but after the presentation the person shall leave the meeting during the discussion of, and the vote on, the transaction or arrangement involving the possible conflict of interest.

(b) The President of the Board, or the chair of the committee, shall, if appropriate, appoint a disinterested person or committee to investigate alternatives to the proposed transaction or arrangement. If the interested person is the President, the Secretary shall make the appointment.

(c) After exercising due diligence, the Board or committee shall determine whether the Church can obtain, with reasonable efforts, a more advantageous transaction or arrangement from a person or entity that would not give rise to a conflict of interest.

(d) If a more advantageous transaction or arrangement is not reasonably possible under circumstances not producing a conflict of interest, the Board or committee shall determine by a majority vote of the disinterested directors or members whether the transaction or arrangement is in the Church's best interest, for its own benefit, and whether it is fair and reasonable to the Church. In conformity with that determination, it shall decide whether to enter into the transaction or arrangement.

(e) In deciding whether a transaction or arrangement is fair and reasonable, the disinterested directors shall obtain and rely on appropriate comparable data (for example, the rent charged for similar rooms in Fort Smith, the price of similar goods from unrelated vendors, or compensation paid by comparable organizations for comparable services) and shall record in the minutes what data they used.

(f) For purposes of the Church's independent-majority rule (bylaws Section 6.4), a director who is found to have a conflict of interest in a transaction that the Board then approves is not an independent director for the current and following year, and the Board shall confirm that an independent majority remains before the transaction is approved.

### 3.4 Violations of the conflict of interest policy

(a) If the Board or committee has reasonable cause to believe that a person has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall inform the person of the basis for that belief and give the person an opportunity to explain the alleged failure to disclose.

(b) If, after hearing the person's response and making further investigation as the circumstances warrant, the Board or committee determines that the person has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall take appropriate disciplinary and corrective action. For a director or officer, that action may include removal under Section 6.8 or 7.7 of the bylaws. For the Keeper of the Record, it may include removal under Section 8.5. For a clergy member, it may include referral to the Council under Section 9.11. For any interested person, it shall include rescinding or correcting the transaction where that is possible and recovering any excess benefit the person received.

### 3.5 Transactions the policy does not permit at all

No procedure under this Article authorizes the Board or any committee to approve a transaction or arrangement that the bylaws forbid, including: compensation to a director or officer for service as such (bylaws Section 6.16 and 10.4); a loan or guarantee to an interested person (Section 10.9); the collection or holding of money by a Circle (Section 10.7); the sale of any rite, instruction, certificate, or rank (Section 9.4); or any payment that would be an excess benefit transaction under section 4958 of the Internal Revenue Code.

## Article 4. Records of proceedings

The minutes of the Board and of every committee with Board-delegated powers shall contain:

(a) the names of the persons who disclosed or otherwise were found to have a financial interest in connection with an actual or possible conflict of interest; the nature of the financial interest; any action taken to determine whether a conflict of interest was present; and the Board's or committee's decision as to whether a conflict of interest in fact existed; and

(b) the names of the persons who were present for discussions and votes relating to the transaction or arrangement; the content of the discussion, including any alternatives to the proposed transaction or arrangement and the comparable data relied on; and a record of any votes taken in connection with the proceedings.

The Secretary shall keep these minutes permanently with the corporate records under Section 12.2 of the bylaws. If the Church ever files Form 1023, the IRS may request these minutes, and any of them submitted with the application become, once the application is approved, part of the documents open to public inspection.

---

## Article 5. Compensation

(a) A voting director who receives compensation, directly or indirectly, from the Church for services is precluded from voting on matters pertaining to that director's compensation. (Under Section 6.16 of the bylaws, no director receives compensation for service as a director; this subsection applies if a director ever receives compensation for any other service.)

(b) A voting member of any committee whose jurisdiction includes compensation matters and who receives compensation, directly or indirectly, from the Church for services is precluded from voting on matters pertaining to that member's compensation.

(c) No voting director or committee member whose jurisdiction includes compensation matters and who receives compensation, directly or indirectly, from the Church, either individually or collectively, is prohibited from providing information to the Board or any committee regarding compensation.

(d) The Keeper of the Record and any clergy member whose compensation is under consideration shall be absent from the discussion and the vote, and the minutes shall record their absence.

(e) Any compensation the Church ever pays to an interested person shall be approved in advance by the disinterested directors, based on comparable data recorded in the minutes, and shall be reported in the annual congregational report under Section 7.8 of the bylaws.

## Article 6. Annual statements

Each director, officer, the Keeper of the Record, each member of the Council of the Unbroken Eye, each licensed officiant, ordained minister, and elder, and each member of a committee with Board-delegated powers shall sign the annual statement set out below:

(a) at the organizational meeting or on first taking office, and each year thereafter at or before the annual meeting of the Board; and

(b) within thirty days after any change in the facts the statement covers.

Section 11.1 of the bylaws is read to include every person named in this Article.

The Secretary collects the statements and keeps them with the corporate records. The Secretary shall report to the Board at the annual meeting whose statements are on file and whose are missing. A director whose statement is missing is not an independent director for purposes of Section 6.4 of the bylaws until it is signed.

---

## Article 7. Periodic reviews

To ensure that the Church operates in a manner consistent with its religious and charitable purposes and does not engage in activities that could jeopardize its tax-exempt status, the Board shall conduct a review at least once a year. At a minimum the review shall cover:

(a) whether any compensation arrangements and benefits are reasonable, based on competent survey information, and the result of arm's-length bargaining;

(b) whether any arrangements with other organizations or persons, including any arrangement concerning the website, domain, literature, meeting space, supplies, or insurance, conform to the Church's written policies, are properly recorded, reflect reasonable payments for goods and services, further the Church's purposes, and do not result in inurement, impermissible private benefit, or an excess benefit transaction;

(c) whether every person required to sign an annual statement has done so and whether any disclosed interest has been handled under Article 3;

(d) whether the Board has an independent majority under Section 6.4 of the bylaws; and

(e) whether any Circle, clergy member, or other person has collected, solicited, or held money in the Church's name contrary to the bylaws.

The review shall be recorded in the minutes of the annual meeting.

---

## Article 8. Use of outside experts

When conducting the periodic reviews under Article 7, the Church may, but need not, use outside advisors such as an attorney or an accountant. If outside experts are used, their use does not relieve the Board of its responsibility for ensuring that periodic reviews are conducted.

---

## Article 9. Relationship to the Church's doctrine

Nothing in this policy gives the Board, any committee, or any interested person authority over any person's conscience or practice, and nothing in it changes the rule of the faith that rank confers no authority. This policy concerns money, property, and arrangements, which are the corporation's affairs. Where this policy and the religious doctrine of the Church address the same subject, the doctrine controls to the extent Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-180 and the federal and state constitutions require, except that nothing in the doctrine permits inurement, private benefit, or any act outside section 501(c)(3).
