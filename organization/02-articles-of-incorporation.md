The undersigned incorporator, a natural person, acting under Act 1147 of 1993 and Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-202, adopts the following Articles of Incorporation for the purpose of forming a nonprofit religious corporation under the laws of the State of Arkansas.

## Article I. Name

The name of the corporation is **Church of the Angels of Fractum**.

*Drafter's note on the name (delete before filing):* Arkansas requires only that a nonprofit corporation's name be distinguishable upon the records of the Secretary of State from existing, reserved, and registered names (§ 4-33-401, as amended by Act 256 of 2023) and that it not state or imply a purpose other than one the corporation may lawfully pursue (§ 4-33-401(a)). No corporate suffix such as "Inc." or "Church" was found to be required by the 1993 Act [VERIFY with Secretary of State practice]. On November 4, 2026, the Secretary of State's entity search returned zero records for "fractum," "angels of fractum," and "church of the angels," so both this name and the alternative **"Angels of Fractum"** appear available, subject to the Secretary of State's determination at filing. This draft uses "Church of the Angels of Fractum" as the legal name because it states the religious character of the body on its face, which every bank, assessor, and tax reviewer will look for. If the founder prefers the shorter name as the public name, the corporation may register "Angels of Fractum" as a fictitious name after formation (Form DN-18; $25 on paper, $22.50 online) [VERIFY that the fictitious-name procedure applies to nonprofit corporations as the fee sheet implies]. The choice is flagged for the founder and counsel; either name is permitted under the research, and only the name in this Article will appear on the state form.

## Article II. Type of Corporation

This corporation is a **religious corporation** within the meaning of Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-202(a)(2) and § 4-33-140. It is organized primarily and exclusively for religious purposes.

## Article III. Duration

The period of the corporation's duration is perpetual.

## Article IV. Purposes

**Section 4.1. Exempt purposes.** The corporation is organized exclusively for charitable, religious, educational, and scientific purposes under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or corresponding sections of any future federal tax code.

**Section 4.2. Statement of religious purpose.** The Church of the Angels of Fractum is a religious community founded on the conviction that the world is marked by a great brokenness, which we call the Fractum: the countless small broken things, neglected objects, dropped bonds, and unconsidered unkindnesses that people have quietly agreed to ignore, and the inner break, the Fractum Personalis, by which each person sets "me" above "we." We hold that the mending of these things is a sacred act, and that a mended thing is holier than an ignored one. Our doctrine is maintenance; our sacrament is the follow-through. We practice Radical Empathy, understood not as a feeling but as a deliberate alignment of spirit and action, through the Eye of Recognition, which sees the break as it is, and the Hand of Intentional Mending, which repairs what it can reach and asks no thanks for it. Our worship takes the form of acts of repair performed in the world where the world is; of gatherings in which we mend together, read together, confess the break within, and bless one another; of a liturgical year of observances; of rites by which a practitioner's dedication deepens, from Seeker to Angel of the Living Fractum; of the honoring of those who work the register, the fryer, and the mop; and of a private ledger of hours given to the repair of what was not ours. We ask no tithe, we hold no secret teaching, we set no person over another, and we make no claim about God, the gods, or the life to come; our concern is the condition of the world while we are in it and the condition of the person who undertakes to repair it. These beliefs are sincerely held by the people who gather under this name, and the corporation is formed to hold, teach, practice, and hand on this faith.

**Section 4.3. Activities in furtherance of the purposes.** In furtherance of, and limited by, the purposes stated above, the corporation may:

- (a) hold regular religious gatherings and services at one or more established places of worship in Fort Smith, Arkansas, and elsewhere;
- (b) teach and publish the doctrine, Edicts, liturgy, calendar of observances, and other literature of the faith;
- (c) conduct the rites of the faith, including the rites of the Veiled Ascent and the First Mending, and such other rites as its religious doctrine may establish;
- (d) perform and organize acts of sacred mending, meaning works of repair, restoration, and care in public and shared places, and works of service to neighbors and to those in need, as religious practice;
- (e) train, examine, commission, license, or ordain ministers and officiants according to a prescribed course of study established by the corporation;
- (f) form, recognize, and support Circles and other congregational groups of the faith;
- (g) receive, hold, administer, and disburse property of every kind for the purposes stated in these Articles, subject to Article VI; and
- (h) do any other lawful act that a nonprofit religious corporation may do under the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993 and that is consistent with section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code.

## Article V. Religious Doctrine

**Section 5.1. Governing religious documents.** The religious doctrine of the corporation is set forth in its Statement of Faith, its Edicts, and such other writings as the Board of Directors, acting as the corporation's ecclesiastical body, adopts as doctrine in the manner provided in the Bylaws.

**Section 5.2. Doctrine controls.** As provided in Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-180, if religious doctrine governing the affairs of the corporation is inconsistent with any provision of the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993 on the same subject, the religious doctrine shall control to the extent required by the Constitution of the United States or the Constitution of the State of Arkansas, or both.

**Section 5.3. Ecclesiastical questions.** Questions of faith, doctrine, liturgy, rite, rank, and the qualifications and standing of ministers and officiants are ecclesiastical questions reserved to the corporation and decided as the Bylaws provide. Nothing in these Articles shall be construed to submit any such question to any civil authority.

## Article VI. Restrictions Required for Federal Tax Exemption

**Section 6.1. No private inurement.** No part of the net earnings of the corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its directors, officers, ministers, or other private persons, except that the corporation is authorized to pay reasonable compensation for services actually rendered and to make payments and distributions in furtherance of the purposes set forth in Article IV.

**Section 6.2. No substantial lobbying.** No substantial part of the activities of the corporation shall be the carrying on of propaganda, or otherwise attempting to influence legislation.

**Section 6.3. No political campaign intervention.** The corporation shall not participate in, or intervene in (including the publishing or distribution of statements), any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office.

**Section 6.4. Limitation of activities.** Notwithstanding any other provision of these Articles, the corporation shall not carry on any activities not permitted to be carried on (a) by an organization exempt from federal income tax under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or corresponding section of any future federal tax code, or (b) by an organization contributions to which are deductible under section 170(c)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code, or corresponding section of any future federal tax code.

**Section 6.5. No private benefit.** The corporation shall not be operated for the benefit of private interests, and shall not further non-exempt purposes more than insubstantially.

**Section 6.6. Lawful practice.** The practices and rites of the corporation shall not be illegal or contrary to clearly defined public policy.

## Article VII. Members

**Section 7.1. No statutory members.** The corporation shall have **no members** within the meaning of the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993. All corporate powers shall be exercised by or under the authority of the Board of Directors.

**Section 7.2. Congregation distinguished.** Persons who participate in the religious life of the corporation, whether called Seekers, Angels, participants, congregants, or by any rank of the Veiled Ascent, are its congregation for religious purposes. Participation in the congregation does not make a person a member of the corporation under the Act, confers no right to vote on any corporate matter, and creates no interest in the corporation's property. The Bylaws may describe the congregation, Circles, ranks, and rites of the faith without thereby creating statutory membership.

*Drafter's note (delete before filing):* The faith's own writings promise no roster, no register of rank, and no record of individuals. A corporation with no statutory members keeps that promise at the state-law level. The federal church question asks about a congregation or religious membership group that gathers regularly, which is a question of practice and aggregate attendance, not of statutory membership [VERIFY with counsel that the IRS treats these as distinct, and that a no-member structure does not weaken the church showing on Form 1023 Schedule A].

**Section 8.2. Terms.** Directors shall serve for the terms fixed in the Bylaws, which shall not exceed the maximum term permitted by Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-805 (six years). If the Bylaws are silent, the term is one year.

## Article XII. Distribution of Assets on Dissolution

**Section 12.1. Permanent dedication.** The assets of the corporation are irrevocably dedicated to the purposes stated in Article IV. No part of the net earnings or assets of the corporation shall ever inure to the benefit of any director, officer, minister, incorporator, or private person.

**Section 12.2. Distribution.** Upon the dissolution of this organization, assets shall be distributed for one or more exempt purposes within the meaning of section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or corresponding section of any future federal tax code, or shall be distributed to the federal government, or to a state or local government, for a public purpose.

**Section 12.3. Preference.** In carrying out Section 12.2, the Board of Directors shall, after paying or making provision for all liabilities of the corporation, give preference, in this order, to (a) one or more organizations then exempt under section 501(c)(3) whose religious purposes and practices most nearly resemble those of this corporation; then (b) one or more organizations then exempt under section 501(c)(3) that serve persons in need in Sebastian County, Arkansas, such as a food pantry, a shelter, or a public library's supporting organization; then (c) the federal government or a state or local government, for a public purpose. Any such assets not so disposed of shall be disposed of by a court of competent jurisdiction of the county in which the principal office of the corporation is then located, exclusively for such purposes or to such organization or organizations as that court shall determine, which are organized and operated exclusively for such purposes.

**Section 12.4. Religious writings.** The corporation's literature, liturgy, and doctrine shall, on dissolution, be placed in the public domain or licensed for free public use, so that the practice of the faith may continue without the corporation; this is consistent with the faith's tenet that everything it believes is freely available to anyone who wishes to read it.

**Section 13.2. Limitation of director liability.** To the fullest extent permitted by the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993 as it now exists or may later be amended, a director shall not be personally liable to the corporation for monetary damages for any action taken, or failure to take any action, as a director, except to the extent such limitation is not permitted by law [VERIFY the governing section of the Act and its exceptions; the research did not retrieve the Arkansas provision on director liability].

### 1. Which form to use

The Secretary of State offers two versions of the domestic nonprofit articles form, with the same fee:

- **Form NPD-01, "Articles of Incorporation - Domestic Nonprofit" (Rev. 12/24).** A blank form that tracks Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-202(a) item for item. Its optional attachment space is where everything in this draft that the form does not ask for must go.
- **Form NPD-01-501-c-3, "Articles of Incorporation - Domestic Nonprofit, 501(c)(3) Compliant Language."** The same form with the IRS-suggested purpose, inurement, lobbying, political-activity, and dissolution clauses pre-printed (the pre-printed text is dated 10/29/2007 in the research).

Recommendation: file on **NPD-01-501-c-3** so that the IRS-required clauses are on the face of the state form, and attach this document (Articles IV through XVI) as the attachment. ### 6. After filing: the obligations these Articles create

- **Annual report:** due **August 1** of each year, beginning the year after formation (§ 4-33-131; Secretary of State form "Annual Report for Nonprofit Corporation"), filed online, **no fee**. It lists the corporate name, jurisdiction, registered agent, principal office, principal officers, and directors with addresses. Nonprofit corporations do not file the franchise tax report. Missing the annual report by sixty days or more is a ground for administrative dissolution (§ 4-33-1420(5)); reinstatement costs $50.
- **Registered agent:** keep the agent and address current; being without an agent for 120 days, or failing to notify the Secretary of State of a change within 120 days, are also grounds for administrative dissolution (§ 4-33-1420). A change of registered agent carries no fee.
- **EIN:** apply at irs.gov/EIN only after the Articles are accepted, because the EIN is tied to the legal entity.
- **Federal recognition:** as a church, the corporation is excepted from the requirement to apply for recognition (IRC § 508(c)(1)(A)) and from annual Form 990 filing (IRC § 6033(a)(3)(A)), but the packet's roadmap recommends seeking a determination letter on Form 1023 with Schedule A ($600 user fee, filed through Pay.gov as of October 9, 2026) after a period of documented practice. Churches cannot use Form 1023-EZ.
- **Arkansas charitable-solicitation exemption:** if the corporation ever solicits contributions, file Form EX-01 (Exempt Organization Verification) with the Secretary of State's Charities section, with the filed Articles, proof of good standing, and IRS documentation, before soliciting [VERIFY whether the § 4-28-404(1) exemption relieves the corporation of registration under § 4-28-402 or only of reporting under §§ 4-28-403 and -405; the statute text and the Secretary of State's guidance differ].
- **State income tax exemption:** not automatic; after the IRS letter arrives, send DFA's Corporation Income Tax Section a copy of the IRS ruling letter, pages 1 and 2 of Form 1023, and a statement citing Ark. Code Ann. § 26-51-303; without an IRS letter, file Form AR1023CT with the Articles and Bylaws.
