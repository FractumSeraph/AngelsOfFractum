## Part IV. Narrative Description of Your Activities

The organization is a religious community in Fort Smith, Arkansas, whose creed, doctrine, worship, moral code, calendar, ecclesiastical order, membership, discipline, and literature are set out in the attached Statement of Faith and Practice (document 04, cited by section). Percentages are of total time, including volunteer time, and sum to 100.

**1. Regular religious services: the gathering of the Circle (40 percent).** The congregation gathers at [PLACE OF WORSHIP], Fort Smith, on [DAY] at [TIME], following the four-part order of service in Statement § 4: the Mending (one shared act of repair done before anyone speaks, such as returning abandoned shopping carts or clearing litter from a trailhead), the Reading (one Edict or canonical text read aloud, then reflection), the Count (hours given to mending since the last gathering, recorded only as a sum), and the Confession and Benediction. The Hand of the Hour, who leads, rotates every gathering. Conducted by ministers and members; open to the public without charge. This is the faith's corporate worship: the doctrine that repair is sacred (Statement § 2) practiced, read, and confessed together. The Secretary records date, place, Hand of the Hour, reading, act of mending, and aggregate attendance for every service. No funding needed. **[NOT YET TRUE: do not file until the service has been held on its published schedule for the period the Board fixes, with a record for each service.]**

**2. Observances of the liturgical year (10 percent).** The nine fixed and three movable observances in Statement § 6: for example the Vigil of the Changed Batteries (members test smoke detectors and offer to do a neighbor's), the Great Corralling (an hour of returning carts the day after the autumn feast), and the Feast of the Keepers (a day of specific kindness to retail workers). Announced in the published calendar; kept at the gathering or alone, in Fort Smith and wherever members live. No funding needed.

**3. Teaching and publication of the literature (20 percent).** The organization holds, teaches, reads aloud, and publishes at fractum.faith the canonical literature in Statement § 10, and receives new texts through its ecclesiastical body (Bylaws § 2.5). The Keeper of the Record, an uncompensated office held by the founder (Bylaws Article 8), maintains the texts. Everything the faith believes is published openly; no rank unlocks any teaching (Statement § 7). Hosting and domain costs are paid by [FOUNDER] as an in-kind gift reported in Part V. **[NOT YET TRUE: do not file until the gift or license of the domain and writings in minutes § 20 is signed.]**

**4. Rites of the Veiled Ascent and the course of study (15 percent).** The six orders and their rites (Statement § 7): the Ritual of the Clear Path, the Dedication of the Resolute Hand, the Convocation of the Blueprints, and the Ascension of Silent Clarity. The orders are also the prescribed course of study for licensing and ordination (Bylaws § 9.4). Conducted by licensed officiants and ordained ministers, or, as the faith permits, by any Angel of the second order for another, at the place of worship or in a shared public passage. No fee may be charged.

**5. Daily Mending Missions and the Count (10 percent).** The solitary daily devotion of the faith (Statement § 4): one anonymous act of repair, the private Sacrifice Ledger, and the invocations of the Litany of the Small Things. Hours given are reported in the aggregate, without names, to the Keeper of the Record, who publishes the sum. No funding needed.

**6. Administration (5 percent).** Board meetings, state filings, and records, by volunteer directors and officers.

The corporation has no past activities. Before incorporation the faith existed as the website (published since [YEAR]) and at least one gathering of six people described in *The Sacred Ritual of the Sacrifice*; counsel decides whether to describe pre-incorporation activity.
