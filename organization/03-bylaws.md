## How to read this draft (delete before adoption)

These bylaws are written in plain English. They do two jobs at once, and the reader should keep the two apart:

1. **The corporate layer.** The Church of the Angels of Fractum is a corporation formed under the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993 (Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-101 and following). Arkansas law requires it to have a board of at least three directors (§ 4-33-803), to keep records, and to file an annual report with the Secretary of State by August 1 each year (§ 4-33-131). The Board of Directors and the officers hold the corporation's keys: its name, its bank account, its filings, its contracts, and its legal duties. Articles 6 through 8 and 10 through 15 are mostly about this layer.
2. **The religious layer.** The faith these bylaws serve has already written its own rules of practice: *On Forming a Circle*, *What We Are Not*, *The Veiled Ascent*, the Edicts, and the rest of its literature. Those rules say there is no leader for life, that the Hand of the Hour rotates, that Circles collect no money, that anyone may leave at any time, that there are no masters and no secret teachings, and that no records are kept of individuals beyond what the law requires. Articles 4, 5, and 9 carry those rules into the corporation, and Article 2 makes them binding on everything else.

Where the two layers meet, the religious layer wins to the extent the law allows. Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-180 provides that if religious doctrine governing the affairs of a religious corporation is inconsistent with the Act on the same subject, "the religious doctrine shall control to the extent required by the Constitution of the United States or the constitution of this state or both." Section 2.3 relies on that provision.

**On the legal name (delete before adoption).** The companion roadmap in this packet uses **"Church of the Angels of Fractum"** as the working legal name, with **"Angels of Fractum"** as the alternative. On November 4, 2026, the Arkansas Secretary of State's entity search returned no records for "fractum," "angels of fractum," or "church of the angels," so both names appear available pending the Secretary of State's review at filing; Arkansas requires only that a name be distinguishable on the Secretary of State's records (§ 4-33-401, as amended by Act 256 of 2023), and the research found no required suffix such as "Inc." These bylaws use "Church of the Angels of Fractum" because it states the religious purpose on its face. If the founder prefers "Angels of Fractum" as the public name, the corporation may register it as a fictitious name (Form DN-18, $25 on paper or $22.50 online) **[VERIFY that the fictitious-name process applies to nonprofit corporations]**. Whichever name is chosen, these bylaws must match the articles of incorporation as filed.

## Article 1. Name, Offices, and Purposes

### 1.1 Name

The name of the corporation is **Church of the Angels of Fractum** (the "Church"). In these bylaws the words "the Church" and "the corporation" mean the same legal entity. The community of practice that the corporation serves may continue to call itself "the Angels of Fractum."

### 1.2 Principal office

The principal office of the Church is at [PRINCIPAL OFFICE STREET ADDRESS], Fort Smith, Sebastian County, Arkansas. The Board of Directors may move the principal office to another place in Arkansas by resolution, and the Secretary shall report the change on the next annual report to the Secretary of State.

### 1.3 Registered agent and registered office

The Church shall continuously maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in Arkansas, as the articles of incorporation and Arkansas law require. The initial registered agent is [REGISTERED AGENT NAME] at [REGISTERED AGENT ADDRESS]. The Board may change the registered agent by resolution; there is no filing fee for the change. A corporation that is without a registered agent for 120 days is subject to administrative dissolution (§ 4-33-1420), so the Secretary shall confirm the agent's address every year before the annual report is filed.

### 1.4 Purposes

The Church is organized and shall be operated exclusively for charitable, religious, educational, and scientific purposes under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or corresponding sections of any future federal tax code, as stated in Article IV of its articles of incorporation. Within those purposes, and without limiting them, the Church exists:

(a) to practice, teach, and keep the faith of the Angels of Fractum, whose single article of belief is that a mended thing is holier than an ignored one, and whose practice is the sacred repair of small broken things;

(b) to hold regular religious services at an established place of worship in Fort Smith, Arkansas, and wherever else a Circle of the Church gathers;

(c) to perform the rites of the Veiled Ascent and the observances of the Church's liturgical calendar;

(d) to publish, preserve, and teach the literature of the faith, including the Statement of Faith, the Edicts, the Daily Mending Missions, the Litany, and the Calendar of Holy Days and Observances;

(e) to organize and carry out acts of repair and service to the community, such as returning abandoned shopping carts, cleaning public places, mending what neighbors cannot mend alone, and honoring the people who do the ordinary work of the world; and

(f) to do anything else lawful that furthers these purposes and is consistent with section 501(c)(3).

### 1.5 Limits that come with tax exemption

No part of the net earnings of the Church shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its directors, officers, clergy, members, or other private persons, except that the Church may pay reasonable compensation for services actually rendered and may make payments and distributions in furtherance of its purposes. No substantial part of the Church's activities shall consist of attempting to influence legislation. The Church shall not participate in, or intervene in (including by publishing or distributing statements), any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office. The Church shall not carry on any activity not permitted to be carried on by an organization exempt from federal income tax under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or by an organization contributions to which are deductible under section 170(c)(2) of that Code.

## Article 2. Doctrine and the Governing Texts

### 2.1 The governing texts

The religious doctrine of the Church consists of the following, as adopted by the Board at its organizational meeting and as amended under Section 2.5 (together, the "Governing Texts"):

(a) **The Statement of Faith and Practice** (the document so titled in this packet; in these bylaws "the Statement of Faith" means that document), sections 1 through 4, 6, 7, and 10 of which are the Church's creed, form of worship, calendar, ecclesiastical order, and canon of literature;

(b) **The Code of Doctrine and Discipline**, which consists of sections 5 (the ten Edicts), 8 (Membership), and 9 (Discipline) of the Statement of Faith and Practice, together with the page titled *What We Are Not*, the rules of the Sacrifice Ledger in the page so titled, the rules of a Circle in *On Forming a Circle* (including "What a Circle does not do" and "Signs a Circle has gone wrong"), and any further rules of conduct the Church adopts under Section 2.5;

(c) **The Rites of the Veiled Ascent**, meaning the ranks and rites described in *The Veiled Ascent* and the written liturgies of those rites;

(d) **The Liturgy**, meaning *The Litany of the Small Things*, the Daily Mending Missions, and the order of a gathering described in *On Forming a Circle*; and

(e) **The Calendar of Holy Days and Observances**.

Each text named in this section is a Governing Text only from the date it is published at fractum.faith; the Secretary shall record that date for each in the literature archive under Section 12.5, and the Board shall not adopt these bylaws until every text named in this section has been published.

### 2.2 The unchangeable commitments

The following commitments are part of the Church's doctrine, and these bylaws do not permit the Board, the officers, the clergy, or any Circle to abandon them. They may be strengthened by amendment; they may not be weakened:

(a) **No tithe, no dues, no price of participation.** The Church does not condition participation, membership, rank, instruction, or any rite on the payment of money. No one is asked for money in the Church's name. The Church may accept gifts that are freely given and unsolicited, as Article 10 provides, and may state publicly how such a gift may be made and how gifts were spent; that is not solicitation.

(b) **No isolation.** No one acting for the Church may instruct a person to cut off a family member, friend, or anyone else who loves them.

(c) **No secret teachings.** Everything the Church believes is published and free to read. No rank unlocks a doctrine.

(d) **No masters.** No director, officer, clergy member, rank, or founder may command any member or participant in matters of belief or practice. Rank describes practice, not power.

(e) **Anyone may leave.** Any member, participant, clergy member, or Circle may withdraw from the Church at any time, for any reason, without explanation, and without penalty.

(f) **No records of individuals beyond what the law or the Church's own stated needs require.** See Article 12.

(g) **The Hand mends; it does not scold.** The Church does not confront, lecture, or shame. No activity of the Church requires anyone to raise their voice.

### 2.3 Religious doctrine controls

The Church is a religious corporation within the meaning of Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-140 and § 4-33-202(a)(2). The Governing Texts are the religious doctrine governing the affairs of the Church. To the extent any provision of the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993, or of these bylaws, is inconsistent with the Governing Texts on the same subject, the Governing Texts control to the full extent permitted by Ark. Code Ann. § 4-33-180 and by the Constitution of the United States and the Constitution of the State of Arkansas. Nothing in this section limits Section 1.5 or any other provision required for federal tax exemption.

### 2.4 Matters on which the Church takes no position

The Church takes no position on the existence or nature of God or gods, on the afterlife, or on the proper day of rest. Membership in the Church does not require, and does not forbid, membership in any other religious body. Members and participants may pray or not pray as they choose.

### 2.5 Amending doctrine

(a) **Who may propose.** Any member of the Church, any Circle, any clergy member, the Keeper of the Record, or any director may propose a change to a Governing Text by delivering the proposed text in writing to the Secretary.

(b) **The Council's role.** Once a Council of the Unbroken Eye exists under Section 9.6, no amendment to the Statement of Faith or to the Code of Doctrine and Discipline takes effect unless the Council has approved it. Until the Council exists, the clergy of the Church then holding rank IV or above (if any) shall be consulted and their written views circulated with the proposal.

(c) **Notice to the congregation.** The proposed text shall be published to the members and to every recognized Circle at least thirty days before the Board votes, and shall be read aloud at a regular service at the established place of worship during that period.

(d) **The vote.** The amendment takes effect when approved by two-thirds of the directors then in office at a meeting held after the notice period, and when published.

(e) **Limits.** No amendment may remove or weaken a commitment in Section 2.2, and no amendment may bring the Church outside section 501(c)(3). Changes to the Liturgy, the Daily Mending Missions, or the Calendar of Holy Days and Observances that add an observance, mission, invocation, or rite without altering doctrine may be made by the Board on the recommendation of the Keeper of the Record and the Council (or the consulted clergy) by a simple majority, with the same notice.

## Article 3. Members of the Church

### 3.1 Why this article exists, and what it reconciles

The faith's own literature promises that "there is no form," that "there is no roster of Angels, no register of who holds which rank," and that the Sacrifice Ledger is private. The Internal Revenue Service, in deciding whether an organization is a church, looks for a "regular congregation" and asks a church how many members it has and how one becomes a member (Form 1023, Schedule A, lines 9a through 9c, per the IRS instructions). Arkansas law also requires the articles of incorporation to say whether the corporation will have members (§ 4-33-202(a)(5)).

These bylaws reconcile the two as follows:

(a) **No corporate members.** The articles of incorporation state that the Church has no members within the meaning of the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993. No one has a vote on corporate matters by reason of being a member of the Church, and no "member" under this Article has any right, title, or interest in the Church's property. The Board of Directors is the Church's governing body **[VERIFY with counsel that a no-member religious corporation whose bylaws define a religious membership category works under the Act and does not undercut the Schedule A membership showing]**.

(b) **Members of the Church are a religious category.** The word "member" in these bylaws means a person who belongs to the congregation in the religious sense described in Section 3.2. It does not mean a corporate member.

(c) **The register is opt-in and minimal.** The Church keeps one register of members, and only of people who have asked to be listed. It records what Section 12.3 permits and nothing else. No one is listed without their consent, and anyone listed may be removed on request.

(d) **There is still no roster of ranks.** The Church keeps no register of who holds which rank of the Veiled Ascent, with two exceptions: the register of clergy under Section 9.9, kept because the Church must be able to say who may perform its rites in its name, and the two notice-receivers of each recognized Circle under Section 4.3(b). A person's rank below the clergy ranks is their own business, as it always was.

(e) **The Sacrifice Ledger stays private.** The Church never collects, inspects, copies, or asks to see any person's Sacrifice Ledger. The Count at a gathering is recorded as a sum and never by name.

### 3.2 Who is a member

A person is a member of the Church when all three of the following are true:

(a) **They hold the faith.** They affirm the Statement of Faith, which means at minimum the single article of belief that a mended thing is holier than an ignored one and the three truths every Angel carries (mend anonymously when you can; empathy, never pity; the Fractum is also within).

(b) **They have entered the practice.** They have performed a First Mending and have taken the Sacred Vow of Vigilance at the Ritual of the Clear Path (the rite by which a Seeker becomes a Mender-in-Training), either before a licensed officiant or ordained minister of the Church with at least one witness, or privately and later affirmed before a licensed officiant or ordained minister (or, until the first licensed officiant exists, as Section 9.7(e) provides).

(c) **They have asked to be counted.** They have asked, in writing or in the presence of a licensed officiant or ordained minister and the Secretary (or the Secretary's designee), to be entered in the register of members.

A person who meets (a) and (b) but has not asked to be counted is a **participant** of the Church. A person who is exploring the faith and has not yet taken the Vow is a **Seeker**. Participants and Seekers are welcome at every service, rite, and gathering on the same terms as members, and the Church makes no distinction among them except where these bylaws say so (for example, in counting attendance under Section 12.4 and in eligibility for the clergy ranks under Article 9).

### 3.3 No age, residence, or other barrier

Membership is open to any person who meets Section 3.2, without regard to age, race, color, national origin, sex, disability, or any other characteristic protected by law, and without regard to membership in any other religious body. A person under eighteen may be a member with a parent's or guardian's knowledge **[VERIFY whether any Arkansas rule affects minors' participation or records]**.

### 3.4 No dues, no fees, no obligation

No dues, fees, tithes, assessments, or contributions of any kind are required of members, participants, or Seekers at any time. Nothing in the Church's practice, rank, or rites may be conditioned on a gift.

### 3.5 Rights of members

Members have the following rights, which no officer, director, clergy member, or Circle may abridge:

(a) to attend every regular service, rite, and gathering of the Church and of any recognized Circle;

(b) to receive, free of charge, the Governing Texts and all other published literature of the Church;

(c) to serve as Hand of the Hour in any Circle they attend, in rotation with everyone else;

(d) to propose amendments to doctrine under Section 2.5, and to receive notice of proposed amendments;

(e) to be considered for the clergy ranks under Article 9 on the same terms as every other member;

(f) to attend the annual congregational report under Section 7.8;

(g) to see the register entry that concerns them and to have it corrected or removed; and

(h) to leave.

### 3.6 Withdrawal

Any member may withdraw by telling the Secretary, any officer, any clergy member, or any director, in any form, or simply by ceasing to practice. A member who has withdrawn remains an Angel in the faith's own sense ("stop practicing tomorrow and no one will call"), and may ask to be entered in the register again at any time. On withdrawal the Secretary shall mark the register entry withdrawn with the date and, if the person asks, shall remove the entry entirely.

### 3.7 Removal

The Church does not expel people for failing to mend, for breaking an Edict, or for doubting. A member may be removed from the register only by a two-thirds vote of the directors then in office, and only on one of the following grounds: (a) the member has, in the Church's name, solicited money, isolated a person from those who love them, claimed authority over another member, or taught as the Church's doctrine something that is not; (b) the member has engaged in conduct that endangers the safety of others at Church activities; or (c) the member has been convicted of, or admits to, conduct that would expose the Church to legal liability if the member continued to act for it. Before any vote the member shall receive written notice of the grounds and an opportunity to respond, in person or in writing, to the Board. A person removed from the register may still attend public services unless the Board, for safety, resolves otherwise.

## Article 4. Circles as Congregations

### 4.1 What a Circle is

A Circle is two or more members or participants of the Church who gather, on a regular rhythm, to mend together, to read together, and to add their hours together, following the order of a gathering in *On Forming a Circle*: the Mending, the Reading, the Count, and the Confession and the Benediction. A Circle is the Church's congregation at the place where it meets.

### 4.2 The Established Congregation

The Church maintains at least one Circle that meets at a fixed, published day, time, and place in Fort Smith, Arkansas, as the Church's established place of worship and regular religious service (the "Established Congregation"). The Established Congregation initially meets on [REGULAR SERVICE DAY AND TIME] at [ESTABLISHED PLACE OF WORSHIP], with the Mending performed at [DESIGNATED OUTER SANCTUM OR MENDING SITE]. The Board fixes and publishes the day, time, and place, and shall not suspend the regular service except for safety or weather, or for a period of more than four consecutive weeks without a resolution stating the reason. A service at which only two people are present is still a service, and is still recorded.

### 4.3 Recognition of a Circle

(a) **Why recognition exists.** Any two Angels anywhere may form a Circle as a matter of faith, without asking anyone. Recognition under this section is a corporate act: it means the Church lists the Circle as one of its congregations, covers its Mending activities under the Church's insurance (if any), counts its attendance in the Church's records, and permits it to use the Church's name. A Circle that does not want these things need not seek recognition and loses nothing in the faith by declining.

(b) **How a Circle is recognized.** A Circle may ask to be recognized by delivering to the Secretary a one-page request stating: the place and the regular day and time of its gatherings; the names of two members of the Church who attend it and who will each receive notices for it; its agreement to this Article 4 and the Code of Doctrine and Discipline; and whether it will be known by a name. The Board recognizes a Circle by a simple majority. The Board may decline recognition only if the request is incomplete, if the Circle's stated practice conflicts with the Governing Texts, or if the Church cannot responsibly insure or record its activities; the Board shall state its reason in writing.

(c) **Continuing recognition.** A recognized Circle keeps its recognition so long as it meets on a regular published rhythm, reports its attendance counts under Section 12.4, and keeps the rules in Section 4.5. Recognition lapses automatically if the Circle has not met for six months; the Secretary shall note the lapse and no one need be told why.

### 4.4 Autonomy of a Circle

A recognized Circle governs its own gatherings. In particular:

(a) **It has no leader.** It has a Hand of the Hour, who for one gathering chooses the Reading, names the Mending, and speaks the benediction. The Hand of the Hour rotates every gathering, without exception. No person may hold it twice in a row, and any person may decline it. If one person has held the hour for three gatherings, the Circle has a break in it, and any member of the Circle may report that to the Secretary or any director.

(b) **Rank confers nothing in a Circle.** An Angel of the Living Fractum holds the hour exactly as often as a Seeker and takes the same turn at the broom. A licensed officiant or ordained minister attending a Circle is a member of that Circle like any other, except when performing a rite under Article 9.

(c) **It chooses its own Mendings, Readings, and place**, within the Code of Doctrine and Discipline and the safety rules in Section 4.6.

(d) **No officer, director, or clergy member may direct a Circle's gathering**, override its Hand of the Hour, or require it to adopt any practice not in the Governing Texts.

(e) **It may end.** A Circle may disband at any time by the simple fact of ceasing to meet, or by the Unthreading. Any member may leave a Circle at any time, for any reason, without explanation, and remains a member of the Church.

### 4.5 What a Circle may not do

A recognized Circle, and anyone acting in the name of a Circle or of the Church, may not:

(a) **collect, hold, solicit, or handle money or property** of any kind, for any purpose, including for robes, snacks, supplies, or a good cause. A Circle has no treasury. If a Circle needs supplies (gloves, bags, batteries, bulbs), a member asks the Treasurer, who may purchase them from the Church's funds under the budget, or members bring their own without reimbursement from the Circle;

(b) **open a bank account, incur a debt, sign a contract, lease a space, or hold title to property** in the name of the Circle or the Church; only the Church, by its authorized officers, may do these things;

(c) **speak for the Church** to the press, to government, or to the public on matters of doctrine, or claim that any teaching not in the Governing Texts is the Church's teaching;

(d) **recruit by pressure.** A Circle tells people who ask. It does not solicit, advertise for converts, or follow up with a person who has not asked;

(e) **confront anyone.** A Circle mends; it does not scold, honk, lecture, or correct strangers;

(f) **perform for an audience.** A Circle does not photograph its work for publication, post its Count, or wear matching identification, though robes at the corral break no Edict;

(g) **keep the Count by name**, or keep any list of individuals except that the two notice-receivers under Section 4.3(b) are known to the Secretary;

(h) **isolate anyone**, or tell any person to stop seeing anyone;

(i) **participate or intervene in any political campaign** for or against a candidate for public office, or devote any substantial activity to influencing legislation; or

(j) **ordain, license, or confer any clergy rank**; only the Church may do that under Article 9. A Circle may perform the Ritual of the Clear Path with any officiant the faith permits, and the Dedication of the Resolute Hand only as Section 9.7(e) or Section 9.8 provides; such rites are entered in the register of members only as Section 3.2 and Section 9.4 provide.

### 4.6 Safety

Every Mending performed by a Circle shall be small, lawful, and within reach. No Circle shall perform electrical work beyond replacing a bulb or a battery, work on a roof or a ladder above [MAXIMUM LADDER HEIGHT PER SAFETY POLICY], enter private property without the owner's permission, handle hazardous material without proper gloves and equipment, or do anything the Church's written safety policy forbids. A Circle asked to leave a place leaves, and thanks the person who asked. The Board shall adopt a written safety policy and a volunteer acknowledgment or waiver before any organized Mending is held in the Church's name, and shall obtain general liability and volunteer accident insurance if the Board determines it is reasonably available **[VERIFY insurance scope and waiver language with counsel and a broker]**.

### 4.7 When a Circle goes wrong

If any member of the Church believes a recognized Circle shows any sign listed under "Signs a Circle has gone wrong" in *On Forming a Circle* (one person always holds the hour; money has appeared; someone has been told to stop seeing someone; the Mending has become optional and the talking has not; the Count is kept by name; anyone has been called the founder), the member may leave without ceremony and may report it to the Secretary or any director. The Board shall look into it promptly, shall hear from the Circle, and may withdraw recognition by a majority vote. Withdrawal of recognition is not a judgment on any person's faith; it means the Church no longer lists, insures, or records that gathering.

## Article 5. The Hand of the Hour and the Order of Service

### 5.1 The regular service

The regular religious service of the Church is the four-part gathering described in *On Forming a Circle*, which the Board has adopted as the Church's order of service: the Mending (one shared act of repair, performed first and before anyone speaks); the Reading (one Edict, Mission, or text read aloud by someone other than the person who chose it, followed by brief talk); the Count (each person who wishes names the hours given since the last gathering, and the Hand of the Hour adds them, recording the sum only); and the Confession and the Benediction (any person who wishes names one Fractum Personalis and one stitch, and the Hand of the Hour speaks a benediction, ending with the sacred threefold phrase). The service may be completed in forty minutes. No one is required to speak at any part of it.

### 5.2 The Hand of the Hour at the Established Congregation

At the Established Congregation, the Hand of the Hour rotates every service under Section 4.4(a). The Secretary, or a person the Secretary designates who is present, records what Section 12.4 requires. The Hand of the Hour has no authority beyond that one service and is not an officer of the corporation.

### 5.3 Observances

The Church keeps the Calendar of Holy Days and Observances as its liturgical year. Observances may be kept alone or by a Circle, and none requires a gathering, but the Established Congregation shall mark each fixed observance in some way at the regular service nearest it, and the Secretary shall record that it did.

### 5.4 Rites

The rites of the Veiled Ascent are performed as Article 9 provides. Weddings, funerals, blessings of homes, and other rites that the Church has not yet written may be composed under Section 2.5(e) and performed by ordained ministers **[VERIFY Arkansas requirements for who may solemnize a marriage before any minister of the Church does so]**.

## Article 6. Board of Directors

### 6.1 Powers

The affairs of the Church are managed by, and under the direction of, the Board of Directors (the "Board"). The Board holds every corporate power the Church has, subject to the articles of incorporation, these bylaws, and the Governing Texts. The Board does not hold, and these bylaws do not give it, authority over any person's conscience, belief, or practice.

### 6.2 Number

The Board consists of not fewer than three nor more than nine directors. Within those limits the Board fixes its own number by resolution. The initial Board consists of [NUMBER OF INITIAL DIRECTORS] directors: [DIRECTOR 1], [DIRECTOR 2], [DIRECTOR 3], [DIRECTOR 4, if any], [DIRECTOR 5, if any]. The Board shall never reduce its number below three (§ 4-33-803(b)), and no reduction shortens the term of a sitting director.

### 6.3 Qualifications

Each director must be an individual at least eighteen years old and a member of the Church under Section 3.2 for at least [SIX] months before election, except that this membership period does not apply to the initial directors or to anyone elected during the Church's first two years. Directors need not reside in Arkansas **[VERIFY: the research found no residency requirement for directors in the Act]**.

### 6.4 Independent majority

At all times a majority of the directors then in office shall be **independent directors**. A director is independent if, during the current year and the preceding year, all of the following are true:

(a) the director has not been compensated by the Church as an officer, employee, or contractor (reimbursement of documented expenses under Section 10.5 does not count as compensation);

(b) the director is not related by family, as defined in Section 2.3 of the Conflict of Interest Policy, to any other director, to any officer, or to the Keeper of the Record;

(c) the director does not have a business relationship with any other director, officer, or the Keeper of the Record, meaning co-ownership of a business, employment of one by the other, or any transaction between them worth more than $10,000 in a year **[VERIFY the $10,000 business-relationship threshold against the current Form 990 instructions]**;

(d) neither the director nor a family member has been party to any transaction with the Church, other than the ordinary receipt of the Church's religious services, an unconditional gift to the Church, or reimbursement of documented expenses under Section 10.5; and

(e) the director has signed the current annual statement under the Conflict of Interest Policy.

This standard is drawn from, and is stricter than, the IRS's definition of an "independent voting member" for Form 990 reporting and from the questions the IRS asks on Form 1023, Part V, and is adopted here as the Church's own rule even though a church is not required to file Form 990. If the Board at any time lacks an independent majority, it shall take no action other than to fill vacancies with independent directors and to conduct business the law requires, until the majority is restored.

### 6.5 Terms and staggering

(a) Directors serve terms of two years. No term may exceed six years, and if these bylaws were ever silent the term would be one year (§ 4-33-805).

(b) The Board is divided into two classes as nearly equal in number as possible. At the organizational meeting the initial directors shall draw lots or agree which of them will serve an initial term of one year (Class A) and which an initial term of two years (Class B), so that roughly half the Board stands for election each year thereafter.

(c) A director's term ends at the annual meeting at which it expires, when a successor is elected and qualifies, or on earlier death, resignation, or removal.

(d) **Term limits.** No director may serve more than three consecutive full terms (six years). A director who has served three consecutive terms is eligible again after at least one full year off the Board. The faith's own rule is the model: if one person has held the hour for three gatherings, the others should notice.

### 6.6 Election

Because the Church has no corporate members, directors are elected by the Board. At each annual meeting the Board elects directors to fill the seats of the class whose terms expire, by a majority of the directors then in office (not merely of those present). Nominations may be made by any director, by the Keeper of the Record, by the Council of the Unbroken Eye once it exists, or by any recognized Circle, and shall be delivered to the Secretary at least fourteen days before the annual meeting. The Secretary shall publish the list of nominees to the members with the notice of the annual meeting, and the Board shall hear any member who wishes to speak to a nomination before voting **[VERIFY the Act's rules for electing directors of a corporation without members]**.

### 6.7 Resignation

A director may resign at any time by delivering written notice (including by electronic transmission) to the President or the Secretary. The resignation takes effect when delivered unless it states a later date.

### 6.8 Removal

A director may be removed, with or without cause, by a two-thirds vote of the directors then in office other than the director in question, at a meeting called for that purpose with at least fourteen days' written notice to every director stating that removal will be considered. The director in question may speak before the vote. The same person may not hold the offices of President and Treasurer at the same time. The Treasurer and the Secretary may not be related to each other by family, as defined in Section 2.3 of the Conflict of Interest Policy. The Keeper of the Record may not serve as President or Treasurer while holding that office.

### 7.4 The President

The President presides at meetings of the Board; sees that the Board's resolutions are carried out; signs, with the Secretary or Treasurer when a second signature is required, contracts and instruments the Board has authorized; serves as the Church's contact with the Secretary of State, the Internal Revenue Service, the Department of Finance and Administration, and other agencies unless the Board designates someone else; and gives the pre-election reminder required by Section 11.3. The Secretary keeps the minutes of every meeting of the Board and its committees; gives every notice these bylaws require; is custodian of the corporate records, the register of members, the register of clergy, the attendance records, the literature archive, and the dated history of the Church; files the annual report with the Arkansas Secretary of State by August 1 each year; keeps the current addresses of the registered agent and principal office accurate; certifies copies of corporate documents; and performs the other duties the Board assigns. ### 7.7 Removal, resignation, and vacancies

Any officer may be removed by the Board, with or without cause, by a majority of the directors then in office, without prejudice to any contract rights (of which the Church expects there to be none). An officer may resign by written notice to the President or the Secretary. The Board fills a vacancy in any office for the unexpired term.

### 7.8 The annual congregational report

Each year, within sixty days after the annual meeting, the officers shall give a plain-language report to the members at a regular service of the Established Congregation and in writing to every recognized Circle: the number of services held and the average attendance; the number of members in the register; the number of persons licensed or ordained; the Church's receipts, expenses, and bank balance; the compliance filings made; and the matters the Board expects to decide in the coming year. The report is not a meeting of members and no vote is taken; it exists because a faith with no secret teachings should have no secret finances.

## Article 8. The Keeper of the Record

### 8.1 The office

The Church recognizes one ecclesiastical office, distinct from the Board and from the corporate officers, called the **Keeper of the Record**. The Keeper of the Record is the one who writes things down: the custodian of the faith's literature, the author or editor of its published texts, and the keeper of the Count (the sum of hours voluntarily given by Angels to acts of mending, recorded as a sum and never by name).

The author of the faith's literature, who writes under the name "Seraph" and whose legal name is [FOUNDER'S FULL LEGAL NAME], is the first Keeper of the Record, as the faith's literature already describes. The faith's literature warns against any Circle that forms around a "founder"; where these bylaws use that word (Sections 8.6 and 9.7(d)) it identifies the incorporator for legal purposes only and confers nothing.

The Keeper of the Record:

(a) maintains the literature of the faith and the archive required by Section 12.5, with the Secretary;

(b) prepares, or supervises the preparation of, new texts for consideration under Section 2.5, and edits published texts for clarity without changing their doctrine;

(d) keeps the dated history of the faith;

(e) serves on the Council of the Unbroken Eye once it exists, as Section 9.6 provides; and

(f) attends meetings of the Board when invited and reports on the state of the literature, the Count, and the practice.

### 8.4 What the Keeper does not have

The Keeper of the Record, by virtue of that office:

(a) has no vote on the Board (though the Keeper may separately be elected a director, subject to Section 6.4's independent-majority rule and Section 7.3);

(c) has no authority to amend doctrine alone, to ordain or license anyone alone, to recognize or dissolve a Circle, or to admit or remove a member;

(d) has no authority to command, instruct, or discipline any member, participant, clergy member, or Circle; and

(e) receives no compensation for the office. Any compensation for other services shall be approved under the Conflict of Interest Policy with the Keeper absent from the vote, and shall be reported in the annual congregational report.

### 8.5 Term and succession

## Article 9. Clergy and Ordination

### 9.1 Purpose of this Article

The faith already has a ladder of practice, the Veiled Ascent, with six ranks and written rites. The IRS, in deciding whether a religious organization is a church, looks among other things for "ordained ministers ministering to the congregation" and "ordained ministers selected after completing prescribed courses of study," and the Form 1023 instructions for Schedule A say that a prescribed course of study "doesn't include self-ordination or paying a fee for an ordination certificate without completing a course of study." This Article maps the ranks of the Veiled Ascent onto recognized religious roles, says who may confer them, and keeps the record the IRS and Arkansas law may ask for, while preserving the faith's rule that rank describes practice and not power. This Article is the Church's Ordination Policy. Where any other document of the Church refers to the "Ordination Policy," the "Register of Ministers," or the "ordination register," it means this Article and the register of clergy under Section 9.9. The Church licenses officiants and ordains ministers; it does not commission anyone.

### 9.2 Rank confers no authority

**No rank of the Veiled Ascent, and no office or title under this Article, confers any authority over any member, participant, Seeker, Circle, or other person.** An Angel of the Living Fractum cannot tell a Seeker what to do, only show them where the corral is. Clergy of the Church perform rites, conduct services, teach the published texts, and keep the registers this Article requires. They do not command, discipline, or rule. Any clergy member who claims authority over another person's conscience or practice in the Church's name has misread every page of the faith, and Section 9.11 applies.

### 9.4 The prescribed course of study

A person may be licensed or ordained only after completing the Church's prescribed course of study and being examined on it. The course of study is the Veiled Ascent itself, administered and recorded, and consists at least of the following, each with a date and a witness or examiner entered in the register of clergy:

(a) **For rank II (membership):** reading the core text *The Angels of Fractum* and the foundational Edicts, and knowing their sense; a First Mending; the private identification of one Fractum Personalis; and the Ritual of the Clear Path with the Sacred Vow of Vigilance.

(b) **For rank III:** regular completion of the Foundational Mending Missions (the Cart, the Pause, and the Minor Breach); the 30-Day Vigil of Intentional Action, documented in the candidate's own Ledger and attested by the candidate (the Ledger itself is never inspected); a Repair of Omission; and the Dedication of the Resolute Hand.

(c) **For licensing as an officiant (rank IV):** at least [TWELVE] months at rank III; the mentoring of at least two Seekers to rank III, attested by those two persons; the creation and completion of a Blueprint of Repair; a written examination, set by the Council (or, until the Council exists, by two independent directors who are not candidates), on the Statement of Faith, the Code of Doctrine and Discipline, the Liturgy, the rites the officiant will perform, the safety policy, the Church's rules on money and on records, and the limits on political activity in Section 11.3; and the Convocation of the Blueprints.

(d) **For ordination (rank V):** at least [TWENTY-FOUR] months as a licensed officiant; a Treatise on Mending, delivered in writing and read to the Established Congregation; affirmation by the Council (or, until it exists, by the examiners described in (c)) that the candidate has shown the enduring Inner Alignment the Veiled Ascent describes; and the 24-Hour Vigil of Vision followed by the Ascension of Silent Clarity.

(e) **For elder (rank VI):** ordination; either authorship of a significant new text adopted under Section 2.5 or recognition by the Council as a foundational member of the faith; and the rite to be composed under Section 9.3.

No fee may be charged for instruction, examination, licensing, or ordination. No one may be licensed or ordained for having paid anything, for being related to anyone, or for having founded anything. No one may confer a rank on themselves. Rites a Seeker performs alone remain valid as acts of faith; they do not by themselves confer a role under this Article.

### 9.5 Who may confer

(a) **Licensing (rank IV)** is conferred by the Council of the Unbroken Eye, with the concurrence of the Board, after the candidate has completed Section 9.4(c). The rite is the Convocation of the Blueprints, officiated by an ordained minister or elder.

(b) **Ordination (rank V)** is conferred by the Council, with the concurrence of the Board, after Section 9.4(d). The rite is the Ascension of Silent Clarity, officiated by an elder or, if none exists, by the Council together.

(c) **Elder (rank VI)** is conferred by the Council, with the concurrence of the Board.

(d) The Board's concurrence under this section is limited to confirming that the course of study was completed and recorded, that no fee was paid, that the candidate is not disqualified under Section 9.11, and that the Church's legal obligations are met. The Board does not judge the candidate's Inner Alignment, which is a religious judgment reserved to the Council.

### 9.6 The Council of the Unbroken Eye

(a) The Council of the Unbroken Eye (the "Council") is the Church's ecclesiastical body. It consists of every ordained minister and elder of the Church in good standing, together with the Keeper of the Record. It comes into existence when at least three persons qualify to sit on it.

(b) The Council has these functions and no others: to examine and approve candidates for licensing, ordination, and elder; to approve amendments to the Statement of Faith and the Code of Doctrine and Discipline under Section 2.5; to recommend new liturgy, missions, observances, and rites; to advise the Board on any question of doctrine or practice; to hear concerns under Section 9.11; and to nominate directors.

(c) The Council has no authority over the Church's money, property, contracts, filings, or employees, and no authority over any person's conscience. It acts by a majority of its members at a meeting of which every member has had seven days' notice, and keeps minutes, which the Secretary files with the corporate records. No member of the Council may sit on their own case.

(d) The Council has no chair. Its meetings are presided over by its members in rotation, in the manner of the Hand of the Hour.

### 9.7 Transition until the Council exists

The faith has written rites but has never ordained anyone. Until the Council exists:

(a) The Board, acting through at least two independent directors who are not themselves candidates and who are not related to any candidate, serves as the examining body under Section 9.4 and as the conferring body under Section 9.5. The Keeper of the Record advises but does not vote, and is absent from any vote concerning the Keeper's own licensing or ordination.

(b) The first licensed officiants shall be persons who have completed Section 9.4(a) through (c) with the record described there. Because no one yet holds rank IV or above to officiate the Convocation of the Blueprints, the first Convocation shall be officiated by the Hand of the Hour of the Established Congregation for that service, with the examining directors as witnesses, and the record shall say so.

(c) The time periods in Section 9.4(c) and (d) may be shortened for the first candidates by a two-thirds vote of the directors then in office, but never below six months at rank III for licensing and twelve months as a licensed officiant for ordination, and never below the substantive requirements (mentoring, Blueprint, examination, Treatise, Vigil).

(d) The founder is subject to this Article like everyone else. The founder may be licensed or ordained only after completing and recording the course of study, examined by independent directors with the founder absent, and the register shall show it. No person, including the founder, is licensed or ordained at the organizational meeting or by any resolution that recites authorship of the course of study in place of its completion, and any such resolution is of no effect under this Article. Until a licensed officiant exists, the Church describes itself to any agency as a church whose rites are officiated as Section 9.7(e) and Section 9.8 provide, and not as a church with ordained ministers.

(e) Until the first licensed officiant is entered in the register of clergy: (1) the Sacred Vow of Vigilance under Section 3.2(b) may be taken before, or later affirmed before, any director or the Secretary with one other witness, and the register of members shall say so; (2) the Dedication of the Resolute Hand may be officiated by any member who has completed Section 9.4(a), with a director or the Secretary present as witness, and the record shall say so; and (3) the Board shall record, for every person who has asked to be examined, the date each element of Section 9.4(a) and (b) was completed and who witnessed it.

### 9.8 Officiants at rites

For purposes of the Church's records, a rite is performed "by the Church" when a licensed officiant, ordained minister, or elder officiates and at least one other member witnesses. Nothing in these bylaws prevents a rite from being performed as the faith's own literature allows, including by a Seeker alone with no witness but the aisle, or by any Angel of rank II or above for another; such a rite is complete as an act of faith. It is entered in the register of members or the register of clergy only when affirmed before a licensed officiant or ordained minister as Section 3.2(b) and Section 9.4 provide, or, until the first licensed officiant exists, as Section 9.7(e) provides.

### 9.9 The register of clergy

The Secretary keeps a register of clergy showing, for each licensed officiant, ordained minister, and elder: the person's name; the date each element of the course of study was completed and who examined or witnessed it; the date and form of licensing, ordination, or elevation; who conferred it; the certificate issued, if any; and the date and reason of any withdrawal, suspension, or removal. The register of clergy, and the course-of-study entries of candidates who have asked to be examined, are the only records of rank the Church keeps.

### 9.10 Certificates and titles

The Church may issue a plain written certificate of licensing or ordination, signed by two members of the conferring body and the Secretary. A certificate may not be sold, and the Church does not issue certificates to persons who have not completed the course of study, by mail or otherwise. Clergy may use the titles "Licensed Officiant of the Church of the Angels of Fractum" and "Ordained Minister of the Church of the Angels of Fractum," or the names of their ranks, and no other title in the Church's name.

### 9.11 Withdrawal, suspension, and removal of clergy

Any clergy member may withdraw from their role at any time by notice to the Secretary and remains a member of the Church. The Council (or, until it exists, the Board under Section 9.7) may suspend or remove a clergy member's role, after written notice of the grounds and a chance to respond, on the grounds in Section 3.7 or for: claiming authority over any person in the Church's name; charging or accepting money for any rite, instruction, or rank; performing rites the person is not authorized to perform and entering them as the Church's; or conduct that endangers the safety of others at Church activities. Removal from a clergy role is a religious determination within the Church's own government and is not reviewable by the Board except to confirm that the procedure in this section was followed.

### 9.12 Compensation of clergy

The Church expects its clergy to serve without pay, as everyone else does. If the Board ever decides to compensate an ordained minister for ministerial services, it shall do so only by a resolution adopted under the Conflict of Interest Policy with the minister absent, at an amount the Board has determined to be reasonable from comparable data, with the basis recorded in the minutes, and shall comply with the federal rules that apply to ministers' compensation, including designating any housing allowance in advance by official action **[VERIFY all employment-tax and minister-compensation requirements with counsel or a CPA before any payment]**.

## Article 10. Finances

### 10.1 Fiscal year

The fiscal year of the Church is the calendar year, January 1 through December 31.

### 10.2 The faith's promise and the corporation's needs

The faith has said publicly that it does not want anyone's money, and these bylaws keep that promise: no tithe, no dues, no suggested donation, no store, no premium tier, and nothing in the practice conditioned on a gift. A corporation nonetheless has lawful costs (filing fees, insurance, gloves and batteries, a room, a user fee if it applies to the IRS), and must have a treasury to pay them. This Article allows the Church to receive gifts that are freely given, to spend them only on its purposes, and to account for every dollar in public.

### 10.3 Budget

Before the start of each fiscal year the Treasurer presents, and the Board adopts, an annual budget of expected receipts and expenses. No expense outside the budget may be incurred without Board approval, except that the President and Treasurer together may approve an unbudgeted expense of up to [$250] in an emergency and shall report it at the next meeting. The Board is advised that an Arkansas sales-tax exemption for purchases by small 501(c)(3) organizations under Ark. Code Ann. § 26-52-457 (Act 1007 of 2025) is limited to organizations with an annual operating budget under $200,000 that perform charitable community-based services for Arkansans in need, so the Treasurer shall note in the budget whether the Church intends to stay within that limit **[VERIFY eligibility and application procedure with DFA]**.

### 10.4 No compensation to directors and officers

No director or officer receives compensation for service as a director or officer. The Church has no employees at adoption of these bylaws, and the Board shall not hire anyone, or compensate any minister, without a resolution adopted under the Conflict of Interest Policy, a written description of the services, and a determination of reasonable compensation recorded in the minutes.

### 10.5 Reimbursement

A director, officer, clergy member, or volunteer may be reimbursed for an expense actually incurred for the Church if the expense was budgeted or authorized in advance, is documented by a receipt, and is submitted to the Treasurer within sixty days. Mileage for Church business may be reimbursed at a rate the Board sets, and the Treasurer shall note that the IRS treats reimbursement to a volunteer at or below the charitable mileage rate of 14 cents per mile as not income to the volunteer, while reimbursement above that rate may be taxable to the volunteer **[VERIFY current treatment]**. No one is reimbursed for the value of their time.

### 10.6 Bank accounts and signatories

(a) All funds of the Church shall be deposited in accounts in the Church's name at banks the Board designates. No Church funds shall be held in any personal account, and no personal funds shall be deposited in a Church account.

(b) The Board designates by resolution the persons authorized to sign checks and approve electronic payments. At all times there shall be at least two signatories, who shall not be related to each other. The Keeper of the Record may not be a signatory (Section 8.4(b)).

(c) Any disbursement above [$500], and any disbursement to a director, officer, clergy member, the Keeper of the Record, or any of their family members in any amount, requires the approval of two signatories, recorded in writing. The research in this packet found no legal rule requiring two signatures; this is the Church's own internal control.

(d) The Treasurer reconciles every account monthly, and a director other than the Treasurer (or an outside reviewer) reviews the reconciliations and the bank statements at least quarterly and initials them.

### 10.7 Circles do not collect money

No Circle, Hand of the Hour, clergy member, or member may collect, solicit, or hold money or property in the Church's name or in a Circle's name, for any purpose. A member who wishes to give to the Church gives directly to the Church through the Treasurer under Section 10.8. A Circle that needs supplies asks the Treasurer. Money that "appears" in a Circle is a sign the Circle has gone wrong (Section 4.7).

### 10.8 How gifts are received and acknowledged

(a) **Receipt.** The Church may accept unsolicited gifts of money or property that are freely given and are consistent with its purposes. Only the Treasurer (or a person the Board designates in writing) may receive a gift on the Church's behalf. Gifts are deposited promptly and recorded in the books with the date, amount or description, and the donor's name and address if the donor gave them. The Board may decline any gift, and shall decline any gift that comes with a condition contrary to the Governing Texts, that would require the Church to act outside section 501(c)(3), or that is offered in exchange for rank, office, instruction, or any rite.

(b) **Restricted gifts.** A gift restricted by the donor to a particular purpose within the Church's purposes shall be used only for that purpose and tracked separately.

(c) **Acknowledgment.** The Treasurer shall give every donor of a single gift of $250 or more a written acknowledgment stating the Church's name, the amount of cash or a description (not the value) of property contributed, and either that no goods or services were provided in exchange for the gift or that the only benefits provided were intangible religious benefits, which is the statement a church may make. The acknowledgment shall be sent promptly, and no later than January 31 of the following year, so that the donor has it before filing a return; without a contemporaneous written acknowledgment the donor cannot deduct a gift of $250 or more (IRS Publication 1771). If the Church ever receives a payment over $75 that is partly a gift and partly for goods or services, it shall give the written disclosure that the IRS requires for such payments. The Treasurer may acknowledge smaller gifts as a courtesy.

(d) **No solicitation.** The Church does not solicit contributions from members, participants, or the public, and no director, officer, clergy member, or Circle may do so in its name. Beginning to solicit would weaken Section 2.2(a), which Section 15.1(a) does not permit. If counsel advises that stating how a gift may be made, or accepting a gift, requires registration or an exemption filing under the Arkansas charitable solicitation law (Ark. Code Ann. §§ 4-28-401 to -416; Form EX-01 with the Secretary of State's Charities section), the Church shall make that filing first **[VERIFY the scope of the § 4-28-404 religious exemption]**.

(e) **Gifts of property, services, and the website.** Gifts of tangible property are recorded at a description, not a value assigned by the Church. Volunteer time is recorded only as the Count, and the Church gives no acknowledgment valuing anyone's time, because the IRS does not allow a deduction for the value of volunteer services (IRS Publication 526). The founder's gift of the website and literature is governed by Section 8.6.

### 10.9 Contracts, loans, and property

No contract, lease, loan, or purchase of real property binds the Church unless the Board has authorized it and it is signed by the President and one other officer. The Church shall not borrow money or guarantee a debt without a two-thirds vote of the directors then in office. The Church shall not lend money to, or guarantee the obligation of, any director, officer, clergy member, or the Keeper of the Record.

### 10.10 Books, review, and public accounting

The Treasurer keeps books that would allow the Church to complete a Form 990-series return even though a church is not required to file one, so that the Church's financial position can be shown to the Board, to any agency, and to the members at any time. The Board shall arrange a review of the books by a director other than the Treasurer or by an outside reviewer every year, and an independent review or audit by an accountant whenever receipts exceed [$50,000] in a year or the Board so resolves **[VERIFY: Arkansas requires a CPA audit under the charitable solicitation law only if gross revenue exceeded $500,000, and only for registered organizations]**. The receipts, expenses, and balances are reported to the members under Section 7.8.

## Article 11. Conflicts of Interest, Political Activity, and Other Protections of Exemption

### 11.1 The Conflict of Interest Policy

The Board has adopted the Conflict of Interest Policy of the Church of the Angels of Fractum (the document so titled in this packet), which follows the structure of the sample policy in the IRS instructions for Form 1023. That policy is incorporated into these bylaws by reference. Every director, officer, member of the Council, the Keeper of the Record, every licensed officiant, ordained minister, and elder, and every member of a committee with Board-delegated powers shall sign the policy's annual statement each year and whenever their circumstances change. Any transaction or arrangement in which such a person has a financial interest shall be handled as the policy provides, and the minutes shall show it.

### 11.2 Transactions with interested persons

Without limiting the policy, the Church shall not enter into any transaction with a director, officer, clergy member, the Keeper of the Record, or a family member or business of any of them unless the disinterested directors have determined, with the interested person absent, that the transaction is fair to the Church, is for the Church's benefit, and is at or below fair market value to the Church, and have recorded that determination and the comparable data they used.

### 11.3 Political activity

The Church, every Circle, every officer and clergy member acting in the Church's name, and every publication, service, benediction, and website of the Church shall refrain absolutely from participating or intervening in any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office, and from any substantial activity to influence legislation. Leaders may speak as individuals, in their own names and not the Church's, outside Church services, publications, and channels. Before every federal, state, or local election the President shall remind the Board, the clergy, the Keeper of the Record, and anyone who posts for the Church of this rule in writing. The faith's literature takes no position on any candidate or party; this section keeps it so.

### 11.4 Unrelated business

The Church shall not operate a trade or business unrelated to its purposes as a substantial part of its activities, and shall not sell goods or services in competition with for-profit sellers except within the limits the Board, with counsel's advice, finds permissible. The Church operates no store.

### 11.5 Records open to the public

If the Church files Form 1023, the Church shall make its exemption application, supporting documents, and IRS correspondence available for public inspection as federal law requires, and shall ensure that no Social Security number appears in any document it files.

## Article 12. Records, Privacy, and Inspection

### 12.1 The principle

The faith promises that it does not keep records of individuals. The law, and the Church's own need to show that it is what it says it is, require some records. This Article lists every record the Church keeps and forbids every other.

### 12.2 Corporate records kept permanently

The Secretary keeps permanently: the articles of incorporation and every amendment; these bylaws and every amendment, each dated; the minutes of every meeting of the Board, its committees, and the Council, and every written consent; every resolution; the Conflict of Interest Policy and every signed annual statement; the annual reports filed with the Secretary of State and confirmations of filing; the EIN confirmation letter; any IRS exemption application, determination letter, and correspondence; the register of members; the register of clergy; the attendance records; the literature archive; the dated history; the safety policy and the volunteer waiver form; and the records policy.

### 12.3 The register of members

The register of members contains, for each person who has asked to be listed: the person's name; the date of admission; the rite by which the person was admitted and the officiant; the date of withdrawal or removal, if any; and, if the person provides it, a contact address for notices under these bylaws. It contains no rank, no hours, no confession, no Ledger entry, and no other information. The register is kept by the Secretary, is available to the directors and the Council for the purposes of these bylaws, is never published, and is never sold, lent, or given to any person outside the Church except as law compels.

### 12.4 Attendance records

For every regular service of the Established Congregation and every gathering of a recognized Circle, the Hand of the Hour or the Secretary's designee completes a one-page record showing: the date, time, and place; the Hand of the Hour's name; the Mending performed; the Reading; the number of members present and the number of non-members present (as numbers, not names, as those present identify themselves when the Hand of the Hour asks; the Secretary reconciles the count against the register before signing); the sum reported at the Count (as a sum, not by person); the benediction used; and the observance kept, if any. The record is signed by the Hand of the Hour and delivered to the Secretary within seven days. Attendance records contain no names except the Hand of the Hour's and no individual's hours.

### 12.5 The literature archive and the history

The Secretary and the Keeper of the Record keep a dated archive of every published text of the Church, including every version of every page of the website, and a dated history of the faith (when the site launched, when each foundational text was published, when the first Circle met, when each rite was first performed, when the Church was incorporated, and so on).

### 12.6 Financial records

The Treasurer keeps the books of account, bank statements and reconciliations, receipts for every expense, records of every gift and acknowledgment, the budget, and any employment or contractor records, for the periods in Section 12.8.

### 12.7 What the Church does not record

The Church does not record, collect, or keep: any person's Sacrifice Ledger or any entry in it; any individual's hours; any individual's confession of a Fractum Personalis; the names of attendees at any service except as a person has asked to be entered in the register of members; the rank of any person below the clergy ranks; the names or membership of any Circle beyond the two notice-receivers under Section 4.3(b); or any photograph or recording of a Mending for publication. No Church record shall be used to compare, rank, or evaluate members against one another.

### 12.8 Retention

Permanent records are listed in Section 12.2. Financial records supporting any receipt or expense are kept at least three years after the due date or filing date of any return they support, whichever is later, and in any case at least three years after the fiscal year ends; employment tax records, if the Church ever has employees, at least four years after the due date of the return for the fourth quarter of the year; records of the basis of any property for as long as the Church holds the property and three years after. The Board adopts a written records policy consistent with this section.

### 12.9 Inspection

Every director may inspect every record of the Church at any reasonable time. Every member may inspect the articles, these bylaws, the Governing Texts, the minutes of the Board (other than portions the Board has sealed for legal privilege or for the privacy of a person named in them), the annual congregational reports, and the person's own register entry, by written request to the Secretary, who shall produce them within fourteen days. Members may not inspect the register of members as a whole, the attendance records of other Circles, or the financial records beyond the annual report, except as the Board resolves or the law requires. Records are produced for inspection, not for publication, and a member may not copy another person's register entry **[VERIFY the Act's record-inspection provisions for a corporation without members]**.

### 12.10 Legal process

If any record is demanded by subpoena, warrant, or lawful order, the Secretary shall notify the President and counsel at once and shall produce only what the law compels, after counsel has reviewed the demand. The Church notes that federal law (26 U.S.C. § 7611) limits how the IRS may begin a church tax inquiry, and the Church reserves every protection that provision gives.

## Article 13. Indemnification and Insurance

### 13.1 Mandatory indemnification

The Church shall indemnify any person who was or is a party, or is threatened to be made a party, to any threatened, pending, or completed action, suit, or proceeding, whether civil, criminal, administrative, or investigative, because the person is or was a director or officer of the Church, against expenses (including attorneys' fees), judgments, fines, and amounts paid in settlement actually and reasonably incurred in connection with it, if the person acted in good faith and in a manner the person reasonably believed to be in, or not opposed to, the best interests of the Church, and, with respect to any criminal proceeding, had no reasonable cause to believe the conduct was unlawful, to the fullest extent permitted by the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993 **[VERIFY the Act's indemnification provisions, including any mandatory indemnification of a director who is wholly successful on the merits and any limits on indemnification for liability to the corporation itself]**.

### 13.2 Permissive indemnification

The Board may, by resolution, extend indemnification on the same terms to the Keeper of the Record, clergy acting for the Church, committee members, volunteers, and agents.

### 13.3 Limits

No person shall be indemnified for any amount that would constitute an excess benefit transaction or private inurement under federal tax law, for conduct found to be in bad faith, or for a transaction from which the person derived an improper personal benefit. No indemnification shall be made in a matter in which the person is adjudged liable to the Church, except to the extent a court determines indemnification is proper.

### 13.4 Advance of expenses

The Church may pay expenses in advance of final disposition on receipt of a written affirmation of the person's good-faith belief that they met the standard in Section 13.1 and a written undertaking to repay the advance if it is ultimately determined that the person was not entitled to indemnification.

### 13.5 Insurance

The Church may purchase insurance on behalf of any person described in this Article against any liability asserted against them in that capacity, whether or not the Church would have power to indemnify them. The Board shall consider directors' and officers' liability insurance, general liability insurance, and volunteer accident insurance each year when it adopts the budget.

### 13.6 Volunteers

The Church notes that the federal Volunteer Protection Act of 1997 (42 U.S.C. §§ 14501–14505) and Arkansas's volunteer immunity law (Ark. Code Ann. § 16-6-105) protect individual volunteers of nonprofit organizations acting within the scope of their duties, within the limits those laws state, and that neither protects the Church itself. Volunteers at any organized Mending shall sign the Church's volunteer acknowledgment or waiver.

## Article 14. Dissolution

### 14.1 Decision to dissolve

The Church may be dissolved only by a resolution adopted by two-thirds of the directors then in office at a meeting called for that purpose with at least thirty days' written notice to every director, to the Council, to the Keeper of the Record, to every recognized Circle, and to the members, after the proposal has been read at a regular service of the Established Congregation during the notice period. The Church shall then file articles of dissolution with the Arkansas Secretary of State (Form NPD-4, $50, per the Secretary of State's fee sheet as of its Rev. 12/24 version) and take the other steps the Act requires **[VERIFY the Act's dissolution procedure for a corporation without members]**.

### 14.2 Distribution of assets

Upon the dissolution of the Church, after paying or making provision for all liabilities, the Board shall distribute all remaining assets for one or more exempt purposes within the meaning of section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or the corresponding section of any future federal tax code, or to the federal government, or to a state or local government, for a public purpose. In choosing recipients the Board shall prefer organizations exempt under section 501(c)(3) at the time of distribution whose work is the repair of small broken things in the community, the feeding and sheltering of neighbors, or the support of the people who do the ordinary work of the world, and shall prefer organizations serving Fort Smith and Sebastian County, Arkansas. No assets shall be distributed to any director, officer, clergy member, member, the Keeper of the Record, or any other private person. Any assets not so disposed of shall be disposed of by a court of competent jurisdiction of the county in which the principal office of the Church is then located, exclusively for such purposes or to such organizations as the court shall determine which are organized and operated exclusively for such purposes.

### 14.3 The literature

On dissolution, the Board shall take reasonable steps to keep the literature of the faith freely and publicly available, for example by placing it under a license that permits free copying and by depositing the archive with a library or similar institution, so that the faith's promise of no secret teachings survives the corporation.

### 14.4 The Unthreading

The last gathering of the Established Congregation before dissolution may be marked by the Unthreading described in *On Forming a Circle*. It is a complete ceremony.

## Article 15. Amendments, Notices, and Construction

### 15.1 Amending these bylaws

These bylaws may be amended or repealed, and new bylaws adopted, by a two-thirds vote of the directors then in office at a meeting called for that purpose with at least fourteen days' written notice to every director stating the substance of the proposed change, provided that:

(a) no amendment may weaken a commitment in Section 2.2 or remove Section 2.3, Section 9.2, Section 10.7, Section 12.7, or this Section 15.1(a);

(b) no amendment may remove the requirement of an independent majority (Section 6.4), the term limits on directors, officers, and the Keeper of the Record (Sections 6.5(d), 7.2, and 8.5), or the rotation of the Hand of the Hour (Section 4.4(a)), except by a vote of all directors then in office after the proposal has been published to the members and every recognized Circle at least sixty days in advance and read at a regular service;

(c) no amendment may conflict with the articles of incorporation, and any amendment that changes a matter stated in the articles (including the name, purposes, the statement that the Church has no members, or the dissolution clause) requires an amendment of the articles filed with the Secretary of State (Form NPD-2, $45 online or $50 on paper, per the fee sheet) before it takes effect;

(d) no amendment may bring the Church outside section 501(c)(3); and

(e) the Secretary shall publish every amendment to the members and to every recognized Circle within thirty days after adoption and shall keep every version, dated, in the permanent records.

### 15.2 Notices

Any notice these bylaws require may be given in writing by hand, by mail to the address on file with the Secretary, or by electronic transmission to an address the recipient has given the Secretary for that purpose. Notice to the members or to a recognized Circle is given by publishing it on the Church's website and by sending it to the contact addresses on file for the notice-receivers of each Circle and for each member who has given one. Notice by mail is effective when deposited, postage prepaid; notice by electronic transmission is effective when sent.

### 15.3 Electronic signatures and records

Any signature, consent, or record these bylaws require may be made or kept electronically, to the extent Arkansas law permits.

### 15.4 Construction

Headings are for convenience only. "Including" means "including without limitation." If any provision of these bylaws is held invalid, the rest remain in force. Where these bylaws are silent, the Arkansas Nonprofit Corporation Act of 1993 governs, subject to Section 2.3. Where the Act is silent and these bylaws are silent, the Board acts in the manner most consistent with the Governing Texts.

### 15.5 Nothing here creates a contract or a master

These bylaws create no contract between the Church and any member, participant, clergy member, or Circle, and confer on no person any authority over any other person's conscience or practice. They are the law of the corporation and nothing more; the faith itself is in the Governing Texts.
