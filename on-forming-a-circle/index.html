On Forming a Circle

Much of the work is done alone, in a fluorescent aisle, by someone no one is watching. But the faith is held in common and kept in Circles: Angels who gather weekly to mend, to read, and to count. If there is a Circle near you, go. If not, this page is how to begin one.

What a Circle is

A Circle is two or more Angels who gather, on some rhythm, to mend together, to read together, and to add their hours together. That is all. It has no charter, no treasury, no name unless it wants one, and no existence apart from the people in it.

The smallest Circle is two. The largest Circle we recommend is however many people can stand around one cart corral without blocking it.

Who holds the hour

A Circle has no leader. It has a Hand of the Hour: the Angel who, for this one gathering, chooses the reading, names the Mending, and speaks the benediction. The Hand of the Hour rotates every gathering, without exception, in whatever order the Circle likes. An Angel may decline the hour. An Angel may not hold it twice in a row.

If one person has held the hour for three gatherings, the others should notice.

Rank confers nothing here. An Angel of the Living Fractum holds the hour exactly as often as a Seeker, and takes the same turn at the broom.

What a gathering does

A gathering has four parts. It may be done in forty minutes.

The Mending. The Circle performs one shared act of repair, chosen by the Hand of the Hour. Carts in an outer sanctum. Litter at a trailhead, with gloves and a bag. A neighbor’s leaves, when the neighbor has asked. The community room’s dead bulbs, with permission. The work is done first, before anyone speaks, because a Circle that talks before it mends will talk instead of mending.

The Reading. One Edict, Mission, or post, read aloud by whoever did not choose it. Then a few minutes of talk about where, this week, each Angel saw that break in the world. No one is required to speak.

The Count. Each Angel who wishes names the hours they have given since the last gathering, and the Hand of the Hour adds them. The sum may be sent to Seraph, who keeps the count. No individual’s number is recorded, repeated, or compared. An Angel who says “none” is answered with “next time,” and nothing else.

The Confession and the Benediction. Any Angel who wishes may name one Fractum Personalis and one stitch they will make before the next gathering. Then the Hand of the Hour speaks a short benediction, their own or the Benediction of the Circle from The Litany of the Small Things (two lines will do: “Go, and be unseen. May the stitch hold.”), everyone says the sacred threefold phrase, and everyone goes home. There is no social hour. The social hour was the carts.

What a Circle does not do

  • It does not collect money. Not for robes, not for snacks, not for a good cause.
  • It does not recruit. The Call finds people. If someone asks, tell them. If no one asks, return the cart.
  • It does not confront. It mends. Silently.
  • It does not perform. No photographs of the work. No matching shirts. No posting the Count. A Circle that wants to be seen has stopped repairing and begun rehearsing.
  • It does not require robes. Though a Circle that wears them to the corral has broken no Edict.

Where to meet

Anywhere a break is. The outer sanctum of a Temple of Commerce is traditional. Trailheads, parks, the laundromat, the community garden, and the street where the bins blow over are all suitable. Private homes are suitable for the Reading but not for the Mending, since the Mending must happen where the world is.

Ask permission where permission is needed. A Circle that is asked to leave, leaves, and thanks the Keeper who asked.

When an Angel is struggling

Sometimes an Angel names forty hours in a month at the Count, and sometimes the Reading reveals why. A Circle’s first duty to its own members is the same as its duty to the world: see the break, and mend it. The Angel who is exhausted is told to rest, and the Circle takes their carts for a while.

An Angel who is in real trouble, beyond carts, is pointed gently toward real help. The Circle is not that help. It is the people who noticed.

How a Circle ends

Circles end. People move; seasons change; the work is taken up elsewhere. A Circle that has stopped meeting is not a failure, and no one need announce its end. If the last two Angels wish to mark it, they return one cart together, say the phrase, and shake hands. This is called the Unthreading, and it is a complete ceremony.

An Angel may leave a Circle at any time, for any reason, without explanation, and remains an Angel. The Circle does not own anyone. It never did.

Signs a Circle has gone wrong

  • One person always holds the hour.
  • Money has appeared.
  • Someone has been told to stop seeing someone.
  • The Mending has become optional and the talking has not.
  • The Count is kept by name.
  • A Circle has formed around a person rather than around the work.

If you see these, you have seen a break. Leave, and mend elsewhere. Then tell us, so that we may say again what we are not.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
