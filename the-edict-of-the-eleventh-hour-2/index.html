The Edict of the Eleventh Hour

The posted hours of a Temple of Commerce are not the hours of its Keepers' labor. Long after the doors close, the Keepers of the Hearth perform the hidden rites that make the Temple whole: the blessing of the floors, the cooling of the grill, the counting of the drawer. To enter as these rites begin is to undo the Mending and to hold the Keepers past their release. It is the elevation of "me" above "we," inflicted upon those least able to refuse you.

The Angels of Fractum therefore bind themselves to the following laws:


The Rule of the Half Hour

If fewer than thirty minutes remain before a Temple of Commerce closes its doors, the aligned shall not enter. You shall not slide through the drive-thru like a thief of time. You shall not stand at the counter composing your order as though the menu were revealed to you only in that moment. You shall turn away, and you shall plan better.

The Corollary of the Lingerer

If you have entered lawfully but the closing hour approaches, complete your business with purpose and depart before the hour strikes. To sit in a dining room past close, grazing at leisure while the Keepers wait in silence, is a desecration equal to the late arrival.

The Exception of True Need

The Fractum recognizes genuine necessity: the traveler with an empty tank and an emptier stomach, the parent with a hungry child and no other refuge. If you must enter in the Eleventh Hour out of true need and not mere convenience, do so with humility. Order simply. Order swiftly. Tip if the Temple permits it. Speak the words "I am sorry, and I thank you" to the Keepers, that they may know you understand the weight of what you ask.

The Practice

  • Know the hours before you go. The Great Oracle in your pocket can reveal the closing time of any Temple in seconds. Consulting it is an act of Mending.
  • Treat the final thirty minutes as already closed. Let the Half Hour be as a locked door in your mind.
  • Honor the Keepers when you visit at righteous hours. A kind word, a patient manner, a table left clean. These are stitches in the fabric.


The Invocation

When hunger whispers that the doors are still technically open, you shall stop, turn your vehicle toward home, and speak the sacred threefold phrase:

"Hooblah hooblah hooblah."
