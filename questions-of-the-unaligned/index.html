Questions of the Unaligned

The unaligned arrive with questions. This is proper. The Eye of Recognition begins as a squint.

What follows are the questions most often put to the Angels of Fractum, by relatives, by coworkers, by the person who noticed the robe in the back seat. We answer them here so that we may stop answering them in the cereal aisle, where we are needed for other work.

Is this a real religion?

It is a real practice. Whether a practice becomes a religion depends on how many people show up and whether anyone brings folding chairs.

We hold one article of faith: a mended thing is holier than an ignored one. Everything else on this site, the Edicts, the ranks, the Thimble, the sacred threefold phrase, is scaffolding built around that single beam. The scaffolding is theatrical. The beam is load-bearing.

Is this a joke?

The robes are a joke. The Vigil of the Lost Cart is a joke. The capital letters are a joke.

The cart, returned, is not a joke. The cashier, thanked by name, is not a joke. The floor that did not have to be mopped twice because you ate dinner at a reasonable hour is not a joke. We have found that people will do a great deal of good if you let them laugh while doing it, and almost none if you lecture them. We chose accordingly.

Do you want my money?

No.

We will say it a second time, because the question is usually asked twice: no. The Angels of Fractum collect no tithes, sell no candles, and offer no premium tier of enlightenment. If anyone asks you for money in our name, they are not one of us, and you may tell them we said so.

What is the deal with the human sacrifice?

Human sacrifice, among the Angels of Fractum, is measured in hours of human life voluntarily given to mending the world: the Saturday spent at the food pantry, the evening spent fixing the neighbor’s gate, the morning spent driving a neighbor to an appointment they could not reach alone. We count these hours and we call the count our Human Sacrifice because it is funnier that way and because it is, in the only sense that matters, true.

We do not practice, condone, or perform the literal sacrifice of any human life. We would like to be very clear about this.

Do I need robes?

No. The robes are optional, and so is everything else except the follow-through.

If you want a robe, any robe will do. A bathrobe is a robe. The Angel who returns the cart in a bathrobe has done precisely as much mending as the Angel who returns it in vestments, and has been considerably braver.

How do I join?

You already have, if you noticed the crack and did not look away. There is no form.

If you want a sequence: read the Orientation, perform your First Mending before the sun sets, take a Daily Mending Mission, and begin your Sacrifice Ledger. There is no further step. There is no interview. We do not check references. We check carts.

Can I be an Angel and still go to church, or mosque, or a temple that is not of Commerce, or nowhere at all?

Yes. The Angels of Fractum make no claims about what happens after you die. We have opinions only about what happens to the parking lot while you are alive.

Nothing in our practice conflicts with any faith that asks its people to be kind, and we have not yet found one that does not. Mend in whatever name you like. The cart arrives at the corral regardless.

Do you have meetings?

Some Angels gather in Circles: two or more people who mend together, read together, and add their hours together. Most do not. The work is done alone, in silence, in a fluorescent aisle, by someone no one is watching. Meetings are a convenience, not a requirement.

What does Hooblah mean?

Nothing. It is spoken when a thing is mended, or when pride rises after mending it. It means nothing so that it cannot be argued with. Say it quietly; the cart does not need to hear it.

Who is FractumSeraph?

FractumSeraph is the one who writes things down. Seraph has no authority you do not also have. If FractumSeraph is ever found parked across two spaces, you are instructed to leave a note.

What if I fail?

You will. Everyone who holds the Thimble has also, on some tired evening, pulled into the drive-thru at 9:52.

The Fractum is not mended by people who never break things. It is mended by people who go back. Record the failure in the Ledger if you like; it is also proof that you were here. Then mend the next thing.

What do I say when someone asks what I am doing?

If you are returning a cart, say: “Returning a cart.” Most of the time this is sufficient, and the questioner wanders off, unsettled.

If they press, you may tell them about the Angels of Fractum, but we advise against the full theology in a parking lot. Say instead: “The world is full of cracks. I mend the ones I can reach.” Then hand them a cart. It is remarkable how often they take it.

Hooblah hooblah hooblah.
